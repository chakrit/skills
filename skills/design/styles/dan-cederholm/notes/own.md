## 1. Moves

### 1.1 One warm signature colour, carried across every redesign

- **What.** Orange (later a red-orange) is the one accent, and it survives each rebuild.
  2005: orange headline and sidebar header bars. 2011: orange hexagon badge and orange
  caps labels. 2016: orange drop cap and end mark (#f45923). 2026: orange-red link
  underlines, the arrows after section heads, and the ground of the shop's "&" shot
  [observed].
- **Why.** In 2010 he brought back "the original orange from the first ... design" when
  he redrew the mark [stated, evolution].
- **Replaces.** A palette picked fresh for each redesign, or a cool tech blue.
- **Fits.** A personal or small-studio brand that should feel continuous across years.

### 1.2 Evolve the mark; keep a trace of every earlier version

- **What.** The pixel-art logo became a 3D stack of cubes (2007), then a hexagon taken
  from the inner cube (2010), then a cube glyph (2026). Each version keeps a piece of
  the one before [stated, evolution; observed].
- **Why.** "Keep hints to the past", which he calls "evolution rather than revolution".
  The 2010 redraw was made because the old mark did not work at small sizes, and he
  posted it on Dribbble for feedback first [stated].
- **Replaces.** A clean-slate rebrand.
- **Fits.** Any redesign of an existing identity. Name the element you are keeping.

### 1.3 Make the type and ornament warm, and keep the layout quiet

- **What.** The layouts are plain: a centred fixed column with one main column and a
  narrow sidebar (2005 to 2011), then a single column of about 500px (2016) [observed].
  The warmth comes from small touches:
  - a cream ground instead of white (2007, rgb 243/241/233)
  - braces around the current nav item and the dates, as in "{HOME}" (2007)
  - an italic ampersand in a colour of its own, in "Hand-crafted pixels & text" (2007)
    and "Handcrafted pixels & text from Salem, Massachusetts" (2009)
  - small icons beside permalinks, comments and event dates (2007)
  - in 2011, tab-shaped markers hanging off the left edge of the white sheet, showing
    whether a post is a quote, a link or a list [observed]
- **Replaces.** A decorated grid, or a perfectly neutral one.
- **Fits.** Text-led sites. Put the personality in the type, the punctuation and the
  margins, not in the structure.

### 1.4 Tiny letterspaced caps for labels

- **What.** Section labels are set very small, uppercase and widely tracked.
  - 2007: Georgia 9.88px caps, 2px tracking, green, over a hairline rule, as in "LATEST
    FEATURED WORK".
  - 2011: Milo bold caps in orange.
  - 2016: nav in Freight Sans 18px caps with 2px tracking, inactive items at 30% black.
  - 2026: "LATEST FONTS →" in his own Cartridge, spaced caps with an orange arrow
    [observed].
  Nav items often carry a lowercase grey second line, as in "WORK / interface design"
  (2009) and "WORK / things I made" (2013) [observed].
- **Replaces.** Bold sentence-case headings, and nav labels that do not say what is
  behind them.
- **Fits.** Index and home pages with many small sections.

### 1.5 Pick type for its job, and change it as the site changes

- **What.** The faces changed with each era:
  - 2007: Verdana text with Georgia headings, the standard web-safe pairing.
  - 2011: FF Milo, which he chose because it is "versatile in various sizes" and "looks
    great in all caps", plus FF Meta Serif italic for quotes [stated, evolution;
    observed].
  - 2012: Whitney for text, Tungsten condensed caps for titles [stated, hfj; observed
    2013].
  - 2016: Brandon Grotesque bold caps for titles, Freight Sans 20/30 for text [observed].
  - 2026: Söhne for text, his own display faces for headings [observed].
  Each era pairs a plain text face with a caps display face.
- **Replaces.** One sans-serif doing everything.
- **Fits.** A plain text face plus a condensed or characterful caps face for titles.

### 1.6 Display type made from a found object, finished rough or soft

- **What.** Every SimpleBits font starts from a piece of vintage commercial lettering:
  - Kyroh: the hand-lettered word "EGYPT" on a luggage label from a Cairo hotel. He
    extended the alphabet from those five letters and aimed for "imperfect, inky,
    rounded-but-rough shapes" [stated].
  - Hubano: lettering on a Cuban cigar box. The "wacky crossbars of the A and H set the
    tone" [stated].

  Of the 19 families in the shop, most are all-caps display faces, and many ship "Rough"
  (worn, inky edges) or "Soft" (rounded terminals) versions [observed]. His newsletter
  keeps returning to ghost signs, 1970s Topps trading cards with flat offset printing,
  and retro packaging [stated, Studio Notes].
- **Replaces.** Clean geometric display type with no source in the world.
- **Fits.** Headlines and identities that should feel made by hand and rooted in a
  place, a period or a trade.

### 1.7 Show the brand as a photographed still life of real objects

- **What.** The 2026 home page hero is a photo on off-white of the studio's own things:
  game cartridges labelled in his fonts, wood type spelling SIMPLEBITS, an ink brayer, a
  mug with a red ampersand, dice, stickers and an embroidered patch [observed]. The about
  page uses a black-and-white photo of the desk with a SIMPLEBITS pennant and a licence
  plate.
- **Replaces.** An abstract hero illustration or a gradient.
- **Fits.** Makers and shops that have real goods to show. It proves the craft instead
  of claiming it.

### 1.8 A dry, self-deprecating voice in the copy

- **What.** Some examples: "Danmade since 2002. Never perfect, always a okay", "Danmade™
  typefaces for people like you", the "Secret Club™" set in blackletter, and
  strikethrough jokes inside a list (2007) [observed].
- **Replaces.** Earnest marketing copy.
- **Fits.** A personal brand. The type stays plain so the jokes come through in the
  words.

## 2. Process and thinking

- **His site is a sandbox.** He calls personal sites "a perpetual sandbox" [stated,
  evolution]. He tries a logo out on Dribbble, takes feedback, then ships it [stated].
- **Small steps over a rebuild.** On Dribbble's small-screen version he chose an
  adaptive stylesheet on the existing site over a full redesign. He called it "Baby
  steps" and said that small improvements "keep us moving" [stated, adapted].
  - When space was tight, horizontal items were stacked and short vertical lists were
    set in columns; he avoided hiding content [stated].
  - Flexible layout finally "feels like web design", not "designing sheets of paper"
    [stated, adapted].
- **Type through people he trusts.** Greg Storey suggested Whitney about five years
  before the 2012 refresh. It became the base of the SB mark, and the H&FJ catalogue
  became his default for slides [stated, hfj].
- **Fonts start from a found word.** He takes the few letters on the source object, works
  out the rest of the alphabet from them, and keeps their quirks. He now draws in Glyphs
  and records the sessions for members [stated, kyroh; newsletter].
- **Progress over perfection.** Getting everything 100% correct is hard. Any gain makes
  the site "better than it was" [stated, 2003]. This later became the brand line "Never
  perfect".

## 3. Taste

- Likes: vintage commercial lettering such as luggage labels, cigar boxes, ghost signs,
  trading cards, game cartridges and racing badges [stated, observed]; letterpress, wood
  type and inky imperfection; the ampersand as an ornament (italic in 2007 and 2009, the
  red "&" print and mug in 2026); H&FJ and FontFont faces; warm cream grounds.
- Recurring motifs: the cube and hexagon mark; orange; braces and small icons; signed
  prints; cloth-bound books with a ribbon marker and one small icon on each cover.
- Dislikes (inferred, not stated in what I read): starting over from scratch, and a
  perfectionism that blocks shipping.
