## Process

- **Discovery and environment first.** In 2007 he described his process as starting
  with discovery: learning what message the client wants to convey and what its
  customers expect; before that, a place he feels creative, surrounded by books and
  objects, a sketchbook and physical "morgue files" of clippings, old ads, photos,
  matchbooks, textures. [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/,
  2007-10-04]
- **Research as "the most important part".** For Comhaltas (an Irish music
  organisation) the Happy Cog team went to Ireland and sat in pubs listening; they then
  studied traditional motifs and colours, and rejected the "preconceived notions" of
  leprechauns and shamrocks. [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/,
  2007-10-04]
- **Sketch, then a grey-box comp, then the comp.** "If I go from a sketch right to
  Photoshop, I'm too caught up in the details already." The step between has no colour
  and covers hierarchy and layout only. [reported,
  https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **Three directions, then rounds.** He presented three broad-stroke comps per client
  (AIGA; WordPress), each standing on its own idea, asked the client not to
  "Frankenstein" them, then iterated a chosen one; in the second round he focused on
  typography. "It's much easier to revise than create." [reported,
  https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04] For WordPress 2.5:
  three designs, client picks one, a couple more rounds; Photoshop comps shown as
  images on a private intranet. [stated,
  https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2014_01_22_Santa_Maria.html, 2014-01-22]
- **Typeface choice by word association.** He writes down words for the mood, then
  looks for typefaces that carry them. For his studio Mighty: "stable, powerful,
  industrial, boxy, angular, geometric, machined, blue collar"; he chose a bold
  condensed Proxima Nova set large in all caps, rejecting Blackoak for "Wild West
  baggage" and Eurostile as "too open". [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 4,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/shop/on-web-typography-ebooks.zip, 2014]
- **Body text face first.** "I like to start by picking a text face for body copy, as
  this is what a reader will spend the most time looking at." [stated,
  https://alistapart.com/article/on-web-typography/, 2009-11-17]
- **Type sketches in the browser.** Dummy HTML pages with a few headings and
  paragraphs, each set in a different face or size, tested across browsers and
  devices; he leaves with "two or three typefaces as starting points when I begin
  comping pages". [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 4, zip URL above, 2014]
- **Let the content show how it wants to be set.** He works in "rough typographic
  explorations", in Photoshop or a browser, to see "how it wants to be set", comparing
  content to the grain of wood. Some projects follow an ordered path set by fixed
  constraints; others are "like working with clay". [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 6,
  zip URL above, 2014]
- **Zooming between scales.** He works "by zooming in and out between the general and
  the specific", citing the Eames film *Powers of Ten*; "The width of a grid column
  influences the line length of a paragraph." [stated, *On Web Typography*
  introduction, zip URL above, 2014]
- **Hierarchy from an audit of elements.** Build a typographic system by first auditing
  the site's basic elements, by semantics (h1, h2) and by job (captions, block quotes),
  then keep the rules consistent. [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 5, zip URL above,
  2014]
- **Many ugly ideas first.** "I want a big sprawling mass of ugly ideas because it helps
  get past the most obvious ones." [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/piles-of-ideas/, 2014-11-19] Sketchbooks
  are "about being a good thinker", not drawing well. [stated,
  https://v4.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/pretty-sketchy/index.html, 2009-04-22]
- **Explore to extremes.** Drawing the Citywide lowercase n, he pushed shapes "toward
  an extreme (angular or curved)" to feel out "too much", then pulled back. [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/learning-curves, 2025-10-30]
- **Design the system, not one look (v4, 2008).** Instead of one site design he built
  "a system": "a basic eight column grid, a few typefaces", fixed masthead and utility
  information, with typefaces, colours, images, backgrounds, columns and layout open to
  change per post, "much the same way one would approach the design of a magazine".
  Each post got "design tags" for colour, layout and typeface. [stated,
  https://v4.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/a-new-day/index.html, 2008-06-17]
- **Start small and in public (Slate).** Slate's redesign began with one weekly Cover
  Story as a "sandbox", deliberately incomplete (no comments, search, navigation),
  tested with readers, then grown into the brand. "We didn't set out to reach a finish
  line, but to make a sustainable process." [stated,
  https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2016/12/slate-is-changing-its-approach-to-design-heres-how-and-why.html,
  2016-12-18; https://slate.com/briefing/2018/01/jason-santa-maria-on-how-slate-redesigned-the-way-we-work.html,
  2018-01-16]
- **Brief and mood board with the client (Megazoid).** He and the type designer
  gathered references they both liked (retro packaging, VHS and cassette, Polaroid,
  Trapper Keepers, calculators, game controllers) before designing. [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/new-work-megazoid-microsite, 2025-10-03]
- **Logos.** With Matt Mullenweg the WordPress brief was "lots of abstract ideas"
  (publishing, personal journal, letterpress); they set guidelines for what the logo had
  to do before drawing. He explored word balloons, metal type, typewriter keys in about
  30 variations. [stated,
  https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2014_01_22_Santa_Maria.html, 2014-01-22;
  reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]

## Reasons given for particular moves

- **Base size 16–18 px.** From reading distance (18–24 in. from a desktop) compared to
  print's ~10 pt; "When in doubt, make it bigger." [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 5,
  zip URL above, 2014]
- **Headline at 2–3x body.** "I usually work in simple math, doubling or tripling the
  body text size"; e.g. 12 px text, 32 px headline, 16 px subhead, then judged by eye.
  A small headline "can flatten your hierarchy". [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- **Big type treated as an image.** Very large type is "a picture of type that anchors
  a layout", like a movie poster or book cover; a one-off decorative face may fit
  there; tailor its line breaks per screen size. [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- **Sans serif for headlines and UI.** Sans serifs pack tighter, so more characters
  per line and a larger size fit; for UI every bit of space counts, including for
  translated labels. [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- **Slightly condensed headline faces** for news sites, to absorb varying headline
  lengths without awkward breaks. [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- **Small type ≈80% of base** signals a change in tone, like lowering your voice; a
  separate caption face can signal a different speaker. [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- **Paragraph spacing.** Blank line equal to the type size or a touch smaller, or a
  1 em indent; web paragraphs are "chunked", so they can stand apart. [stated, ch. 5,
  2014]
- **Measure and leading.** 45–75 characters; longer lines need more line-height;
  start at 1.2–1.8; nudge 1.5 down to 1.2–1.3 on small screens. [stated, ch. 5–6, 2014]
- **Margins** of "at least around 1.5–2 ems" around body text, for breathing room.
  [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- **Text colour #333 and a "medium gray" typographic colour,** so running text is not
  too high in contrast. [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- **Hierarchical grid plus column grid on his own site.** Horizontal bands stacked down
  the page lead the reader along one message and collapse easily into one column on
  small screens; a column grid inside the bands spaces elements and separates content
  types. [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- **No baseline grid on the web.** Images, forms and rendering throw it out of phase;
  CSS lacks InDesign's controls; its print purpose (show-through on thin paper) is moot
  on screen; "Type ... needs to be judged optically first, not by the numbers."
  [stated, https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/baseline-grids-on-the-web/,
  2012-10-19; ch. 6, 2014]
- **Scales as a start only.** "I'm a chronic eyeballer"; a scale is "my starting point
  ... but I rarely adhere to it", because readers see relationships, not maths.
  [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- **Two type families, varied by size and colour.** On his site (then): Chaparral plus
  a couple of widths of Proxima Nova; "the more typefaces I use in a design, the weaker
  the design becomes". [stated, ch. 3–4, 2014]
- **Pairing: avoid two serifs or two sans.** Too similar weakens hierarchy (Caslon with
  Baskerville); pair by distinction and harmony, e.g. faces on shared principles
  (Futura with Bodoni) or a deliberate old/new clash. [stated, ch. 4, 2014;
  https://alistapart.com/article/on-web-typography/, 2009-11-17]
- **Text faces with "a touch of personality (and I mean ever so little)".** Bree's
  closed-loop descenders "play cuter in text than I may want". [stated, ch. 4, 2014]
- **System fonts first, web fonts as enhancement**, because FOUT is acceptable but a
  layout jump when fonts load is what he avoids most. [stated, ch. 3, 2014]
- **Orange in the WordPress 2.5 admin.** "something really punchy to offset everything
  else, so that when you needed a color it was going to be distinct"; in the whitest
  comp "little bits of orange would really pop off the page" as action colours.
  [stated, https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2014_01_22_Santa_Maria.html,
  2014-01-22]
- **Spare WordPress admin.** Lightening the heavy blues gave "the feeling of being
  faster". [stated, same, 2014-01-22]
- **WordPress mark, not only a wordmark,** so it could live on a T-shirt or as a tiny
  icon and eventually stand alone. [stated, same, 2014-01-22] The mark alludes to a
  typewriter key: someone wrote this by hand. [reported,
  https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **A List Apart redesign made bookish.** Books are read "religiously, not
  skimmed"; issues numbered, banners and stamps, a laurel wreath, a colour scheme per
  issue and an illustration per article, to be "not quite so templated". [reported,
  https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **AIGA article page.** Bigger reading column and text, a drop cap, inline images, so
  the page "can feel full all the way down"; a 120-column grid divisible into 3, 4 and
  6, with an image-size style guide. [reported, same, 2007-10-04]
- **Happy Cog tagline as navigation**, since both said the same thing. [reported, same]
- **Megazoid site.** Colours "right up to the line of 'clashing'"; a subtle paper
  texture "to evoke the feeling of discovering these materials in an attic box";
  almost everything reacts to hover or touch, "because this is the very place to have
  fun with web design". [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/new-work-megazoid-microsite, 2025-10-03]
- **Loud Room site.** Minimal, "less a portfolio and more a statement of purpose", a
  few paragraphs he accepts as "a small amount of friction"; no frameworks; HEX
  Franklin; a randomising colour palette, each checked for basic contrast, enjoying
  "the bit of chaos when a garish palette shows up". [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/meet-loud-room, 2026-01-28]
- **Slate design direction** (with the firm Gretel): show the story-making process,
  "allowing elements to overlap and pile on top of one another", with the editor's
  hand as emphasis and marginalia; Retina for body, Register for headlines; the past
  maroon kept and refined, plus a darker maroon and bright supports; distinct cues per
  vertical. [stated,
  https://slate.com/briefing/2018/01/jason-santa-maria-on-how-slate-redesigned-the-way-we-work.html,
  2018-01-16]
- **Interludes** (drop caps, big photos, colour, pull quotes at intervals) hold a mood
  in long articles "like the chorus of a song". He writes this about other people's
  work (a *Matter* story), not his own. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/interludes-in-design/, 2014-09-01]

## What he refuses or dislikes, and why

- **Helvetica and Arial as defaults.** "To me Helvetica has become a generic default";
  people use it to avoid "the fear of making a bad choice". "Don't just throw up your
  hands and use Helvetica (or, gasp!, Arial)." He also argues it is a poor wayfinding
  face for the New York subway. [stated, *On Web Typography* intro, ch. 3–4, 2014]
- **Dumb quotes.** He built Smart Quotes for Smart People; bad quotes are more visible
  in big type. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/dont-be-hasty-use-smart-quotes/, 2013-09-24;
  reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **Baseline grids on the web** (reasons above). [stated, 2012-10-19]
- **More than one display or script face**; too much personality in text faces; free
  fonts (often poor kerning and hinting); Caslon on screen ("I have trouble
  recommending Caslon" over Chaparral or FF Meta Serif); icon fonts (screen-reader
  problems). [stated, ch. 3–4, 2014; https://alistapart.com/article/on-web-typography/,
  2009-11-17]
- **Designing by the numbers.** "It's very easy to become bound to the numbers instead
  of what looks appropriate." [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- **Content dropped into a template.** In 2008 he was "disillusioned" that design was
  "a bit player rather than a worthy partner to that content". [stated,
  https://v4.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/a-new-day/index.html, 2008-06-17] "Offhandedly
  dumping text into a column on a webpage used to pass for an article." [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/interludes-in-design/, 2014-09-01]
- **Clichés and trend-chasing in branding.** Leprechauns for Irish music; a fictional
  "Double‡Dagger" logo to show piggybacking on a trend "makes us look like anybody
  else". [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **Over-decorated skeuomorphism** ("stitching, leather, torn pages. Blech.") and
  "flat" treated as more than a veneer. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/the-space-between-the-notes/, 2013-06-06]
- **"The fold."** "I actually don't really concern myself too much with the fold."
  [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **Design by committee,** when every opinion weighs the same. [stated,
  https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2014_01_22_Santa_Maria.html, 2014-01-22]
- **Designing logos** he has "tried to avoid" as he got older: stressful, not his
  strongest skill. [stated, same, 2014-01-22]
- **Grid frameworks.** "I'm not big on frameworks." [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/golden-grid-system/, 2011-08-30]
- **Snark as critique.** "Critique is not synonymous with 'being mean.'" [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/take-a-breath/, 2014-02-18]
- **AI models trained without consent;** "generic design spat out by a machine".
  "When you get that artifact without the journey, you make nothing." [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/large-language-muddle, 2025-06-03;
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/meet-loud-room, 2026-01-28]
- **In-house reporting, OKRs and metrics,** which kept him "too far from the actual
  design". [stated, https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/funsizing, 2025-05-06]

## Clients and phones

- **Clients.** Set expectations in the first meeting, define the process, play back
  the client's feedback at every stage; "It's their site; it's your job to steer them
  in a good direction visually"; some jobs are a loss. [reported,
  https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04] As an outside contractor
  "we could never know them as well as the people at WordPress do"; handing comps to
  the client's own developers worked through "a really good amount of communication".
  [stated, https://archive.wordpress.org/interviews/2014_01_22_Santa_Maria.html,
  2014-01-22] Now: "making decisions together", "intentionally small", working
  "directly with clients who care about craft", calling in trusted freelancers when
  needed. [stated, https://loudroom.com/; https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/meet-loud-room,
  2026-01-28] At Slate his role was "instigator and barrier-remover" across editorial,
  design, development, product and sales. [stated, Slate 2018-01-16]
- **Phones.** Treat the web as fluid: relative units (em, rem, %) for type and columns;
  line length governs type size per breakpoint; shorter lines take less line-height;
  tailor big-headline breaks per screen; a hierarchical grid stacks into one column.
  "Type is like water flowing into a glass." [stated, *On Web Typography* ch. 5–6,
  2014] Readers may be "at the park reading on their phone"; make type "as sturdy as
  possible" in all conditions. [stated, https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-read/,
  2014-08-05] In 2007, asked about a site that must fill any window width: crop
  thumbnails that open larger, swap stylesheets, "Fluid sites are never easy, so fight
  the good fight." [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/,
  2007-10-04]

## Beliefs about design in general

- "Typography is a design's voice." Type is "design's smallest atomic unit". [stated,
  book intro, 2014]
- Contrast is "the most crucial tenet of graphic design"; the most important thing need
  not be the largest, only the most distinct. [stated, ch. 5, 2014]
- A grid "merely serves as a collection of possibilities", not paint-by-number.
  [stated, ch. 6, 2014] "Grids are means for organization and improvisation." [stated,
  2012-10-19]
- Readability asks "Do you want to read it?"; good typography goes unnoticed. [stated,
  https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-read/, 2014-08-05]
- Whitespace "is just as important as your company logo". [stated, ch. 6, 2014]
- Feel over encyclopedic knowledge; "Trust your gut." [stated, intro, conclusion, 2014]
- The web is its own medium, not to be judged as print; CSS "finally gave the web its
  own grain". [stated, https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/discourse-in-web-design/,
  2014-01-22; https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/css-is-still-awesome, 2026-08-31]
- Innovation is mostly iteration; "Trying to be innovative, just like trying to be
  cool, usually fails." [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/the-space-between-the-notes/, 2013-06-06]
- No design detail is too small. [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/,
  2007-10-04]
- Answers come from discussion among peers; asking questions is "at the heart of
  collaboration". [stated, https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/ask-a-question/,
  2014-03-17]
- "Good design can make complex things fun-sized and accessible to everyone." [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/]
- Preserve old work: he keeps past site versions live "to keep links alive and to track
  my own progress". [stated, https://jasonsantamaria.com/;
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/saved/, 2015-02-13]

## Where he changed his mind

- **Reading.** "When I first started designing websites, I assumed everyone read my
  work the same way I did"; he later saw reading as shaped by surroundings and needs.
  [stated, https://alistapart.com/article/how-we-read/, 2014-08-05]
- **Craft versus concept.** Out of college he was "pretty cocky"; an early poster with
  the rainbow in the wrong order taught him there is an art-direction component.
  [reported, https://blog.fawny.org/2007/10/04/aeasf07-jsm/, 2007-10-04]
- **Sketching.** Early on he felt putting an idea on paper gave it "a permanence greater
  than its value"; later he came to want many ugly ideas. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/piles-of-ideas/, 2014-11-19]
- **Critique.** For years he offered answers; now he asks questions. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/ask-a-question/, 2014-03-17]
- **Saying no** used to make him uncomfortable. [stated,
  https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/saying-no/, 2012-11-14]
- **Desert-island typefaces** changed between 2009 (Caslon, Trade Gothic) and 2010
  (Garamond Premier Pro, News Gothic); the shortlist "changes every so often". [stated,
  ch. 4, 2014]
- **Logos.** In 2014 he said he avoids designing logos; in 2026 he "had a blast
  creating the logo" for Loud Room. He does not describe this as a change of mind.
  [stated, wp interview 2014-01-22; https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/meet-loud-room,
  2026-01-28]
- **In-house to contract.** After roles at Typekit, Slate, InVision, Resolve to
  Save Lives, Netlify, Shopify he decided in 2025 that he does not "mesh well with the
  structure of in-house design". [stated, https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/funsizing,
  2025-05-06]
- **Citywide.** He did not plan a lowercase, then drew one; following the caps too
  closely "made the letters feel stale". [stated,
  https://jasonsantamaria.com/blog/learning-curves, 2025-10-30]
