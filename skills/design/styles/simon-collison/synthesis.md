# Simon Collison: synthesis

The design model behind `style.md`, drawn from four research streams in `notes/`.
Citations name the note and its section: `own 1.3` is move 1.3 in `notes/own.md`, `work
§2` is section 2 of `notes/work.md`, and likewise for `talks` and `accounts`. Each claim
keeps the note's evidence label: **(stated)** he says it, **(observed)** seen in the work,
**(reported)** someone else says it.

## 1. The core idea: motive, then an honest object in a strong voice

Collison designs from a motive before a layout. He names the drivers in every talk from
2010 to 2012: nostalgia, rhetoric, vernacular and humour, with play, personality and "the
courage and conviction" to commit to a bold direction (talks 1.2) (stated). His own work
shows each of them: the Edwardian journal of his v4 site is nostalgia and humour, the
Rushmore stickers are vernacular, the New Adventures copy is humour (own 1.1; work 1.5;
accounts 1.4) (observed).

The motive becomes one period or cultural object that wraps the whole site, down to the
smallest label: v4 was "The Celebrated New Miscellany of Mr. Simon Collison", with
sections called "Potted Autobiography" and "Exhaustive Archives" and comments closed
because of "spamming guttersnipes" (own 1.1, 1.4) (observed). The object is carried by
voice and typography, never by fake wear. He stripped the worn textures from v4 after
one honest critic, and his 2009 talk argued for "truth to materials" (own 1.5; talks
1.6) (stated). Peers read the result as restraint with personality: "minimal colors and
typography", "it takes guts to strip back" (accounts 1.3) (reported).

Two things follow:

- **Voice is design material.** Labels, captions, footers and error copy are written in
  the conceit's voice, and readers notice: "delightfully literate" labels (accounts 1.4)
  (reported). Copy is finalised before visual design (work §2, Erskine) (stated).
- **He divides opinion on purpose.** "The work I'm most proud of has been the stuff that
  divides opinion… it tells me that I'm not being boring" (own §2) (stated).

## 2. Where ideas come from

- **Period print and ephemera:** Schott's Miscellany, Victorian and Edwardian
  engravings, an old book about botanists and Darwin, record sleeves, paperback covers,
  stickers, newspapers (own 1.1, 1.2, §3; work 1.5, 1.6) (stated).
- **The audience's own vernacular:** record-shop culture for a music platform, album
  sleeves as reward stickers ("we did, of course, do Unknown Pleasures") (work 1.5)
  (stated).
- **Music, art and landscape:** New Order, Sigur Rós, Boards of Canada; the St Ives
  painters and land art; the Peak District, the Pennines, Iceland; birds (own §3; talks
  §3) (stated). The 2019 home page tells his life through songs, paperbacks and stickers
  (own 1.6) (observed).
- **Creative inquiry outside the field,** brought back to the web: he studied fine art and
  ran two art festivals (talks §2) (stated).
- **Provocation:** "What if it was an Apple app? What if we made it less Swiss? What if the
  text was huge?" (work 1.8) (stated).

## 3. Process

1. **Many concepts, most discarded.** About a dozen concepts for v4 over a year, thrown
   away "with no regrets"; the winner came fast and was built night after night (own §2)
   (stated). Early R&D is unpressured: "Sketch, think, go for walks" until "spongy stuff
   calcified into explainable logic" (work §2) (stated).
2. **Constraint first.** "Having complete freedom is possibly the worst way to start any
   project." Make sense of the brief's limits and add your own (talks 1.3) (stated). New
   Adventures 2019 was a written kit: a grid, two typefaces, two colours, one grey, almost
   black, white (work 1.1) (stated).
3. **Type leads.** The v4 design was "type-led"; he looked for faces "that would inform
   the whole feel" before layout (own §2) (stated).
4. **Juxtaposition test.** v4 clicked when a butterfly engraving first sat beside the type
   (own §2) (stated).
5. **Mostly in the browser.** About 10% pencil, 40% Sketch, 50% browser for New
   Adventures; CSS is "one of the very best design exploration tools"; "throwing the
   components around, squinting at the screen a lot" (work §2) (stated). Reason away from
   the screen, refine on it (talks §2) (stated).
6. **Articulate every decision.** Replace "because it feels right" with a reason in a
   visual grammar: line and point, form, formal and informal structure, balance, pattern,
   texture, repetition and rhythm, paths and movement, colour, white space as "the pause",
   depth, affordance, juxtaposition, economy, grids (talks 1.1) (stated).
7. **Blunt audits.** Regular audits, "blunt but never unnecessarily negative", always with
   solutions; patterns found in audits become the system (work §2) (stated). Sit with
   finished work and remove what is not needed (talks §2) (stated).
8. **Systems, not pages.** Understand the system, then "break rules creatively" (talks
   1.5) (stated).

## 4. Type

- **A tired face, used with full craft.** v4 was almost all Times New Roman, Regular and
  Italic, Bold only for drop caps: "squeeze every possible ounce" from it (own 1.3)
  (stated). Rutter answered with Bringhurst, make it "richly and superbly ordinary"
  (accounts 1.1) (reported). The craft shows in composition, size and colour steps,
  spaced capitals for navigation, small-caps dates (own 1.3, 1.4) (observed).
- **A condensed display face fitted to the name.** Condensed Franklin Gothic, because a
  wide face "sucks" with the double L in "Collison" and a narrow sans "doesn't feel too
  modern"; Clarendon for the logo, Mrs Eaves italic "to antiquate", a Baskerville
  ampersand (own 1.3) (stated).
- **Loud type with a small hand.** Erskine's huge lowercase red "hello." over stacked
  condensed caps, next to a pencil bird and a handwritten logotype (work 1.4) (observed).
- **Later, quiet type.** The 2019 site: FF Meta Serif for reading, PT Sans for labels, a
  small italic tagline; "the colour does the mood" (own 1.7) (observed). New Adventures
  2019: Aperçu for display, Source Sans for body (work 1.1) (stated).
- **Name and descriptor pairs.** A caps name over an italic serif descriptor ("DAN MALL /
  *Art Director at Big Spaceship*"; "*Designer, type nerd, em dash abuser*"); the italic
  carries the wit and the caps the order (work 1.3) (observed).
- **Typography belongs to the designer** (talks 1.5) (stated).

## 5. Composition

- **A firm grid under the ornament.** Snook: everything "sits nicely on a grid";
  Gustafson picked v4 for its "rigid grid structure" (accounts 1.2) (reported). A visible
  grid overlay was judged a gimmick, and he agreed (accounts 1.2) (stated).
- **Editorial furniture:** big drop caps, a § sign as drop cap, catalogue numbers in a
  roundel between prev and next, red numerals down the archive list, an aside column of
  italic metadata between dotted rules, a double rule under the title, footnotes with
  return links (own 1.4) (observed). A non-designer singled out the footnotes (accounts
  1.4) (reported).
- **Lockups.** The v4 masthead stacks an italic line, spaced caps and condensed red caps;
  the footer wraps a huge ampersand in a warning notice (own 1.1) (observed).
- **The timeline.** The 2019 home page is a horizontal life from 1973 to 2022: poetic
  fragments in narrow columns, small framed objects on stems, years on the rule, rotated
  labels at turning points, lyrics footnoted to their songs (own 1.6) (observed). It is a
  "museum of me" (stated). Graphical timelines and colour keys also appear in his advice
  on taming complexity (talks 1.4) (stated).
- **Breathing room and hairlines in product work.** Samsung: reduction, "everything could
  breathe a little more", a "flexible but stern" system; TeuxDeux: hairlines that "start
  and stop in the right places" and font sizes (work 1.7) (stated).
- **Simplicity is a feeling.** "Less can very often lead to, well… less"; complexity can
  be "beautiful, elegant, necessary"; minimal is not automatically clean (talks 1.3, 1.4)
  (stated). Squint at a layout to judge its weight (talks 1.4) (stated).
- **Modular containers at fixed ratios** (1:1, 2:1, 1:2, 4:3, 16:9) with a shared palette
  in variables (talks 1.5) (stated).

## 6. Colour

- **Few colours, high conviction.** v4: red and black on off-white over a faint graph
  paper (own 1.1, 1.5) (observed); Veerle Pieters credited "minimal colors" (accounts
  1.3) (reported). New Adventures 2012: one bold red; 2019: blue, pink, one grey (work
  1.1, table) (observed; stated).
- **A calm dark ground** for the later site: dark teal rgb(26,55,67), pale blue-grey text
  (own 1.7) (observed).
- **Tint to unify.** Duotone venue photos (red, cyan and pink, purple) make unruly
  pictures one family; "playful tints for unifying images" (work 1.2) (stated; observed).
- **A kit that generates.** From two colours and a grid come "multi-line patterns, solid
  blocks and playful tints" (work 1.1) (stated). A year may branch off with its own
  colours while "the system still carries most of the work" (stated).

## 7. Imagery and surface

- **The irrelevant illustration.** Engraved animals sit one per section and have
  "no correlation to the section… that's where I get my kicks": Facebook gets a snake
  captioned "Bloody Facebook" (own 1.2) (stated). The picture gives no clue, so readers
  read the caption (accounts 1.4) (reported). The name Rushmore was liked for the same
  irrelevance (own 1.2) (stated).
- **Hand-made marks:** pencil bird, torn paper, pencil-line borders (work 1.4; accounts
  1.5) (observed; reported).
- **Paper as subject:** kraft card, memos, stickers, a printed newspaper, newsletters
  that "feel more like letters… on headed paper" (work 1.6, §3) (stated; observed).
- **Honest surface.** No coffee-cup rings, no over-texturing, no gratuitous CSS3 shadows,
  rotations or spinning buttons (own 1.5; talks 1.3, 1.6; accounts 1.3) (stated). Calls to
  action stay identifiable (talks 1.3) (stated).
- **Motion:** ask what force would cause a movement and respect its path (talks 1.1)
  (stated).

## 8. Phones

The evidence is thin. v4 launched fixed and small; critics called the type
"eyeachingly small" and px-bound, and he retrofitted it into an early responsive site
(accounts §2, 1.2) (reported). The 2019 timeline is a flexbox layout he lets "twang
around, shift and realign", accepting that it is "a bit weird" on narrow screens (own
1.6) (stated). `style.md` keeps the conceit and the furniture on phones, lets the layout
reflow rather than shrink, and keeps body text large, which answers the criticism of v4.

## 9. Taste summary

Likes: period ephemera and miscellanies, record sleeves and paperbacks, engravings, land
art and the St Ives painters, birds, moorland, music; wry British copy; tiny kits that
make a lot; footnotes; restraint with personality (own §3; work §3; talks §3; accounts
§3). Dislikes: fakery and over-texturing, gratuitous effects, trend-chasing, chasing
perfection, minimalism assumed to be clean, being boring (own §2–3; talks 1.3, §3).

## Cautions and gaps

- **Ban conflicts.** Three of his moves break `../../references/bans.md` as he made them;
  `style.md` changes them:
  - The italic kicker above each v4 section title ("Bottled for your pleasure / POTTED
    AUTOBIOGRAPHY") and Frieze's tiny grey caps kickers are labels above section headings,
    the eyebrow pattern. `style.md` keeps only the order he used for speakers: the title
    first, the italic descriptor under it.
  - Catalogue numbers are kept on entries in a list or on an item's own page, never as a
    numbered kicker above a section heading.
  - Erskine's "hello." in red beside black caps is two whole lines in a lockup, which the
    bans allow; a single coloured word inside one headline is not.
- **Wayback.** The 2004–2011 site captures and the Erskine and New Adventures captures
  were seen before the no-Wayback ruling and are kept; no later captures were opened
  (own, work headers). The 2012–2018 state of his site rests on the `/v4/` archive only.
- **Attribution.** New Adventures 2011–2013 and Frieze may be partly the work of Erskine
  colleagues (work §4). The 2019–2020 kit he describes as his own.
- **Process over pixels in product work.** Samsung, TeuxDeux and Citipo are known from his
  case-study text, not from close looks at the screens (work §4).
- **Talks without video.** The decks are image-heavy and videos were excluded, so the
  talks stream rests on slide text and titles (talks §4).
- **Accounts are mostly one comment thread** (his 2010 redesign post) and conference
  reviews; no independent critique of the 2019 timeline was found (accounts §4).
