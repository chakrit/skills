Sounding Line is a design studio of four in Pioneer Square, Seattle. We draw identities, books and websites for people around the Sound who make something to be read: a tide almanac, a cinema’s monthly programme, a small press list, a mill’s yearly letter to its growers. We start with the words and the typeface, and we stay until the last proof is signed. Here is some of it.

Selected work, 2022 to 2026

  1. Slack Water

    A pocket almanac of a year’s tides at eleven launch sites for the Shilshole Kayak Co-op, set to be read wet, at arm’s length, in a drizzle.

    Almanac, website · 2026

  2. Hollow Grain

    The Skagit Stone Mill writes to its forty growers once a year. We gave the letter a face, a fold, and a stamp the miller now inks by hand.

    Identity, annual letter · 2025

  3. Harbor Steps Cinema

    A monthly programme folded from one sheet, and listings that keep the same type from a phone in the queue to the board above the door.

    Programme, signs, website · 2024

  4. Low Bank Press

    Twelve chapbooks a year for a poetry press in Port Townsend, all on one grid, told apart on the shelf by where a single rule crosses the spine.

    Series design · 2022 to now

How a job runs

We talk first

Two or three long conversations about what your business is now and what it should be in five years. We read what you already print and send, and we list what your neighbours in the trade look like so we can stand somewhere else.

Two to three weeks

Then we choose type

Most of our work is settled here. We test faces against your real words at the smallest and largest sizes they will ever be set, on paper and on a phone, and we pick one that will still suit you in ten years.

One to two weeks

Then we draw, and proof, and proof

Marks, pages, screens and signs come together, in that order, and you see printed proofs every Thursday. We hand over files your own staff can use, and we answer the phone afterwards.
