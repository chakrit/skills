I make tables, benches, desks and shelves by myself in an old dairy shed outside Creswick. Most of it is alder, and I finish every surface with a hand plane, so nothing here has been sanded. I build one piece at a time, in the order the letters come in.

Alder

The wood, and the plane

Alder is soft and light, and most furniture makers pass it over. When it is first cut it is nearly white; within an hour in the air it turns a warm orange-brown and then settles over a year into the colour of weak tea. I buy it in rough boards from a small mill near Ballarat and let it sit in the shed for a season before I use any of it.

A sharp plane cuts the fibres clean and leaves the surface with a low shine that sandpaper takes away. You can see the strokes if you hold a tabletop up to a window. I leave them.

Ordering

Wait, price, deposit

Write and tell me what you need it for, the room it goes in and its rough size. I answer every letter within a week, with a pencil drawing and a price.

Benches start at $1,400, desks at $3,200 and dining tables at $4,600. A third of the price holds your number in the book, and the rest is due when the piece leaves the shed. The wait is about seven months at the moment. I deliver within two hours' drive myself, in the ute.
