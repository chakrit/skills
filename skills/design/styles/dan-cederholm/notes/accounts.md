## 1. Moves

### A signature strong enough to be recognised in someone else's frame

Garrity reviewed the 2004 Blogger templates (TicTac and TicTac Blue are Cederholm's) and
admired "a strong esthetic", ranking them among his two favourites on looks, but judged
that "Cederholm's personal style is almost too strong": a user would see "a Cederholm
template with my writing" [reported]. Rubin, designing for the same project, aimed the
other way, for designs not too personal [reported]. So peers read his look as a distinct
signature by 2004, and Zeldman later calls him "an extremely gifted designer with a strong
personal vision and style" [reported].

- Default it replaces: the neutral, interchangeable template.
- Fits: a brand or a personal site where the voice is the point. It does not fit a
  container for other people's content; see the next move.

### Recede when the content is other people's work

For Dribbble he did the opposite of his signature. Summers describes a mostly grey and
black interface with pink accents from the logo, so the shots stay in front [reported],
and quotes Cederholm: "kept the UI agnostic (monochrome, Helvetica type, simple glyphs)"
and the priority is "to feature the work and the designer" [stated]. He told Welcome to
the Jungle that resisting his own interface preferences was an ongoing effort [stated].
Personality moved into the brand layer instead: the pink script logo and a basketball
vocabulary (shots, buckets, rebounds) that "just worked" as it emerged [stated, via
Summers].

- Default it replaces: a gallery UI that competes with the gallery.
- Fits: any page whose job is to show work, products or user content. Put the voice in
  the mark, names and copy, not in the chrome.

### Quiet grid, warm muted palette, ornament only at the edges

Grouchnikov's 2010 description of simplebits.com: "A simple three column grid provides a
lot of whitespace around carefully chosen blocks of text"; header navigation "perfectly
aligned with the main layout columns"; a "spartan beige color scheme" with logo and links
in muted pink and footer links in darker beige so they blend; "beautiful geometric
patterns" along the top, left and bottom edges [reported]. The content field stays calm
and the decoration frames it rather than sitting inside it.

- Default it replaces: white page, saturated accent, decoration in the content area.
- Fits: a studio or personal site that should feel warm and crafted without noise.

### Playful illustration to invite people in, at a usability cost

Gruber called Cork'd (2006, with Dan Benjamin) "Terrific design" [reported]. McNally gave
it the edge over a rival for "playful graphic design" that "really invites users to jump
right in", then a year later found it "harder to actually use, especially when searching
for wines"; a rival's founder conceded Cork'd "looked better" [reported].

- Fits: a hobby or community product where charm drives sign-up. Watch that charm does
  not crowd out search and other tasks.

### Craft as finish: enrich where you can, let it degrade elsewhere

Reviewers of all three CSS books report one stance. Haack: the designer must "give up
pixel perfect control in exchange for a better user experience", and each chapter takes a
rigid design and rebuilds it to survive text resizing, missing images and CSS
[reported]. Fortuna: progressive enhancement over sites "identical in every browser";
rounded corners and RGBA colour variations as small rewards [reported]. Unmatched Style:
he is known for "a high level of craftsmanship and finish on everything", the book reads
as "a philosophy", and spends pages on ampersands [reported]. Fisher recalls his advice to
use animation "in subtle and tasteful ways" [reported].

- Default it replaces: either pixel-identical rigidity or bare minimalism.
- Design meaning: small typographic and surface details (a good ampersand, a translucent
  layer, a soft corner, a slight transition) carry the "handcrafted" feel; the structure
  underneath stays flexible.

### Hand-made touches in a digital launch

Dribbble's first ~50 members got a mailed t-shirt and a handwritten invitation note
[reported, Onisko; stated, Welcome to the Jungle: "a T-shirt with some handwritten code on
that"]. Fisher was inspired by him making physical products for Advencher [reported].

- Fits: a launch or brand that wants to feel personal; physical goods extend the visual
  identity.

### Refine the mark; do not restart it

Zeldman noted a "freshly redesigned" SimpleBits in 2008 [reported]; Fonts in Use logged a
2012 redesign in H&FJ's Tungsten and Whitney, "the craftsman himself" [reported]. His own
2014 account of the Dribbble script (Kleenex-inspired, a hybrid of two script faces, later
smoothed, evened and opened up) argues that refining lets "the old and new ... coexist"
[stated]. The pattern across accounts is continuity of identity through tuning.

### Constraint as the format

The Next Web (2010) reported 400x300 shots and a 24-shot quota, and called the teaser
format "a very clever way" to get focused feedback [reported]. The small fixed frame is a
design decision that shapes what people post.

## 2. Process and thinking

- Rebuild from a real example: the Bulletproof chapters start from an existing rigid page
  (mostly real sites) and rework it [reported, Haack]. SimpleQuiz (2003) put competing
  markup options side by side for debate, which Stopdesign called "brilliantly simple"
  [reported].
- He implements other designers' comps faithfully, "reads minds", and turns fixed
  Photoshop sizes into flexible pages [reported, Zeldman].
- Product features follow observed behaviour ("paving the cowpath"; rebounds came from
  people linking shots in comments) [stated, via Summers and Welcome to the Jungle].
- Mentoring: Fisher watched him write and edit and calls working with him a career
  milestone [reported].

## 3. Taste

- Recognisable personal aesthetic, read by peers as strong and warm [reported, Garrity,
  Zeldman, Fisher: "You come through in it so clearly"].
- Muted pinks and beiges; geometric pattern borders; generous white space [reported,
  Grouchnikov, 2010 site].
- Typographic detail as pleasure: ampersands, custom script, H&FJ faces [reported,
  Unmatched Style, Fonts in Use].
- Humour and play in naming and brand: basketball vocabulary, playful Cork'd graphics
  [reported].
- Simplicity as an inherited value; brands, logos and packaging as early interests
  [stated, The Great Discontent; unsigned bio calls his approach "simplistic"].
- Subtle over showy motion [reported, Fisher].
