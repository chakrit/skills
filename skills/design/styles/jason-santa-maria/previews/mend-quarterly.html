Forty hours
with a
Singer 201

Rosa Achterberg inherited her grandmother’s machine with a seized hook and a motor that smelled of hot tar. She kept a log.

WordsRosa Achterberg
MachineSinger 201K, 1951
Hours40 over 9 weeks
Parts bought£23.40

The handwheel would turn a quarter of the way and stop, the way a door stops on a chain. My grandmother had sewn on it until 1994; after that it sat in a cupboard in Haarlem with a bag of lavender on its bed plate, and the oil she had used for forty years turned to something closer to varnish.

The first ten hours went on getting it apart without breaking anything. Singer used slotted screws of a dozen sizes, and a screwdriver that is nearly right will chew a 1951 screw head in one turn. I ground the tip of a cheap one to fit the throat-plate screws and labelled every part in a muffin tin.

“Leave it in kerosene till Thursday and don’t touch it before then.”

That was the advice from Gerrit at the Spaarne sewing shop, who has repaired these since the seventies and charged me nothing for it. The hook came free on the third evening of soaking. The motor was a different story: the brushes were worn to stubs, and the grease in its gearbox had to be dug out with a lolly stick.
