## 1. Moves

### 1.1 Give the reader a map of where they are

- **What.** For a large body of text, a left rail lists every section, grouped by phase
  under coloured labels, and stays visible while you read, so position in the whole is
  always shown, like the thickness of a book under your thumb. [stated, observed]
- **Where.** HCD Guides for Ishmael Interactive: black ground, yellow condensed bold
  headings and labels, a floating bottom pill with two actions ("Buy the books", "Ask a
  question"). [observed]
- **Why.** "One of the most difficult challenges with an extensive web content
  collection is providing clarity about where you are." He added PDF editions so people
  can hold and annotate the guides. [stated]
- **Default it replaces.** A hamburger menu and breadcrumbs.
- **When it fits.** Guides, documentation, multi-part reports.

### 1.2 Loud brand, quiet reading page

- **What.** The brand colour floods the home page and index (gold for Gold's Guide,
  yellow for Ishmael), while article pages drop to plain black on white or white on
  black with a characterful text face. [stated]
- **Where.** Gold's Guide: "we stick with straightforward black text on a white
  background" with Frances for text, Fern for the logo. [stated]
- **Default it replaces.** Brand colour spread evenly over every page.
- **When it fits.** Publications with long articles.

### 1.3 Split the job between logo, grid and colour

- **What.** Each brand element carries one quality: the mark carries expression, a
  rigid grid carries competence, colour carries optimism. [stated, Ishmael]
- **Seen.** Ishmael's square mark stacks "ish / ma / el" in tumbling, rotated letters
  inside a frame of yellow bands that read as grid lines. [observed]
- **Default it replaces.** A logo asked to do everything, applied to a generic site.

### 1.4 Identity by shape, colour left free

- **What.** When a mark must live inside other apps, he drops fixed colour and makes the
  shape alone the identity, so light and dark modes can take opposite palettes (warm
  oranges in light, cool blues and purples in dark). [stated, A New Social]
- **Where.** The overlapping-stars mark for A New Social; the brand guide pages explain
  each asset bluntly ("You're not Nike (yet)"). [observed]
- **When it fits.** Products shown as icons on other platforms.

### 1.5 Stand apart from the category's type and colour

- **What.** List the colours and faces competitors use, then take the opposite: for a
  tech publication, bright colours instead of blue and purple, and contemporary serifs
  (Redaction's pixel-distressed cuts) instead of sans. [stated, We Distribute]
- **Default it replaces.** Picking what "looks like" the sector.

### 1.6 An idea drawn from a children's book or a period

- **What.** He anchors a brand in a specific visual source: Margret and H. A. Rey's star
  diagrams for A New Social; art deco and museum wall colours for Kaplan Art Tours.
  [stated]
- **When it fits.** Small organisations that need warmth and a story.

### 1.7 Make the font's design space something you can move through

- **What.** On a specimen site he turned the type designer's diagram of widths and
  optical sizes ("the horn", a triangular prism) into an interactive object, and set
  the specimen so roman, italic and capitals scroll past while staying editable.
  [stated]
- **Seen.** Roslindale page: black hero with huge serif over the wireframe prism;
  sections colour-blocked in teal, black and butter yellow; single long words at display
  size run off the right edge, each labelled in tiny spaced capitals; "history." set so
  large its descender drops into the text. [observed]
- **Default it replaces.** A static waterfall of sizes.

### 1.8 Fonts are a material: make artifacts

- **What.** To promote fonts nothing has yet been made with, make objects with them: a
  slider pin whose back plate letters (t, s, y) complete "he" into each pronoun; packing
  tape in Gamay reading "CONTAINS MULTITUDES". [stated, Darden Studio]
- **When it fits.** Launches where the product is a tool, not a finished thing.

### 1.9 An ad as a window, not a box

- **What.** A responsive ad unit that reads as a slice of a brand world "you could peer
  into", distinct from the page but not overwhelming it. [stated, Concert]
- **Default it replaces.** Standard IAB rectangles dropped into the layout.

### 1.10 One news serif chosen to outlast redesigns

- **What.** For Vox he proposed Harriet (Okay Type), a serif "news-worthy" yet
  contemporary, and redrew the logo curves; he notes these survived a decade of
  redesigns. Explainers were paginated card stacks. [stated]

## 2. Process and thinking

- **Content before brand.** For Ishmael the guides' home came first; "branding work came
  second". [stated]
- **Research and workshops.** At Mariana Tek every project started with customer
  interviews and whiteboard sessions to separate needs from wants. [stated]
- **Systems to remove friction.** Mockups pieced together from utility classes caused
  inconsistency; he built an Atomic Design system (tokens, atoms, molecules, organisms),
  also used for EL Education. [stated]
- **Scope where it matters.** Vox launched in nine weeks by reusing SB Nation scaffolding
  and spending new effort only on the explainer format and the home page. [stated]
- **Open values in licensing.** For A New Social the typeface had to be OFL "to align
  with the open principles" of the client. [stated]
- **Iterate on fit.** A New Social's first warm, illustrated round was loved but stood
  out too much on other platforms; he tightened the mark and chose more common type.
  [stated]

## 3. Taste

- Independent foundries and named faces: Harriet, Fern, Zenith, Frances, Roslindale,
  Gamay, Kit, Redaction, Libre Caslon, Futura. Serifs lead more often than sans.
  [stated]
- Strong single colours used as fields: gold, yellow, deep green, brown, magenta.
  [observed]
- Period and illustration references over stock photography: deco, Rey star charts,
  engraving-style trees (Ishmael home, attribution uncertain). [stated, observed]
- Metrics on every case study (traffic, velocity, revenue): he frames design by its
  effect for clients. [observed]
