# Yugo Nakamura (中村勇吾): talks, interviews, lectures

Stream: `talks`. Raw text of every source is in `/tmp/designers/yugo-nakamura/raw/talks/`,
one file per source, URL on the first line. Most sources are Japanese; quotations below
are my own translations, kept short, with the Japanese where the wording matters.

Claim tags:

- **[stated]**: his own words in an interview, dialogue, podcast transcript or talk
  report's direct quotation.
- **[reported]**: an interviewer's, collaborator's or reporter's paraphrase or claim.
- **[observed]**: seen in the work. This stream did not look at the work; nothing here
  carries that tag.

## 1. Moves

### 1.1 Design the cause, not only the motion

- **What.** Motion is drawn as the visible effect of a force the viewer can feel: the
  pointer, gravity, sound, time. He says leaves moving tell you the wind is blowing,
  so "designing surface motion means grasping the driving force behind it; what I
  design is the relation between the surface motion and its driving force" [stated:
  Mynavi 2010 talk report]. He loves "the rule behind the motion" (動きの背後にあるルール)
  and traces it to the parabola of a thrown apple in the 1984 game Neutron, Mario's
  jump, and Splatoon's ink spatter [stated: Denfaminico 2023].
- **Where.** Monalisa Nervous Matrix (1999, numeric keys swell a 3x3 grid of the face);
  ribbon lines drawn by the mouse in 3D, where "you feel the rule governing the line"
  [reported: Mynavi 2010]. The yugop.com home page that won MoMA over: "dark,
  two-dimensional cascading balls" whose "simple physics of bouncing" held the curator
  for minutes [reported: MoMA 2010, Shannon Darrough].
- **Default it replaces.** Keyframed or eased decoration that moves because the page
  can move.
- **Why, in his words.** "The source of the feeling is not the computer; it is born in
  you. Your hand's motion makes the screen's motion" [stated: UNIQLO/Kangaeru Hito
  2006].
- **When it fits.** Any motion on a page: tie each movement to an input or a physical
  law the reader can sense, so the motion reads as consequence.

### 1.2 Start from one good detail and multiply it

- **What.** He begins with a detail he finds beautiful, then links two or three of
  them, then imagines a hundred, "simulating from the part to the whole" [stated: WIRED
  2016]. In the brick metaphor: top-down design starts from "family of four, 3LDK";
  he starts from "a really interesting brick" and asks what house makes it shine,
  even if that means ten rooms and a family of ten, because it "breaks the
  pre-arranged harmony" [stated: Design no Temae 2025].
- **Where.** Ecotonoha: a click-grows-a-tree animation demo, then "what if you could
  type text" was added during production [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008]. HUMANITY:
  Boids-style flocking ("three simple rules... very organic motion") applied first to
  birds (GUNTAI), then to people; the game system came from "piling up small
  discoveries", and the story came last [stated: PS Blog 2023; Design no Temae 2025;
  Game Watch 2023].
- **Default it replaces.** Concept, sitemap and wireframe first; detail last.
- **When it fits.** When the page has one strong behaviour or element worth building
  around; less so for a pure information service.

### 1.3 Collage at the level of the algorithm

- **What.** He describes his work as "collage in the dimension of algorithms"
  [reported phrase, quoted in Mynavi 2010 talk report and echoed by panelist on p. 4].
  Take something everyone knows as a fixed reference point, join it to a context from
  elsewhere, and a "new balance, new sense of distance" appears [stated: Mynavi 2010].
- **Where.** Clocks: dropclock (Helvetica numerals falling into water) and clashclock;
  the clock is "the primal form of the algorithmic and also something everyone knows,
  a reference point" [stated: Mynavi 2010]. He traces the clock genre to John Maeda's
  12 o'clocks (1996) [stated: GDR c. 2021]. UNIQLOCK's dance-plus-clock belongs to the
  same family, though its concept talk was given by Koichiro Tanaka, not by him.
- **Default it replaces.** Novelty from a blank page, or a familiar widget left
  familiar.
- **When it fits.** A page needs a signature element: pick a universal object (clock,
  calendar, list, map) and give it one foreign behaviour.

### 1.4 Type as the knot between form and content

- **What.** Letters are "the junction of form and content" (形態と内容の結節点), so
  typography for him is "relation-operation across both sides" [stated: Mynavi 2010].
- **Where.** FPM's site: song titles in a single-stroke font whose centre lines
  ripple with the track's waveform, "a font where form and algorithm are one"; the
  Wonderwall screensaver pulls colour threads from photos and "stitches" the word;
  Ecotonoha's overlapping green letters read as leaves while the old threaded
  bulletin board becomes a literal tree [stated: Mynavi 2010]. Morisawa Fontpark 2.0
  breaks words into elements users rebuild [stated: MoMA 2010]. He polygonises
  outlines to make them wobble, writes his own skeleton data to derive shapes, and
  twisted polygon skeletons for a Design Ah exhibit; he wants a programmer-friendly
  font format with free skeleton and attributes [stated: JDN 2018].
- **Default it replaces.** Type as static label set once, with motion applied to the
  box around it.
- **When it fits.** Display type, titles, wordmarks, loading or idle states; keep body
  text still.

### 1.5 Few typefaces, chosen late, swapped against the motion

- **What.** He uses a very small set of fonts and adds roughly one a year; he wants
  "as few kinds as possible" and dreams of one ultimate Mincho [stated: JDN 2018]. For
  a moving piece the motion exists first, then he swaps font files into it until one
  fits; he chose Adobe's Ten Mincho for the Shuntaro Tanikawa exhibition because it
  "has presence but isn't cloying, with just enough trace of shaping" [stated: JDN
  2018].
- **Taste in faces.** Lately likes "business-like" sans such as Gotham; does not know
  how to use Univers; used DIN Italic he bought long ago because he was tired of the
  regular and light [stated: JDN 2018]. Fonts that last, like chairs that last, "have
  nothing unpleasant" rather than strong features; "free-font-site" quirkiness does not
  last [stated: JDN 2018].
- **Japanese with Latin.** For the INFOBAR A01 iida UI he had a Latin face made, based
  on Masanori Sakamoto's Deltro font, tuned to sit with the built-in Morisawa UD Shin Go
  M, because stock Android Latin did not pair [stated: k-tai Watch 2011].
- **When it fits.** Always: one or two families, picked by testing in context, with a
  Latin companion matched to the Japanese (or other non-Latin) face.

### 1.6 Typeset by nudging until it is "just right"

- **What.** "Tighten there, loosen here", over and over, until it lands at ちょうどいい;
  then he wants to stare at it with a whisky [stated: designing 2025]. He compares a
  veteran typographer's and a beginner's setting of the same text and font: "totally
  different" [stated: designing 2025].
- **Same method at other scales.** In game development he found the same work:
  "polishing away the unpleasant and conspicuous parts" bit by bit gives a lasting
  comfort, just as his day job is "arguing back and forth over typography placement"
  [stated: Game Watch 2023]. He redoes motion details "dozens of times", which is what
  makes it look like him [stated: Chizaizukan 2026].
- **Default it replaces.** Accepting the first layout the tool produces.

### 1.7 Modules on a grid that look right in any order

- **What.** For INFOBAR he reframed icon, widget and app as S, M and L of one module,
  and treated the icon grid as composition, like stacked hi-fi components that "line
  up crisply" (ピシッと) [stated: k-tai Watch 2011]. He kept the useful clutter of a
  Rakuten-style page but designed each module "so it looks clean however they are
  arranged" [stated: k-tai Watch 2011].
- **Vertical, width-aligned stacking.** He scrolled vertically "not to differentiate"
  but because when many modules are packed tightly, aligning widths feels best, so
  stacking down is cleanest; the layout scales to 2, 3, 4 or 5 columns [stated: k-tai
  Watch 2011].
- **Do not paint the hardware on the screen.** Tile-coloured UI echoing the phone's
  keys looked fake; "reproducing real material in virtual UI space makes the gap
  glaring", so he kept the UI neutral [stated: k-tai Watch 2011].
- **The feeling he aims for.** "The pleasure of precise things fitting in place",
  craft or fetish rather than platform [stated: k-tai Watch 2011].
- **When it fits.** Dashboards, home screens, catalogues, link collections: a
  customisable dense page that must still look composed.

### 1.8 Seventy percent understood, thirty percent not

- **What.** He aims for "about 7 parts understood, 3 not"; making it fully
  understandable, market-in style, is boring; show what "we clearly understand but the
  world doesn't share yet", in the right measure (いい塩梅) [stated: AXIS 2022]. For
  Design Ah he deliberately avoids "colours kids like"; he shows what is cool and "in
  the worst case it's fine if they don't get it" [stated: AXIS 2022].
- **Continuous small betrayal.** "Keep slightly exceeding or slightly betraying
  expectations"; the pleasure happens in the viewer's head, so he nudges their image of
  what will happen [stated: Chizaizukan 2026].
- **Default it replaces.** SNS-ad clarity where "all the grammar is visible" [stated:
  AXIS 2022].

### 1.9 Borrow another medium's grammar

- **What.** When a TV commercial must feel slightly off (違和感), he uses "web grammar"
  rather than "film grammar"; for web and interactive work, film grammar sometimes
  helps; crossing fields adds vocabulary [stated: AXIS 2022].
- **When it fits.** A page should feel unlike its category: borrow pacing, framing or
  interaction from a neighbouring medium (broadcast, print, game).

### 1.10 One interface carries the whole idea, and time is a material

- **What.** For Ecotonoha: "solve everything from presenting the concept to the
  details of how to use it with one interface; keep it strictly simple; build a
  simple interaction with various facets" [stated: SHIFT 2004]. He also names "the
  rhythm with which the site works... designing the time": a leaf a day, a habit,
  "design at regular intervals, which TV cannot do" [stated: SHIFT 2004].
- **When it fits.** Campaign and single-purpose pages: the interaction itself explains
  the offer, and the page changes on a daily or hourly rhythm.

### 1.11 Two layers in parallel instead of a fused whole

- **What.** For MoMA's Design and the Elastic Mind site he separated the two jobs, an
  expressive piece following the show's theme and an information site, into "two
  different layers that exist in parallel" rather than one fused piece [stated: MoMA
  2010].
- **When it fits.** When expression and information compete; let the playful layer
  sit behind or beside a plain, usable one.

### 1.12 Subtract, or pick up what was dropped

- **What.** Normal work keeps adding goodness until everyone is full; "suddenly pull
  something out and it's surprisingly good" [stated: Design no Temae 2025]. He cites
  Gunpei Yokoi's "lateral thinking with withered technology" and wants to "glean"
  ideas the web abandoned at each platform shift [stated: Design no Temae 2025; Web
  Designing 2025].
- **When it fits.** A page is crowded with features or effects; or a dated pattern
  (a clock, a guestbook, a marquee) can be revived with care.

### 1.13 Lean toward the computer side, even at some cost to ease

- **What.** He sees an interface as a water surface between air (human convenience)
  and water (the computer's convenience), and admits he leans to the computer side: he
  wants people to feel "computers are fun, algorithms are fun", and is "often told it's
  hard to use" [stated: Chizaizukan 2026]. In 2001: where the web is media "it should
  always involve new experiences and excitement... even if it is at a cost" [stated:
  Design Indaba 2001].
- **Counterweight.** At Business Architects he made "experimental menus nobody asked
  for" and had to rebuild them as "hard to use" [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008]; his
  early information architecture left pages "linked from nowhere" [stated: Web
  Designing 2025]. Use this move on the expressive layer, not on navigation.

## 2. Process and thinking

- **Engineering before design.** Inspired by a Gaudí book in high school, he aimed at
  architecture, ended up in civil engineering in Osamu Shinohara's landscape lab
  (roads, tunnels, bridges, Edo city structure), and learned that "landscape is born
  from relations between places" [stated: Kangaeru Hito 2006]. He then worked at a
  bridge-design firm (first job: Second Tomei Expressway; a 600 m water crossing)
  [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008; Design Indaba 2001].
- **What engineering taught him.** Civil structures are shaped by external factors
  (terrain, safety, speed); he found an aesthetic in solving the negatives "by
  engineering ingenuity rather than formal expression" [stated: WIRED 2016]. His hero
  was Robert Maillart; he admired Tange's Yoyogi gymnasium as "structure and form in
  complete harmony" [stated: Kangaeru Hito 2006]. He quotes Hiroshi Naito: "the setting
  of the time axis itself decides the form" [stated: WIRED 2016]. Engineering thinking
  is "going back and forth between whole and element" [stated: WIRED 2016]. Architecture
  and web share a core of rules (structure, code) with design as their appearance; the
  difference is time, 100 years against five minutes [stated: AXIS 2022].
- **Why he left.** A four-month design error he had to redo under life-safety pressure
  [stated: Kangaeru Hito 2006], and the span: a bridge designed at 25 is seen at 35;
  he wanted "many things in a shorter span" and a field with no constraints [stated:
  WIRED 2016].
- **Origin of the web work.** John Maeda's Reactive Books, where type scatters as the
  mouse moves, showed him "the material quality of the computer" [stated: Kangaeru
  Hito 2006; designing 2025]. Java and Director were too expensive, so he forced the
  Flash trial into a programming tool, "like making Space Invaders in Excel" [stated:
  Media Arts Plaza 2008]. He began by copying Maeda so well that Maeda emailed to
  complain; when they met he said he wanted to apply Maeda's method in society as the
  web spread, and Maeda said "good" [stated: GDR c. 2021; Design no Temae 2025].
- **Cannot draw.** "I design as someone who can't draw"; his rough sketches do not
  communicate to staff [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008]. So the sketch is a running
  program; at tha "everyone is a designer, and a designer is someone who writes code",
  because shaping the idea while implementing is "the best process" and people who
  can see the implementation take bolder risks [stated: Web Designing 2025].
- **Two ways of working.** Top-down planning toward a hypothesis, or start from
  whatever catches his attention and recombine by trial and error; the second is more
  fun but costly, and "when no-plan improvisation works it makes me happiest" [stated:
  Chizaizukan 2026]. He is weak at planning-first (企画から入る) and needs "discovered
  ideas, even by accident" for a strong whole [stated: Design no Temae 2025]. In 2004:
  ideas come "while moving my hands"; a flash at work or "a shape that appears by
  accident because of a bug" is luck worth keeping [stated: SHIFT 2004].
- **Clients.** He treats an order as a "topic" (お題), goes to the first meeting with a
  working demo, "here's what came out" (もの見せ勝負); rejected demos become stock
  [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008]. Dialogue matters most because he "seeks new
  motivation within a restriction" [stated: SHIFT 2004]. Kashiwa Sato sent him only the
  core phrase "ultra-rationality with an aesthetic sense" and a short text for UNIQLO;
  Sato says that is all Nakamura needs [stated and reported: CINRA 2011]. For SoHo
  Sato gave him "always updating, always moving" and "a system of a different concept
  arrived" [stated: Kangaeru Hito 2006]. He finds ideas in meetings: "my brain has
  limits, so I use other people's brains" [stated: AXIS 2022].
- **Good versus pleasing.** From Kashiwa Sato he learned that what he finds most
  pleasant and what works in business art direction differ; for Sato "goodness arises
  in the viewer, not in the thing"; from Naoto Fukasawa's question "いい or よい?" he
  took permission to pursue subjective rightness (いい) [stated: designing 2025].
- **Seasons and R&D.** tha's fiscal year runs so that September to March is client
  work and April onward adds non-client development, to "plant seeds" and test ideas
  before they go stale [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008; MoMA 2010]. In-house products
  exist "to raise technique; client work alone only uses the tech needed now"
  [stated: WIRED 2016].
- **Style is a by-product.** His site is "a study of expressive technique, not
  expression"; individuality is what still leaks out after thinking hard about how
  things look objectively; his motto is "no more self-consciousness" (ノーモア自意識)
  [stated: Media Arts Plaza 2008].
- **Core: making things move.** After tiring of tech-driven patterns (Kinect, AR) and
  refusing offers to write Flash books, he realised he likes "moving things",
  expressions of "dynamic order", in any medium [stated: designing 2025]. "The web was
  just the medium that carried moving things" [stated: GDR c. 2021].
- **Constraints.** Design is interesting because it has constraints, set by yourself
  or given by others [stated: Design no Temae 2025]; designers perform better with
  some restriction than with a free theme [stated: JDN TMA 2020].
- **Team.** He reaches team decisions by working "doggedly until everyone agrees"
  rather than pushing one idea through [stated: Denfaminico 2023]; he wants extreme,
  high-impact work and relies on partners such as Mizuguchi to balance it [stated:
  Denfaminico 2023; Game Developer 2023]. He praises Taku Satoh on Design Ah for
  building a good working flow by cheering ideas [stated: 6mirai 2013].

## 3. Taste

- **Likes.** John Maeda (Reactive Books, 12 o'clocks); Han Hoogerbrugge, "non-logical
  simple fun" [stated: SHIFT 2004, spelled "Hooger Brudge" there]; the del.icio.us
  bookmark module spread across sites, "like a beautiful structure" [stated: WIRED 2016;
  GDR]; the iOS game FROST (guiding light particles), his favourite game and a source
  for HUMANITY; Playdead's LIMBO and INSIDE for "a sense of omission and the layout of
  light and shadow"; Ueda's Shadow of the Colossus; Miyazaki's crowd shots in The Wind
  Rises [stated: Game*Spark 2023]. Architecture models with tiny faceless figures,
  concrete, glass, stone and steel; brutalism and Tadao Ando as stage references for
  HUMANITY [stated: Game*Spark 2023]. Craft-like competition entries (fired earthworm
  casts, dandelion fluff) [stated: JDN TMA 2020]. "Interactive at a level of a click
  getting a reaction, loved without reason" [stated: WIRED 2016].
- **Visual identity, as reported.** MoMA's writer credits him with putting "life:
  flexibility, mutability, playfulness, depth, speed" into the static web [reported:
  MoMA 2010]. Kashiwa Sato: "the first person to use programs like paint", between a
  structure-minded architect and a graphic designer [reported: CINRA 2011]. Mizuguchi:
  his ideas look intuitive but carry a background; his thinking is "architectural"
  [reported: Denfaminico 2023].
- **Japanese sensibility.** Japanese designers are distinctive less in content than in
  "how they set context: where and how to place content" [stated: CINRA 2011]. On
  Design Ah's purpose he wishes "doing nothing unnecessary" (余計なことをしない) would
  spread, against town-hall tourist-spot clutter [stated: Design no Temae 2025].
- **Dislikes.** Database-style creation (swap eyes and mouths) and assembling Apple UI
  kits, which he expects AI to replace and always found boring [stated: Design no
  Temae 2025]; ads whose grammar is fully visible [stated: AXIS 2022]; over-featured
  free fonts [stated: JDN 2018]; Android's stock icons ("ダサい"; the usual fix is a
  cushion behind them) [stated: k-tai Watch 2011]; designers over-talking design's
  social role ("cooks don't say cooks belong at the heart of companies") [stated:
  Design no Temae 2025]; "design poems" that give an LED an overblown meaning [stated:
  SNDC 2022]; prompting image AI, "like watermelon-splitting: a bit to the right!"; he
  prefers the linear response of typing code [stated: Chizaizukan 2026].
- **Ambition about time.** Early on: "I want to create something that will last
  forever" [stated: Design Indaba 2001]; later: hopes to leave "0.01 lines of history"
  as web work disappears [stated: WIRED 2016].
- **Teaching.** Copy what you love at 120 percent and see what lies beyond [stated:
  Design no Temae 2025]; watch the person behind a design, since rivals motivate
  [stated: CINRA 2024].
