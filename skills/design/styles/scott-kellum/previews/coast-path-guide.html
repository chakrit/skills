6. Tregarrick Ferry to Polwenna Cove

11.2 km, about 4 h 10 min, 255 m of ascent, a tidal beach at 5.8 km

This stage starts with the foot ferry across the Tregarrick river and ends at the lifeboat slip in Polwenna Cove. The first 4.6 km climb along slate cliffs to Hennock Beacon. After the beach the path turns inland on field edges over Withel Downs and drops through gorse to the cove; it is grassy, wide and easy to follow all the way.

One stretch needs planning. At Gwithel sands the path goes down onto the beach for 400 metres, and the beach floods. Work out your day from the tide and the ferry before you work out anything else.

The ground

The ferry and the tide

The ferry is a twelve-seat launch that Jory Pascoe runs from the slip below the Ferry Inn. In winter it crosses on demand from 09:30 to 16:30, Thursday to Sunday; ring the bell on the post. The crossing takes 10 minutes and costs £3.

Allow two hours from the far slip to Gwithel sands at an ordinary pace.

Off the first boat at 09:40, you reach the sands about 11:40. On Thursday that is 28 minutes after they close. The afternoon window opens at 15:37, which leaves the last 5 km to Polwenna Cove in the dark; the sun sets at about 16:20. On Thursday, walk the stage the other way: leave Polwenna Cove by 08:30, reach the sands before 10:30, and take any afternoon boat across at the end. On Friday you would have 21 minutes in hand, which a headwind will eat. Saturday and Sunday work with time to spare. With no ferry on Monday, the way round is 9 km by the inland road through Tregarrick village.

Gwithel sands is under water except from four hours before low water until four hours after. The cliff above it cannot be climbed anywhere along those 400 metres; in 2024 the Kestle lifeboat took two walkers off the rocks there. If you reach the beach outside the times in the table, wait at the top of the rough steps below Hennock Beacon, or at the top of the concrete steps if you are walking from Polwenna.

Directions

  1. From the ferry slip, walk up the lane past the Ferry Inn and turn right at the top onto the cliff path, signed with the Saltway’s benchmark arrow. Keep the sea on your right all day.
  2. The path climbs steadily, dropping once into a combe at 3 km, before the long pull up to Hennock Beacon at 4.6 km. The old coastguard lookout has a bench, and on a clear day you can see south past Carn Du to Hellan Point.
  3. Go down the rough steps on the far side of the beacon to Gwithel sands. Cross 400 metres of beach close under the cliff, where the sand is firm, to the concrete steps at the south end.
  4. Above the steps the path follows a field edge for 2.5 km to Withel Downs. The trig pillar on your left is the highest point of the stage. There is no shelter up here.
  5. Drop through gorse to Polwenna Cove. The stage ends at the lifeboat slip; buses to Kestle leave from the top of the hill at 15:50 and 17:20.
