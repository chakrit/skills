You get your old parts back

Every chain, cable, brake pad and tube Dee takes off your bike goes into a paper bag with your job number on it. The bag is hanging from your handlebars when you collect.

Hold the old chain up next to the new one and you can see the stretch. Look at the pads and you can see why the brakes howled.

Don't want the bag? Leave it on the counter. The metal goes to the scrap merchant on Furnace Row, and what he pays goes in the jar for the Saturday kids' fix-it morning.

House rules

  1. We don't sell bikes.

    Dee would rather fix the one you have. Ask her where to find a good second-hand one.

  2. In before 9, out by 5.

    Commuters jump the queue on weekday mornings. Everyone else gets a time on the tag.

  3. A price before we start.

    And a phone call before we go a penny over it.

  4. No e-bike batteries or motors.

    Their wheels, brakes and gears are fine. The electrics go back to whoever sold them.

  5. Lock it to the railings.

    The drainpipe belongs to the flat upstairs.
