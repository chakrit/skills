# Yugo Nakamura: synthesis

The design model behind `style.md`, drawn from four research streams in `notes/`.
Citations name the note and its section: `own §1 "list is the interface"` is that move in
`notes/own.md`; `work Move "brand mark as a tile"` and `work table` refer to
`notes/work.md`; `talks 1.4` and `talks §2` to `notes/talks.md`; `accounts` sections by
heading.

Evidence marks follow the notes: **(said)** is his own statement in an interview or a
tha.jp project text; **(seen)** was looked at in a browser, on a live page or a published
still; **(code)** rests on markup or script only; **(reported)** is someone else's
account. Most of his early web work no longer runs and is documented only on YouTube,
which was out of scope, so the model leans on what he says and on tha's own descriptions
more than on looking (work, "Limits").

## 1. The core idea: design the behaviour, and let the picture follow

Nakamura trained as a civil engineer and designed bridges before he made web pages
(talks §2 "Engineering before design"). He describes the work as "composition of logics"
(own §2) (said): a page is a set of rules that move things, and what the visitor sees is
the consequence. Three statements carry the model:

- **Motion is the effect of a force.** "What I design is the relation between the surface
  motion and its driving force"; leaves moving tell you the wind is blowing (talks 1.1)
  (said). The force is something the visitor can feel: the pointer, gravity, the beat,
  the minute, the wind in their own city (work Move "outside rhythm") (said).
- **The core is moving things.** After tiring of technology-led fashions he decided what
  he likes is "moving things", expressions of "dynamic order", in any medium; the web was
  "just the medium that carried moving things" (talks §2 "Core: making things move")
  (said).
- **Style is a by-product.** His site was "a study of expressive technique, not
  expression"; individuality is what leaks out after thinking hard about how things look
  objectively; his motto is "no more self-consciousness" (talks §2 "Style is a
  by-product") (said).

A generic page does the reverse: it composes a picture (hero, sections, grid) and then
adds motion that eases in because the page can move (talks 1.1 "Default it replaces").
For Nakamura the behaviour comes first and decides the picture.

## 2. Where ideas come from

- **A good brick, not a room list.** Top-down design starts from the brief ("family of
  four, 3LDK"); he starts from "a really interesting brick" and asks what house makes it
  shine, even if the house turns out larger or odder than planned, because that "breaks
  the pre-arranged harmony" (talks 1.2) (said). He calls himself weak at planning first
  and needs ideas "discovered, even by accident" (talks §2 "Two ways of working")
  (said). Ecotonoha began as a demo where a click grew a tree; typing a message was added
  during production (talks 1.2) (said).
- **Part to whole.** Find one detail you find beautiful, join two or three, then imagine
  a hundred, "simulating from the part to the whole" (talks 1.2, WIRED 2016) (said).
  HUMANITY grew from three Boids rules applied to birds and then to people, and its story
  came last (talks 1.2; accounts "Many visitors, one shared surface") (said).
- **Collage at the level of the algorithm.** Take a thing everyone knows as a fixed
  reference (a clock, a logo, a message board, the Mona Lisa) and drive it with a rule
  from somewhere else, so a "new balance, new sense of distance" appears (talks 1.3)
  (said; the phrase itself is reported, talks §4). The clock is "the primal form of the
  algorithmic and also something everyone knows" (talks 1.3) (said). Clocks recur across
  twenty years of his own and client work: DropClock, Kaze to Desktop, LIFE COLOR CLOCK,
  station and tower clocks (own §3; work §3) (said, works list).
- **Data drawn as type, people as material.** amaztype lays search results into the
  letters of the query; FONTPARK lets people rebuild glyph parts into pictures;
  ecotonoha turns each posted message into a leaf on one tree (work Move "data drawn as
  type") (said; FONTPARK still seen).
- **Borrow another medium's grammar.** A TV spot that must feel slightly off uses web
  grammar; web work sometimes uses film grammar (talks 1.9) (said).
- **Pick up what was dropped.** He cites Gunpei Yokoi's "lateral thinking with withered
  technology" and wants to glean ideas the web abandoned at each platform shift (talks
  1.12) (said).
- **Copy, then go past.** He began by copying John Maeda so closely that Maeda
  complained; he teaches copying what you love at 120 percent (talks §2 "Origin";
  talks §3 "Teaching") (said).

## 3. Process

1. **Take the brief as a topic.** A client's order is a お題 (topic) to answer; he seeks
   "new motivation within a restriction", and finds constraints, given or self-set, what
   make design interesting (talks §2 "Clients", "Constraints") (said). Kashiwa Sato sent
   him only a core phrase, "ultra-rationality with an aesthetic sense", and Sato says that
   is all he needs (talks §2) (said, reported).
2. **Sketch in running code.** "I design as someone who can't draw"; the sketch is a
   working program. At tha "a designer is someone who writes code", because shaping the
   idea while implementing it is "the best process" (talks §2 "Cannot draw") (said).
3. **Show a demo at the first meeting.** "Here's what came out"; rejected demos become
   stock for later (talks §2 "Clients") (said). A Flash prototype set the smoothness
   target for the INFOBAR phone UI (accounts §2) (said).
4. **Choose type late, by swapping it into the motion.** The motion exists first; he
   swaps font files into it until one fits (talks 1.5) (said).
5. **Nudge until it is just right.** "Tighten there, loosen here", over and over, until
   it lands at ちょうどいい; he redoes motion details "dozens of times" (talks 1.6)
   (said).
6. **Polish by removing irritations.** In game work as in typesetting, comfort comes from
   "polishing away the unpleasant and conspicuous parts" bit by bit (talks 1.6) (said).
7. **Subtract.** Normal work keeps adding until everyone is full; "suddenly pull something
   out and it's surprisingly good" (talks 1.12) (said).
8. **Use other people's brains.** Ideas come in meetings; decisions are reached by working
   "doggedly until everyone agrees" (talks §2 "Clients", "Team") (said).

## 4. Composition

- **The list is the interface; the work plays behind it.** tha.jp's home is a black field
  with a quiet left-aligned stack of project titles; hovering one brightens it and plays
  that project full-bleed behind the list, which stays put. No hero, no cards, no intro
  copy (own §1 "list is the interface") (seen). The grid of stills exists one click away
  on /works (seen).
- **One interface carries the whole idea.** For ecotonoha: "solve everything from
  presenting the concept to the details of how to use it with one interface" (talks
  1.10) (said). Accounts describe the same in the MoMA Design and the Elastic Mind site,
  where the index itself is a behaving system rather than labelled categories (accounts
  "Make the interface itself the expressive piece") (reported).
- **Two layers in parallel.** When expression and information compete, he splits them
  into "two different layers that exist in parallel" rather than fusing them (talks 1.11)
  (said). He leans to the computer's side and is "often told it's hard to use" (talks
  1.13) (said); his early experimental menus had to be rebuilt as hard to use. The
  expressive layer takes the risk; the plain layer stays usable.
- **The site as a numbered lab notebook.** yugop.com was a run of numbered small
  experiments, each with a one-line all-caps caption naming the logic first and the
  instruction second ("MOTION LOGIC WITH RIGID BODY DYNAMICS. DRAG EACH CELL TO
  INTERACT"), plus a last-update date (own §1 "numbered lab notebook") (seen, 2005
  capture).
- **Modules that look right in any order.** For INFOBAR he made icon, widget and app the
  S, M and L of one module and designed each to "look clean however they are arranged";
  modules are width-aligned and stack downward because, packed tightly, aligned widths
  look best; the layout scales from two to five columns (talks 1.7) (said). The feeling
  he aims for is "the pleasure of precise things fitting in place" (talks 1.7) (said).
- **The brand mark as a tile, then a game board.** UNIQLO GRID tiles the square mark on
  graph paper in many sizes and lets every visitor move them on one shared board; the
  same idea runs from the 2006 SoHo teaser to the 2021–2025 façade animations (work Move
  "brand mark as a tile") (seen, stills).
- **Everyone on one surface.** One global board, one tree, one wall of posts; a scrolling
  log of moves and a participation count are part of the display (work Move "everyone on
  one surface"; accounts "Many visitors, one shared surface") (said, reported).
- **The studio stays plain on its own index.** From 2009 to 2014 tha.jp was a near-default
  serif blog; today it is black or white with small Helvetica Neue (own §1 "studio's own
  site stays plain") (seen). The pieces perform; the frame does not (inference, marked as
  such in the note).

## 5. Type

- **Letters are the knot of form and content.** Typography is "relation-operation across
  both sides" (talks 1.4) (said). Song titles drawn in a single-stroke font whose centre
  lines ripple with the track's waveform; overlapping green letters that read as leaves;
  glyphs polygonised so they wobble (talks 1.4) (said).
- **Text that decodes itself.** tha.jp's tagline appears as random ASCII and settles
  letter by letter into the sentence; the script steps each character code back toward
  its target one code per frame (own §1 "text that decodes itself") (seen, code). The
  2005 yugop.com captions did the same (seen). Short all-caps lines only; never body
  copy.
- **Few faces, as few as possible.** He adds roughly one font a year and dreams of one
  ultimate Mincho (talks 1.5) (said). Lasting fonts, like lasting chairs, "have nothing
  unpleasant" rather than strong features; over-featured free fonts do not last (talks
  1.5) (said). He likes "business-like" sans such as Gotham, used DIN Italic when tired of
  the regular, chose Ten Mincho for an exhibition because it "has presence but isn't
  cloying" (talks 1.5) (said).
- **His own setting.** tha.jp: Helvetica Neue Thin and Roman at 13.5–15 px with a few
  25–42 px sizes, Noto Sans JP for Japanese; small grey all-caps neo-grotesque labels
  mixed with Japanese (own §1, §3) (code, seen). The 2005 shell used a bitmap pixel font
  (own §3) (seen).
- **Latin matched to the other script.** For INFOBAR he had a Latin face made to sit with
  the Japanese system font, because stock Android Latin did not pair (talks 1.5) (said).
- **Body text stays still.** Every type motion in the notes is display, title, wordmark,
  caption or idle state (talks 1.4 "When it fits"; own §1).

## 6. Colour, image and surface

- **Monochrome ground, colour from the work.** Black or white fields, small neutral type,
  with colour arriving only in the footage or the piece (own §3; work §3) (seen).
- **Flat saturated colour when the piece is graphic.** UNIQLO GRID's cells in red,
  magenta, purple, teal, grey and black on graph paper; KASHIWA SATO's portfolio as
  horizontal bands of flat swatches (work table) (seen).
- **No painted hardware.** He rejected a UI that echoed the phone's coloured keys:
  "reproducing real material in virtual UI space makes the gap glaring"; the virtual
  layer stays neutral, ordered and fast (talks 1.7; accounts "Restraint against literal
  skeuomorphism") (said).
- **Abstract figures on a bare stage.** In HUMANITY people are small uniform figures on
  brutalist architecture so the movement of the mass carries meaning (accounts "Abstract
  figures, sterile stage") (reported). Likes: architecture models with tiny faceless
  figures, LIMBO's "sense of omission and the layout of light and shadow" (talks §3)
  (said).

## 7. Motion and time

- **Tie every movement to an input or a law.** The thrown apple's parabola, Mario's jump,
  the rule you feel governing a line drawn in 3D (talks 1.1) (said). The yugop.com home
  page held a MoMA curator for minutes with the "simple physics of bouncing" (talks 1.1)
  (reported).
- **Time is a material.** "The rhythm with which the site works... designing the time": a
  leaf a day, a habit, "design at regular intervals, which TV cannot do" (talks 1.10)
  (said). UNIQLOCK, whose credits are unconfirmed for him, ran all day with a routine on
  the hour (accounts "Design with time") (reported).
- **Few gestures with physical meaning.** Drag across to cut, circle to merge,
  double-click to turn; the Ars Electronica jury praised "highly economical expressive
  means" (accounts "Few, direct gestures") (reported).
- **Make the beat exact.** UT LOOP! got its own audio engine so every cut lands on the
  beat (work table; work §2) (said). Precision in timing is part of the design brief.

## 8. Measure of clarity

- **Seventy percent understood, thirty percent not.** Fully understandable "market-in"
  design is boring; he shows what "we clearly understand but the world doesn't share
  yet", in the right measure (talks 1.8) (said).
- **Keep slightly betraying expectations.** The pleasure happens in the viewer's head, so
  he keeps nudging their picture of what will happen next (talks 1.8) (said).
- **Do nothing unnecessary.** He wishes 余計なことをしない ("doing nothing unnecessary")
  would spread, against tourist-spot clutter (talks §3 "Japanese sensibility") (said).
- **Setting over content.** Japanese designers stand out in "how they set context: where
  and how to place content" (talks §3) (said).

## 9. Taste summary

Likes: John Maeda's Reactive Books and 12 o'clocks; "non-logical simple fun"; the
del.icio.us bookmark module as "a beautiful structure"; FROST, LIMBO, INSIDE, Shadow of
the Colossus; crowds, flocks and queues; brutalist and model-scale architecture; charm
and whimsy over solemnity; work that is "fun, beautiful, interesting, and exciting"
(talks §3; accounts §3) (said, reported). Dislikes: assembling UI kits and swapping parts
database-style; ads whose grammar is fully visible; over-featured fonts; painted
material in UI; "design poems" that give a light an overblown meaning; designers
over-talking design's role (talks §3 "Dislikes") (said).

## Cautions and gaps

- **Attribution.** Several famous tha pieces were art-directed by colleagues (MUJI web
  pieces and FFFFOUND! by Yosuke Abe, LIFE by Jun Yoshihara, SPRZ NY by Hideki Owa), and
  UNIQLOCK's credits do not name him (work, intro; accounts §4). They are left out of the
  model, except UNIQLOCK where marked.
- **Little seen in motion.** The Flash work cannot be run and its video record was out of
  scope; how ecotonoha, amaztype or the yugop.com sketches moved is known from text
  (own §4; work §4). The motion rules here rest on his statements more than on looking.
- **Ban conflicts.** Four habits touch `../../references/bans.md`:
  - Small all-caps labels are his everyday type. `style.md` keeps them for the list,
    captions, navigation and meta, never as a stamp above a section heading.
  - yugop.com numbers its experiments. Numbers belong to the items of a list or index;
    `style.md` forbids a number set above a heading as a kicker.
  - The INFOBAR modules are dark filled tiles. Filled tiles with an icon, a title and a
    blurb repeated in a grid are cards; `style.md` keeps the module idea (one family of
    sizes, aligned widths, any order) and draws modules with alignment and space, or as
    the live object itself, never as boxes.
  - The UNIQLOCK hub's "MUSIC. DANCE. CLOCK." is fragment copy; its credit is unconfirmed
    and `style.md` does not use it.
- **Phones.** The evidence is INFOBAR (vertical, width-aligned stacking that scales by
  column count) and UTme! (a shake of the phone rearranges the drawing) (talks 1.7; work
  table). The phone guidance in `style.md` extends these.
- **No grid statement for web pages.** No source speaks to grid systems for web layouts;
  the grid evidence is INFOBAR and UNIQLO GRID (talks §4).
- **Reduced motion.** No source speaks to it. `style.md` adds a still state so the force
  still reads without movement, as an accessibility floor rather than his practice.
