Dead Wax Records DW 061 Liner notes by Ruth Ambler

Two records, played all the way through

Once a month we close the shop at six, set out forty chairs, turn the lights down and play two records from the first groove to the last. Nobody talks until the kettle goes on between them.

Ruth Ambler opened Dead Wax in 2009 in the old bobbin store on Shuttle Yard, with four crates of records and the turntable she still plays them on. The listening club started three years later, when a customer asked whether he could hear a whole record before he paid for it. Ruth said yes, and that she would like to hear it too.

This month she has picked two records made fifty years apart that sound as if they came out of the same room. Low Tide Hymnal was recorded by Marguerite Okafor-Lind in a church hall in Whitby in 1974, with one microphone and an upright piano nobody had tuned for a decade. Second Shift is the Halden Street Wire Ensemble's 2023 record of guitars, a bowed saw and their own mill building at night.

Both are in the racks now and will still be there afterwards. Nobody will try to sell you anything. If you want to take one home, Ruth is at the counter until quarter to eleven.

Bring a coat for the walk back across the yard. The chairs are the shop's own, the biscuits are from the bakery next door, and the hooks by the door are for your phone as much as your coat.

Ruth Ambler, Dead Wax Records
