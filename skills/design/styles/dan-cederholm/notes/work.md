## 1. Moves

### 1.1 A specimen poster that sets the font in its own world

- **What.** Each font is promoted with a poster packed edge to edge with words in that
  font, with the outer words cropped by the frame. The words come from the font's source
  world [observed]:
  - Hubano: cigar-trade vocabulary and prices ("HAVANA CUBA", "$53 PER CABINET",
    "SECO MIDDLE LIGERO LEAF") with a pointing hand and cigar icons, on red.
  - Turbeau: the early 1980s ("'83 PONTIAC FIREBIRD TRANS AM", "MICHAEL KNIGHT",
    "KITT"), in cream, orange, teal and green on dark navy.
  - Scorekard: baseball ("9 INNINGS", "Bases empty", "field box seat 50¢").
  - Beeswacks: "BUSYBEE! 1944 HONEYCOMB" in honey yellows.

  Each poster uses three to five flat colours and no gradients. Many lines mix sizes,
  weights and the face's icons.
- **Replaces.** The "quick brown fox" specimen and the lorem-ipsum hero.
- **Fits.** Any hero or poster meant to show a typeface or a brand's voice. Write the
  copy from the subject's own world (its trade terms, numbers, prices and dates) and
  let it run off the edges.

### 1.2 Pastiche a familiar vernacular mark, with your own name in it

- **What.** "Simple Cruz" redraws the SimpleBits badge as the Santa Cruz skateboard dot:
  a red disc with a black rim, a slanted yellow wordmark with a 3D extrusion, and "NEVER
  PERFECT SINCE 2002" running round the edge. "Sans Prix" is a racing badge with a
  chequered flag in a rounded box. The SERIF pin is a sheriff's star [observed].
- **Replaces.** A neutral logo lockup.
- **Fits.** Merchandise, stickers and playful sub-brands. It works when the source is
  widely known and the joke is a pun on the subject.

### 1.3 A plain frame for other people's work, with the personality in the logo

- **What.** In the early Dribbble interface the members' shots supplied all the colour.
  The chrome was grey and black, in Helvetica, with one pink accent taken from the script
  wordmark. The playfulness lived in the basketball names (shots, rebounds) [stated and
  reported; see `talks.md` 1 and `accounts.md`]. Not seen in the browser.
- **Replaces.** A branded, decorated interface competing with the content.
- **Fits.** Galleries, portfolios, marketplaces.

### 1.4 Craft shown as a physical object

- **What.**
  - Letterpress prints, signed and on coloured stock.
  - The "Twenty Bits" books, bound in cloth with a ribbon marker.
  - Early Dribbble members were invited with a mailed t-shirt and a handwritten note
    [reported, `accounts.md`].
  - The 2026 shop is photographed as real objects on a table [observed].
- **Fits.** A digital brand that wants to feel handmade. Put one tangible, imperfect
  artefact in the story.

## 2. Process and thinking

- He starts from a found artefact and keeps its quirks: the odd crossbars of Hubano, the
  "lowercase-ish Y" of Kyroh [stated, `own.md`].
- He shows work in progress on Dribbble and lets feedback shape it [stated, `own.md`
  1.2].
- On a running product he ships small adaptive steps instead of a rebuild [stated,
  `own.md`].

## 3. Taste

- Americana and vintage trade ephemera: cigar boxes, luggage labels, baseball, 1980s
  cars and TV, skateboard graphics, hardware ("Measure twice, cut once").
- Warm flat palettes: red-orange, mustard, cream, navy, teal and forest green. Rough
  edges and ink spread instead of crisp vectors.
- Puns as product: Serif as a sheriff's star, "Forget Me Knot" in rope lettering, a
  cheese-sandwich ampersand.
