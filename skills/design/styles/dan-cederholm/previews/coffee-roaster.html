Coffee roasted on Derby Street, a short walk from the wharf

Salem's ships used to sail to Sumatra and come home with pepper. We roast the coffee from those same hills in a 15-kilo Diedrich behind the café, twice a week, and sell it in bags with the date written on by hand.

Bags go out on Tuesdays and Fridays, by post or by bicycle if you live inside Route 128 and don't mind waiting until after lunch.

Who's burning it →

Farthest Port started in 2012 in a garage in South Salem with a one-kilo roaster and a fire extinguisher we used once. Maria roasts. Dave drives the van, fixes the grinders and answers the email. The cat is called Molasses and has no job.

The name comes from the city seal: a ship under sail and a Latin line about going to the farthest port of the rich East. Salem merchants really did, for pepper, tea and a bit of coffee. We get ours from importers in Boston now, which takes about a week and involves no scurvy.
