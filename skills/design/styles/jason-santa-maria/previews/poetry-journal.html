This issue went to the printer in Leith on the ninth of November, the day the haar sat on the city from breakfast until dark and the lamps on the Mound stayed on all afternoon. We had not planned a fog issue. Five of the poems that came in over the summer had it in them, Fiona Dalgleish sent a sequence of eleven, and by September there was no arguing with it.

Morag Lennie’s poem opens the issue because it starts on a stair most of our readers have climbed, at the top of Advocate’s Close, and goes down. Catrìona NicDhòmhnaill’s “Am Bàta” is printed in Gaelic with her own English beside it. Thirty-eight pages, sewn.

Advocate’s Close

Morag Lennie

Page 3

The fog came up the steps before we did and waited at the top, the way my father waited at the turn of every stair, not tired, he said, just letting the stair know.

Iron runs down the wall below the railing, a brown it took a hundred winters to arrive at. Somebody has a lamp on in a second floor at three o’clock, which in December is the evening.

We go down into it. The Forth is out there under all that white, the boats at Leith are out there, and the Balmoral clock, three minutes fast, keeps the count for the rest of us.

At the foot, Cockburn Street: a bus, a man with chips, his breath and theirs. I put my hand flat on the sandstone. It is wet, and cold, and still the wall.
