## Findings

### Process: what he does first

- [stated] The first step is to define objectives with Why questions; the second is to set
  them aside so they do not crush early ideas: "The second step, however, should be to put
  those objectives in a drawer." (SoD 03-improvisation-and-limitations, 2012)
- [stated] Getting started is phrasing the questions "What are the objectives of the
  project?" and "What are we actually building this for?", which "must be answered before
  any design begins". (Print, 2015-09-24)
- [stated] He wants clarity at both ends and treats the middle as open: "I want a lot of
  clarity at 10,000 feet, and I want a lot of clarity at two feet. Everything in between
  becomes the opportunity." (Print, 2015-09-24)
- [stated] He prefers being given an objective to a task: "'Realign our company to be
  xyz,' rather than 'redesign our letterhead.' How is tasks. Why is objectives."
  (why-vs-how, 2013-03-22)
- [stated] His web job is to find what is true about the client first: "help my clients
  identify and express the one or two uniquely true things about their project or
  company, then enhance it through a memorable design with a light touch."
  (everything-easy, 2018-02-09)
- [stated] He picks one thing a piece should do, then works hard on the best way to do
  it: "Spend a lot of time choosing that one thing that a piece of design or an
  illustration should try to do." (ten-principles, 2009-02-09)
- [stated] To build momentum he first imagines the worst possible way to do the project,
  then brings the objectives back as the rudder. (SoD 03-improvisation-and-limitations)
- [stated] He sets limits before starting, sorted into content, structure and tone, so
  he can say yes while working: "Saying no beforehand allows yes to be said
  unequivocally while working." (SoD 03-improvisation-and-limitations)
- [stated] He starts from the most limiting environment and lets its rules spread to the
  rest of the system. For The Shape of Design: "I needed to start with the most limiting
  factors, develop some rules, then see how those rules applied to the rest of the design
  system". (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] He analyses each project through three levers, message, tone and format, and
  re-examines the defaults of each at the start. (SoD 04-form-and-magic)
- [stated] He says he "almost always start[s] stuck", and the first step is to drop
  preconceptions "that are easy to dress up as experience or expertise". On the
  Creative Works poster, what unstuck him was focusing on words instead of images: the
  image clicked once the talk had a title. (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Sketching: cheap paper, so the first bad ideas cost nothing (ten-principles,
  2009); a wooden pencil and loose copier paper, each page numbered and circled so the
  order is visible, the stack thrown away at the end of the job (Print, 2015-09-24);
  printed outlines, 6 or 8 to a page, "Mistakes don't burn as much" (Grain Edit,
  2009-03-12).
- [stated] He finds the idea while working rather than arriving with it: "I like to
  'unearth' the idea during the process." (Grain Edit, 2009-03-12)
- [stated] Illustration sequence in 2009: "Research > Brainstorming > Freak Out > Sketch
  > Approval > Render Final." Rendering went silhouette, then flat blocks in "radical
  colors" to judge the shapes alone, then palette, then details, then edges and texture.
  He judged an idea from the rough: "it's 80% about the idea and 20% about the
  rendering". (Grain Edit, 2009-03-12)
- [stated] When stuck on screen, move to paper: "print it out and push it around in real
  space. Change contexts when you're stuck." (advice, 2010-08-20)
- [stated] He keeps a wall of quotes, postcards and printouts above his desk and
  rearranges them by hand; the poster concept came from adapting an image already in his
  talk deck. "Everyone should have a wall." (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Talks and essays: images and quotes dropped loosely into a Keynote file, then
  grouped, with slides of joining words ("BUT", "THEREFORE") to make a story; "It works
  much better for me than an outline." (back-to-the-cave, 2017-04-15) In On Margins he
  adds index cards on a wall, the deck as "a visual outline", then writing in full and
  reading aloud. (On Margins 002)
- [stated] Self-initiated work: "once through, cleanly": think, sketch a little, then
  make it in one sitting; if it is bad, walk away and start over, keeping nothing. He
  says this is "probably irresponsible to work this way when other people are involved".
  (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)

### Process: before layout, and on the web

- [stated] He starts every layout at phone width, because it reduces the problem to the
  order of elements in one stack and settles hierarchy first: "you're beginning with
  really foundational design choices, which is how am I going to express the hierarchy".
  Wider layouts then "inherit" it. (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] He uses a canvas tool (Sketch or Illustrator) only to settle colours side by
  side, the typeface, relative type sizes and hierarchy, then moves to code: "for the
  most part, I just sort of hop over to markup as soon as I can." (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] In markup he feels out "what the breakpoints should be, or the measure on our
  type". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Sketch content arrangements before drawing a page frame: "it's better to come
  up with an arrangement of elements and assign them to a size, rather than the other way
  around. We need to start drawing, then put the box around it." He cites Kasper
  Laigaard's content lock-up sketches as the example. (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Design how things build up, not how they break down: "Our words make it seem
  that we're designing how elements break down, but really, we should be focusing on how
  they build up." (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Design for every state of the content: "What does the page look like if it is
  empty? If it is full? And every possibility in between on mobile?" Working with
  software "is defining and designing conditions for a whole set of possibilities."
  (super-hi-foreword, 2017-04-11)
- [stated] He handles complexity by changing the design, not the tools: "Instead of
  changing my tools or workflow, I change my design. It's like designing a house so it's
  easy to build". (everything-easy, 2018-02-09)
- [stated] His listed technical needs for web work: "simple, responsive layout", "web
  fonts and nicely set text", "performant, scalable images". (everything-easy,
  2018-02-09)
- [stated] He redesigns his own site every year. (RWD, 2017-03-13)

### Process: what he shows a client, and how a design changes

- [stated] For responsive work he delivers rules, not a single artifact: "responsive
  designs are not really artifacts, they're like sets of rules for how content can
  adapt". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] For the NPR player's in-between states he showed motion with Keynote Magic
  Move, then a small markup-and-JavaScript prototype. (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] He wants words for behaviours so fewer prototypes are needed: on responsive
  projects he "had to jump into code, and waste time writing non-production markup and
  CSS to prototype a behavior so the developer could see it." (what-screens-want,
  2013-11-14)
- [stated] Midway through, he finds undefined "dark corners" and asks the people
  involved to define them: "I realize after working on this for six weeks that we never
  talked about Y". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Show work early and incomplete; he quotes Ed Catmull, "We show it every
  day—when it's incomplete", and asks for feedback loops that are "tight and open".
  (on-iteration, 2010-01-10)
- [stated] He stays open to new ideas longer: "Two-thirds through is where the good
  surprises tend to happen." (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Criticism should grow with the idea; he accepts every contribution early
  ("Yes, and") and edits late. (SoD 03-improvisation-and-limitations)
- [stated] On critique, his early error was "believing that the critique was for the work
  and about its creator, rather than about the work and for its creator"; and "we don't
  need to be nice, but we do need to be kind". (the-wall, 2011-12-14)

### Reasons given for particular moves

- [stated] One typeface at one size throughout The Shape of Design, hierarchy made with
  negative space and capitals, because e-readers rescale text: "By making all the type
  the same size, and using negative space and capitalization to produce hierarchy, all
  the book's text retains its hierarchy, regardless of the user's settings."
  (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] Quadraat for the book, chosen for "bookish air, legibility on screens, quality
  hinting, and availability for webfont use". (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] A book cover designed to read at icon size in reading apps; the starburst
  became the brand for the merchandise. Line-etching illustration because it looked best
  on a Kindle's e-ink screen. (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] Navigation always visible on phones, no hamburger, because the site has few
  items: "there's like seven things here and let's just put seven words up on the screen
  for you to tap on". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] A side column for navigation on his desktop site, because it has many pages
  and he did not want hidden navigation; he admits it looks "very, very reminiscent of
  90's frames". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] His own site stays plain so the type, colour and structure carry it: "just to
  really let the type and the color choices and the structure of the website do the work
  for me." Typefaces: Fakt and Arnhem, falling back to San Francisco and Georgia; he
  weighs dropping web fonts because each asset costs something, and kept them. (RWD,
  2017-03-13)
- [stated] Flat colours and simple gradients, "because they're lightweight, easy to draw
  with CSS, and can easily scale for areas of unknown proportions." (the-webs-grain,
  2015-02-18; repeated in super-hi-foreword, 2017-04-11)
- [stated] Horizontal stripes of content stacked vertically, "because that is how we
  read, and it is easier for most users to scroll vertically than horizontally".
  (super-hi-foreword, 2017-04-11; the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] In the-webs-grain he lists further common web moves with their reasons: text
  as interface, because technology's abstractions are hard to show visually; blurred or
  tinted background photographs, because the crop is unknown across viewports; big type
  over those images, because clients want both and it avoids the text-versus-image
  scaling problem; mosaics, to show many elements. He calls these the web "forcing our
  hands", "not evidence of a visual monoculture". (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18) These are
  his description of the web at large, not a list of his own moves.
- [stated] Text beside an image breaks responsive layouts because the image grows taller
  as it widens while text grows shorter: "this contradiction is unavoidable and
  unsolvable, so the only choice is to recognize it as implicit to the medium".
  (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Motion as function, not decoration: animated scrolling to preserve context,
  stateful toggles, sticky labels, progressive disclosure. "Movement, change, and
  animation are a lot more than ways to delight users: they are a functional method for
  design." (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14)
- [stated] Plain, default-like materials, with attention to detail: "the surest way
  forward is usually a plain approach done with close attention to detail." He adopted
  Klim's Untitled Sans and Untitled Serif as studio staples because they do not have
  "too much flavor". He avoids Helvetica and Times for plainness because of their loaded
  associations: "we must continually invent new, plain materials." (plainness-and-sweetness,
  2017-03-27)
- [stated] He leans on content more than looks: "the more input I have in the content and
  strategy of the project, the less burden I place on the aesthetics." (plainness-and-sweetness,
  2017-03-27)
- [stated] One strong piece of content over implementation: "a great photo, memorable
  illustration, or pitch-perfect sentence does most of the work." (everything-easy,
  2018-02-09)
- [stated] Line spacing can tell a story: "Slowly decreasing or increasing the line
  height of a block of text, for instance, tells a story by suggesting urgency or
  relaxation." Pacing: a splashy spread with few words after quiet typographic ones;
  scroll can stand for time. (SoD 07-stories-and-voids)
- [stated] Large avatars in an activity feed, because a dense layout had lost the
  feeling of people: "I want my big faces back." (SoD 08-frameworks-and-etiquette)
- [stated] Removing every step but the user's goal (the Ace Hotel WiFi page with one
  button), and treating ugly requirements and error states as places for delight.
  (SoD 09-delight-and-accommodation)
- [stated] Textured, hand-made edges on vector illustration, because flat vector "Looks
  computerish… We need to get some hands in to this." Palette changed for warm/cool
  contrast: "you can't put an orange on the train if the whole train is orange."
  Everyday objects as shared ground because "people understand them." (Grain Edit,
  2009-03-12)
- [stated] Simple palette rule from the student advice: "Red, white, black, and gray
  always go together. Negative space. Size contrast. Directional contrast." (advice,
  2010-08-20) No reason is given.
- [stated] For a self-portrait poster he chose suggestion over specificity: one mode of
  design "requires specificity and clarity"; the other "runs on suggestion, excitement,
  and provocation". This poster needed the second, and a face. (work-looks-back,
  2018-06-06)

### What he refuses or dislikes, and why

- [stated] Simplicity and seamlessness as goals: "Design's golden calf is simplicity…
  Simple things are weak… What I truly want is clarity… Shun seamlessness… Make me things
  that are full of seams". (only-openings, 2014-05-14) Also: "Designers mistake
  invisibility for elegance and simplicity for clarity at their peril." (the-cloud-is-heavy,
  2013-03-13) And: "convergence and simplicity make for poor goals. Instead, we must aim
  for clarity." (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Sites that work against the web, "bicycle bear websites"; Apple's Mac Pro page
  is his example of a "fragile, willfully esoteric website that's good for no one".
  (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18) Marketing "whizzbangery" with the usability problems of
  Flash sites. (RWD, 2017-03-13) High-flux, Flash-style immersive pieces are "rarely a
  good idea on the web". (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14)
- [stated] Hiding mobile navigation behind a show/hide panel; fancy animations and
  transitions in his web work. (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Complex toolchains for small sites: "setting up the system and maintaining it
  seemed to be more effort for an experienced person on a small project than doing the
  work without it." His philosophy: "no razzle-dazzle." He objects to illegible source:
  "the legibility of the source is one of the most important properties of the web."
  (everything-easy, 2018-02-09)
- [stated] Choosing flat or skeuomorphic looks because of the screen: "Screens are
  aesthetically neutral… you shouldn't say you made things look a certain way because the
  screen cared one way or the other." (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14)
- [stated] Things made to look special: he quotes Jasper Morrison, "Special things demand
  attention for the wrong reasons", and says most things "must fade into the background".
  (plainness-and-sweetness, 2017-03-27)
- [stated] Minimalism without cause and trying to be cool: "Be wary of minimalism as an
  aesthetic decision without cause." "Stop trying to be cool: it is stifling." (advice,
  2010-08-20)
- [stated] Showmanship, gimmick delight, imitation without purpose, and iterating only on
  existing forms (the "faster horse"). (SoD 01-how-and-why, 02-craft-and-beauty,
  04-form-and-magic, 09-delight-and-accommodation)
- [stated] Coldness passed off as professionalism: "Projects that seem cold or
  excessively composed are more indicative of a lack of understanding than a mark of
  professionalism." (SoD 09-delight-and-accommodation)
- [stated] Design that creates desire rather than meeting it: "my role is frequently to
  generate desire in other people… maybe instead of using it to make people want things,
  I can use the same skills to give them things that satiate those same desires."
  (the-long-hard-stupid-way, 2011-09-13) "We get to close loops of desire rather than
  open new ones." (SoD 10-gifts-and-giving)
- [stated] Dark patterns, attention commodified, lock-in: "We've taken an opportunity for
  connection and distorted it to commodify attention." (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14)
- [stated] Design as only problem-solving: "It's always felt narrow to say that design is
  problem-solving". (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06; also only-openings, 2014-05-14)
- [stated] Mock-ups in colours that cannot be printed: "a designer may be seeking
  approval for an impossibility." (beyond-the-machine-extras, 2025-10-24)
- [stated] Working under the machine with generative AI ("vibe" work), because "the
  model's limits become your own"; he wants to work beside it, as Eno does. He fights
  the "pull of the averaging effect". (beyond-the-machine, 2025-10-20)
- [stated] Leaning on a habit: when "I have some sort of crutch—either a crutch in the way
  that things look, or a crutch in the way that I'm thinking about something—it means
  I'm not actually doing the hard work of understanding the problem". (Format,
  2015-11-25)
- [stated] Purple: "It's hard to make purple work in a design." (advice, 2010-08-20)

### Where his ideas come from

- [stated] Designers he calls heroes, from the 1960s: Saul Bass, Alvin Lustig, Paul Rand,
  the Eameses. (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03) Alan Fletcher and The Art of
  Looking Sideways as his biggest influence; Rand, Bass, Lustig and Flora as stylistic
  influences; Robert Rauschenberg. (Grain Edit, 2009-03-12)
- [stated] Books as visual translation: Richard Hollis's Ways of Seeing (bold Univers so
  text matches the weight of images), McLuhan and Fiore's The Medium is the Massage,
  Jerome Agel's books, The Electric Information Age Book. (designing-in-the-borderlands,
  2014-04-03)
- [stated] Massimo Vignelli: design as a fight for "beauty, balance, consistency, and
  parity". (massimo-and-me, 2014-05-27)
- [stated] Jasper Morrison's Super Normal, Kris Sowersby's Untitled typefaces, Dot Dot
  Dot magazine's normalised ads, K-HOLE's Normcore report. (plainness-and-sweetness,
  2017-03-27)
- [stated] His father's woodworking lesson on grain: "Pay attention to the wood, follow
  the grain". (super-hi-foreword, 2017-04-11)
- [stated] Film and animation as the parents of screen design: Muybridge; "web and
  interaction design are just as much children of filmmaking as they are of graphic
  design." James Burke's Connections, Ted Nelson, Ethan Marcotte's "The Map is not the
  Territory", Adrian Zumbrunnen's interface animations. (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14)
- [stated] David Hockney's photographic joiners as the model for web layout: "an edgeless
  surface of unknown proportions comprised of small, individual, and variable elements".
  (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Ethan Marcotte's responsive web design article; Tim Berners-Lee's early
  essays. (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Italo Calvino's Six Memos, George Eliot, trickster myths. (designing-in-the-borderlands,
  2014-04-03)
- [stated] Improvisation: improv theatre's "Yes, and", jazz, Miles Davis, Vivaldi,
  Shakespeare's sonnets, Picasso's blue period. (rules-of-engagement, 2014-08-28; SoD
  03-improvisation-and-limitations)
- [stated] Lewis Hyde's The Gift, David Chang's "long, hard, stupid way", The West Wing.
  (SoD 10-gifts-and-giving; the-long-hard-stupid-way, 2011-09-13)
- [stated] Robert Irwin, Edward Hopper's Nighthawks, Charles Eames, the Ace Hotel, Liz
  Danzico. (SoD 06-context-and-response, 07-stories-and-voids, 08-frameworks-and-etiquette,
  09-delight-and-accommodation)
- [stated] Joan Tronto's Who Cares? and Mierle Laderman Ukeles's Manifesto for
  Maintenance Art. (who-cares, 2020-02-04)
- [stated] Brian Eno (ambient music, Oblique Strategies), Holly Herndon and Mat Dryhurst,
  Miyazaki's Spirited Away, Simone Weil; John Salvatier's "Reality has a Surprising
  Amount of Detail", Wendell Berry. (beyond-the-machine, 2025-10-20;
  beyond-the-machine-extras, 2025-10-24)
- [stated] Christopher Alexander's street-to-bedroom privacy gradient. (from-the-porch-to-the-street,
  2014-08-26)
- [stated] He says many favourite designers came "from the world of words instead of the
  world of pictures", and "A text editor is a perfectly viable design tool." (advice,
  2010-08-20)

### Working with clients

- [stated] The designer is paid by the client but answers to the audience: "the designer
  is being paid by the client, but is obligated to the audience", acting "as a proxy for
  the audience's needs while arguing for her own creative concerns". (SoD
  05-fiction-and-bridges)
- [stated] In a disagreement he asks the client why his ideas are wrong, or goes their
  way, and tests both sets of assumptions; "almost every single time we are both wrong."
  (Print, 2015-09-24)
- [stated] Responsive work forces the priority conversation early: "immediately you need
  to get into what their priorities are". At NPR he told the sponsorship team "we just
  couldn't take over the full screen on desktop with advertising." His role: "a clarifier
  and specifier". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Responsive projects break down the walls between disciplines: "nobody has a
  clear domain of work any longer". (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] He calls himself "happiest and most useful at the start of things, which has
  made my branding projects a good fit." (Creative Boom, 2019-06-24)
- [stated] He uses a whiteboard of monthly income targets to decide when to refuse work.
  (whiteboard-accounting, 2010-01-15) In 2009: "to say no more frequently and yes more
  strategically." (Grain Edit, 2009-03-12)
- [stated] Current offer: "a design practice for early-stage teams" (about page); "help
  early-stage teams find their form and make their mark" (studiofrank.co). Roles listed:
  Modern Treasury creative director and head of brand 2022–2025, Fictive Kin 2020–2022,
  Abstract co-founder 2014–2016. (about page)
- [reported] His 2021 portfolio info page, in the third person, describes "warm,
  content-focused design" for clients in technology and publishing, across brand,
  digital product and print. (design.frankchimero.com/info, 2021) The page does not
  say who wrote it.

### Phones and screens

- [stated] Start at phone width, single stack, hierarchy first (see Process above).
  (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] All his digital work had been responsive "for at least the last six years".
  (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] The web's default page is fluid and vertical; stacking "is especially handy
  for design methods like mobile first, since narrower screens can't necessarily hold
  designs where elements are beside one another." (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Screens' grain is "flux", "the capacity for change"; designing the in-between
  states "is half of your work as a designer". (what-screens-want, 2013-11-14) The web
  adds "edgelessness". (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18)
- [stated] Old 90s sites still work: "their fonts just need to be a little bit bigger and
  they need to set a max width on their paragraph"; "you can make a perfectly great and
  serviceable website probably with just … 100 to 150 lines of CSS." (RWD, 2017-03-13)

### What he believes about design in general

- [stated] Design as articulation: "This third definition emphasizes clarity, fluency,
  and coherence over the maddening prettiness of my first definition and the reductive
  cohesion of my second." (designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] "Consistent voice is more important than consistent style." (ten-principles,
  2009-02-09) Repeated in 2015: "it's not necessarily about having a ready style. It's
  having a consistent vision". (Format, 2015-11-25) Asked about a "Frank Chimero-ness" he
  calls his tendencies "sprawling": "I relish taking two things that seemingly don't have
  anything to do with each other and discovering a way to bring them together." (Print,
  2015-09-24)
- [stated] Words and images are equal: "Graphic design has just as much to do with words
  as it does with pictures". (advice, 2010-08-20) His Creative Works talk was about "the
  interdependence of images and words". (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Every material has a grain, including the web and software; go with it.
  (the-webs-grain, 2015-02-18; super-hi-foreword, 2017-04-11; RWD, 2017-03-13; problems
  too, designing-in-the-borderlands, 2014-04-03)
- [stated] Plainness: "I am for a design that's like vanilla ice cream: simple and sweet,
  plain without being austere." (plainness-and-sweetness, 2017-03-27)
- [stated] Design must also attract: "design needs to be attractive… beautiful, yes, but
  also attractive as in magnetic… Design can inform, yet that very often needs to begin
  by seducing." (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Design should be admired, not hidden: "Design doesn't need to be showy to prove
  its value, but it shouldn't be invisible, either." (the-cloud-is-heavy, 2013-03-13)
- [stated] Design as gift-giving: "the best work that I do is when I say something I
  really believe to people that I care about." (the-long-hard-stupid-way, 2011-09-13)
  "The best communicators are gift-givers." (advice, 2010-08-20)
- [stated] "design is a practice built upon making things for other people." (SoD
  00-introduction) "The primary purpose of the design is to have it do something
  particular, not be any particular thing." (SoD 06-context-and-response)
- [stated] Delight is the overlap of empathy, surprise and clarity; "All design is
  experience design." (SoD 09-delight-and-accommodation)
- [stated] Problems are chronic, not solved: "Some designers want to shoot the wolves,
  others want to manage the bears." (only-openings, 2014-05-14)
- [stated] Edit hard: "If it isn't spectacular, it gets cut." (ten-principles, 2009)
  "Take things away until you cry." "Start brave and brash: you can always make things
  more conservative". (advice, 2010-08-20) In technology: "You add things until it
  starts sucking, take things away until it stops getting better." Fit is "paramount".
  (making-the-bed, 2011-01-19)
- [stated] Distinctions he keeps, including "Simplicity vs clarity", "Scale vs
  resonance", "Convenience vs comfort". (the-green-ray, 2021-08-31)
- [stated] Outputs come before outcomes: "outputs (code, design, the products themselves)
  are the load-bearing work". (selling-lemons, 2025-09-30)
- [stated] Products need a point of view: "how few products … seem to have a coherent
  point of view"; some work is better navigated "by one's own internal sense of rightness
  rather than by external signals." (beyond-the-machine-extras, 2025-10-24)
- [stated] Care as the frame for design systems: "Tools are always beholden to values."
  (who-cares, 2020-02-04)

### Where he changed his mind

- [stated] Decoration, then construction, then articulation. Decoration (editorial
  illustration for The Atlantic, Time, Newsweek) wore him out: "I found myself slowly
  turning into the design version of a sad clown." Construction (consulting, interface
  work) "often forces a brute simplicity" that "zaps the potential for heart". By April
  2014 he called articulation his "creative homeland". (designing-in-the-borderlands,
  2014-04-03; repeated in Print, 2015-09-24 and Format, 2015-11-25)
- [stated] He took against Vignelli's "fight against the ugliness" at first, then came
  round: "My grudge left when I inverted Vignelli's words." (massimo-and-me, 2014-05-27)
- [stated] Print, 2015: "all the work featured in that issue [Print New Visual Artist,
  2010] is work I don't do anymore." (Print, 2015-09-24)
- [stated] After three years at Abstract (2014–2016) he returned to web client work in
  2017 and rejected the new toolchains he found. (everything-easy, 2018-02-09)
- [stated] In 2018 his process changed to "a commitment to staying open to new ideas for
  a longer period of time." (work-looks-back, 2018-06-06)
- [stated] Around 2017 he would not again write a book in the same "very writerly" tone;
  early drafts alternated stories and theory, and he moved to integrating them. (On
  Margins 002)
- [stated] Web fonts, 2017: "I kind of waffle on it, I go back and forth probably every
  single day". (RWD, 2017-03-13)
- [stated] Teaching in 2008–2009 made him "a little less dogmatic". (Grain Edit,
  2009-03-12)
- [stated] Of his professor's "Needs more love": he first took it as a note on craft and
  later saw it as something more fundamental. (SoD 02-craft-and-beauty; undated beyond
  the book's 2012 publication)
- [stated] On technology in 2025: "I used to like technology… the machine is not enough."
  (beyond-the-machine, 2025-10-20)
