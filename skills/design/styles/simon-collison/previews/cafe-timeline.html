The Brightside Café

Tea and opinions on Station Road, Carrow Hill, since 1931.

Edie Marsh opened this café with a kettle and a loan from her brother. Four of her tables are still here, and on most mornings so is somebody who knew her.

The long story, 1931 to now

Ninety-odd years of breakfasts on Station Road.

  1. 1931

    Edie unlocks the door on Station Road at half past six; there is a kettle and a loaf; there is a sign her brother painted with the accent the wrong way round.

  2. 1940

    Rationing comes in. Toast is scraped thin and the soldiers waiting for the 6.10 to Derby pay in cigarettes when the coins run out.

  3. Rebuilt.

    1944

    A bomb meant for the railway sidings takes the back kitchen instead. Edie serves from the front window, which she calls the hatch, until the new kitchen opens in 1945.

  4. 1950

    Her son Len comes home from the Navy and learns to fry an egg without breaking it, mostly.

  1. 1956

    A jukebox arrives from a Leicester arcade on the back of a coal lorry. Its most-played record opens meet me where the steam is, Carol1, which it gets through twice before the urn has boiled.

  2. 1963

    The 41 bus starts stopping outside, which settles where Carrow Hill eats breakfast from then on.

  3. 1971

    Len’s daughter Pat asks why the brown sauce goes into the same glass bottles every morning. She is handed a tea towel, which is the answer to most questions here.

  4. Sold.

    1979

    Len sells to the Kowalczyk family, who keep the name. Len keeps his seat by the radiator and comes in every morning anyway.

  1. 1984

    The radio on the shelf stays tuned to the strike news. The café runs a tab for the pit families, and the Welfare choir’s single, which opens kettle never cold2, goes on the jukebox.

  2. 1996

    Marta Kowalczyk puts pierogi on the board for Thursdays. Within a year Thursday is busier than Saturday.

  3. 2008

    The jukebox gives up on the line all the songs we paid for3. A man from Ilkeston mends it and will take only a bacon cob.

  1. Back.

    2021

    After a long shut winter the door opens on a wet Monday in April. Forty-one people queue in the rain, the same number as the bus.

  2. Now

    A card machine now sits by the till, which Edie would have glared at.

Jukebox notes

  1. “Meet Me Where the Steam Is”, The Carrow Hill Five, a Tamworth label, 1955. Played roughly forty thousand times in this room. ↩︎
  2. “Kettle Never Cold”, the Welfare Hall Choir, a single pressed for the strike fund, 1984. Pat still has six copies. ↩︎
  3. “All the Songs We Paid For”, The Brightsiders, a local band of three regulars and a cousin, 1966. It never had a B-side. ↩︎
