The Secretary’s Report

read at the 283rd Annual Meeting, and corrected afterwards by the Recorder of Moths

The Society met eleven times indoors and nineteen times out of doors this year, and lost only one member on Mousehold Heath, who was found again by the café at half past four. Membership stands at 214, the highest since 1911, when the Society first admitted women and the waiting list for the Thursday microscope evenings ran to a second page.

The moth trap in the Secretary’s garden off Unthank Road caught a Clifden Nonpareil on the night of 3 September, the first in the city since 1937.1 The Cathedral peregrines raised four young for the second year running, and were watched from the Close by more people than attended the Michaelmas talk on liverworts. The Society holds no view on this.

We are grateful to the Castle Museum for the loan of the weavers’ cabinet for the winter meetings, and to the Assembly House for not charging us for the extra chairs. Thanks are also due to everyone who sent in records, including the gentleman who sent in a heron every week from the same lamp post on Riverside Road.2

Fixtures for the Winter

field meetings start on time; indoor meetings start when the urn has boiled

  1. 5sat dec

    Winter gulls at Whitlingham Broad. Meet by the flint barn, 9 o’clock. Leader: Roy Gotts. Telescopes useful.

  2. 8tue dec

    “Lichens of the Rosary Cemetery”, a talk with lantern slides. Assembly House, 7.30. Mrs Thurlow, who has counted 61 species on the Colman family plot.

  3. 2sat jan

    New Year plant hunt along the Wensum, Carrow Road to Bishop Bridge. Anything in flower counts, including the gorse by the football ground.

  4. 12tue jan

    The 284th Annual Meeting, and the reading of these Transactions. Members only; tea from 7.

  5. 7sun feb

    Mosses of Mousehold Heath. Meet at the top of Gurney Road, 10 o’clock. Hand lenses lent to anyone who asks.

The Society Since 1743

a short history, longer than the Society intended

1743Twelve silk weavers meet at the Adam & Eve to compare moths and the price of thread.
1788The first printed list of Norwich moths: 112 species, three of them wrong.
1825The Society moves to a room in Colegate and buys its first microscope.
1911Women admitted as members. The Thursday microscope evening fills in a week.
1940Meetings held by post. Members send in 1,200 records from air-raid shelters.
1952The teapot.
2011Peregrines nest on the Cathedral; the Society lends a telescope to the Close.
2026Volume 283 of the Transactions, and 214 members.

Joining the Society

£12 a year; £4 for students and anyone under eighteen

Members receive the Transactions each January, a place on every field meeting, the loan of a hand lens, and a vote at the Annual Meeting on matters such as the gull list. No knowledge is needed. About a third of our members joined because a neighbour found an odd beetle and asked us what it was.

Write to the Secretary with the form opposite, or come to any indoor meeting and pay at the door. We will post you a membership card and a list of what is flowering in the city that month.
