# Yugo Nakamura: work for clients and products

Stream `work`. Raw copies are in `/tmp/designers/yugo-nakamura/raw/work/`, one file per
source, URL on the first line; `_descriptions.txt` and `_credits-summary.txt` collect the
tha.jp project texts and credits. Claim tags: **[stated]** Nakamura or tha said it (tha.jp
project texts count), **[observed: seen]** looked at in Chrome (live page or published
still), **[observed: code]** from markup only, **[reported]** someone else said it.

Limits, 2026-11-23. Almost all of the Flash-era web work (2003–2010) no longer runs, and
tha.jp documents it with embedded YouTube videos only. Chakrit ruled out YouTube, the
Wayback Machine and other workarounds partway through, so most early pieces below rest on
tha's one-paragraph descriptions and credits, not on looking. The UNIQLOCK hub view was
made before the ruling.

Authorship matters at tha: several famous "tha" pieces were directed by colleagues, not
Nakamura. MUJI rhythm, playMUJI, MUJI Motion, MUJI HOME MADE, FFFFOUND!, NEOVISION and
EVE credit Yosuke Abe as art director; LIFE credits Jun Yoshihara; UNIQLO SPRZ NY credits
Hideki Owa. **[stated, tha.jp credits]** They are excluded from his moves below.
UNIQLOCK (2007) is not on tha.jp's works list at all.

## 1. Moves

### Move: the brand mark as a tile, then a game board

- **What.** Treat a square logo as one cell and build the experience by tiling, scaling,
  rotating and recolouring copies of it. UNIQLO GRID puts every visitor on the same grid
  moving logos in real time; the screenshot shows UNIQLO's katakana and Latin marks in many
  sizes, all snapped to a fine graph-paper grid, in red, magenta, purple, teal, grey and
  black. **[observed: seen; stated, tha.jp/49]** The same idea runs from the 2006 SoHo
  teaser ("interactive motion graphic of the UNIQLO logo") **[stated]** to the 2021–2025
  Logo Play façade animations, where the square mark repeats across screens and turns as
  a lit cube. **[observed: seen, stills]**
- **Default it replaces.** A logo placed once in the header.
- **When it fits.** A brand with a strong geometric mark; let the mark become the unit of
  the grid and the content.

### Move: data drawn as type

- **What.** amaztype arranges book covers returned by an Amazon search into the shape of
  the search word's letters; FONTPARK lets people rebuild Morisawa glyph pieces into
  pictures; ecotonoha turns messages into leaves. **[stated, tha.jp texts]** FONTPARK's
  still shows a single black-on-white portrait built from letter parts, with only a tiny
  all-caps nav line at the top and the wordmark bottom-left. **[observed: seen]**
- **Default it replaces.** Results as a list, words as captions.
- **When it fits.** When the content is many small items, let their arrangement spell or
  draw the subject.

### Move: an outside rhythm drives the motion

- **What.** Motion follows something real: the wind in your city (Kaze to Desktop), the
  minute (DropClock, UNIQLOCK, LIFE COLOR CLOCK, the station and tower clocks), the music's
  beat (UT LOOP!, with an audio engine built so cuts land exactly on the beat), the shake of
  a phone (UTme!). **[stated, tha.jp texts]** `talks.md` has his own account of motion
  as the visible result of a force.
- **Default it replaces.** Easing and scroll animations that exist for decoration.
- **When it fits.** Any interface motion: name the force or clock that moves it first.

### Move: everyone on one surface

- **What.** Shared, live, single-canvas participation: GRID's one global board, ecotonoha's
  one tree, UNIQLOOKS ranking by likes on the front page, MUJI and Design Ah! walls showing
  posted photos and drawings. **[stated, tha.jp texts]**
- **When it fits.** Campaign sites where the crowd's activity is the spectacle.

### Move: the giant wordmark band (UNIQLOCK hub)

- **What.** The 2007 UNIQLOCK hub page was one very wide horizontal page: a full-bleed red
  band with "UNIQLOCK" in white condensed caps filling its height, then "MUSIC. DANCE.
  CLOCK." stacked three lines high, repeating sideways; below, red condensed caps on white
  ("MUSIC. FANTASTIC PLASTIC MACHINE / DANCE. WHO WILL? / CLOCK. GET FREE."), a long red
  arrow rule, and "6.15 START" knocked out of a red-striped bar. The embedded HTML declares
  an 8400-pixel-wide layout. **[observed: seen and code, before the ruling]**
- **Caveat.** UNIQLOCK's credit split is not on tha.jp; whether this page's typography is
  Nakamura's is unverified.

## 2. Process and thinking

- tha's texts describe each piece by its mechanism in one or two sentences: what the user
  does, what the system does in response, and what brand idea that hints at (GRID: sharing
  one moment worldwide "loosely hinting" at UNIQLO's global spread). **[stated]**
- Technical invention is part of the design brief, not support for it (UT LOOP!'s
  binary-level audio synthesis engine to keep the beat while swapping video). **[stated]**
- Nakamura usually takes art direction and design, with a technical director beside him
  (Keita Kitamura on most 2003–2011 pieces). **[stated, credits]**

## 3. Taste

- Flat, saturated colour on white, or footage on black; few typefaces; caps for labels.
  **[observed: seen]**
- Clocks and time-keeping as a recurring subject across 20 years. **[stated, works list]**
- Long collaborations: Kashiwa Sato (UNIQLO, Sato's site 2007 and 2016), Naoto Fukasawa
  (INFOBAR), Taku Sato and Cornelius (Design Ah!). **[stated, credits]**
