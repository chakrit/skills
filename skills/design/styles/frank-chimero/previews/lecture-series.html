Slack Water, a public lecture series in Red Hook, Brooklyn

Eight Thursdays this winter, at the moment the tide in Buttermilk Channel stops turning, somebody stands up in the old sail loft on Conover Street and talks for an hour about something slow.

Free, no tickets, 90 chairs. The first lecture is Thursday 14 January, high water slack at 6:10 pm.

Each drawing is that evening’s tide in Buttermilk Channel from 3 to 11 pm, from the NOAA prediction for Red Hook. The dot is where we start. Slack comes about 23 minutes later each week, so the lectures do too.

The doors open half an hour before slack, and the kettle is on by then.

Visiting

Where
The sail loft, top floor of 41 Conover Street, Red Hook, Brooklyn 11231. Ring the bell marked Loft; the stairs are steep and there is a lift at the back.
Getting here
The B61 stops at Van Brunt and Coffey, two blocks away. The NYC Ferry stops at the Atlantic Basin, an eight-minute walk along the water.
Seats
Ninety chairs, first come. If the room fills we open the windows and leave the door to the stairs open so people on the landing can hear.
Afterwards
The speaker stays for questions until the tide is running hard again, roughly forty minutes.
Recordings
We record the sound only, and post it a week later under the series.

The series

Slack Water started in 2019 when the loft’s owner, a rigger named Joe Sasso, let a neighbour give a talk about eels on a Thursday when the room was empty. Forty people came. We have run a winter season every year since, except 2021, when the talks went out on the radio from WGBK instead.

The speakers are people who know one slow thing very well. Some are scientists; more are trades people, keepers of records, and neighbours. Nobody is paid and nobody pays. The loft gives the room, Red Hook Library lends the chairs, and the coffee comes from whoever is closest to the kettle.

If you would like to give a lecture next winter, write to loft@slackwater.nyc with the slow thing you know about. We read every letter and answer by October.

Last slack of the season: Thursday 4 March, 8:52 pm

After that the river carries on without us until January. One letter a month, with the recordings and next winter’s tide table when it is ready.
