## 1. Moves (as he teaches them)

### 1.1 Articulate every decision in a visual grammar

- **What:** web designers should replace "because it feels right" with reasons they can
  explain. He lists a vocabulary: line and point, form and shape, formal and informal
  structure, balance and symmetry, pattern, texture, repetition and rhythm (vary repetition
  by colour, shape or direction), paths and movement, colour, white space as "the pause",
  depth, affordance, juxtaposition, economy and reduction, and grids studied beyond a
  borrowed framework. [stated, 24 ways 2010; DIBI 2010]
- **On motion:** ask what force would cause a movement, and respect its path. He calls much
  CSS3 animation clumsy. [stated, 24 ways 2010]
- **Reading list he gives:** Norman, Leborg's *Visual Grammar*, McCloud's *Understanding
  Comics*, Crow's *Visible Signs*, *Universal Principles of Design*. [stated]

### 1.2 Motive: nostalgia, rhetoric, vernacular, humour

- In every talk from 2010 to 2012 he gives "motive" as what drives direction and
  persuasion: nostalgia, rhetoric, vernacular and humour, plus play, personality,
  self-expression, and "courage and conviction" to commit to a bold direction. [stated,
  Design To Communicate, Subconscious Design, Pursuit of Magic, Elegance Without
  Compromise]
- His own work demonstrates it: the Edwardian v4 site (nostalgia, humour), the Rushmore
  album-sticker parodies (vernacular), New Adventures copy (humour). See `own.md` and
  `work.md`.

### 1.3 Restraint and imposed constraint

- "Having complete freedom is possibly the worst way to start any project." Make sense of
  the brief's constraints and add your own. [stated, Restraint 2012; Embracing
  constraint 2012]
- Know when to stop and throw things out. "Don't underestimate humans. They can fill in
  the blanks." Restraint is economy that gives immediate meaning. [stated]
- Restraint is **not** default white space: he questions the idea that minimal designs are
  automatically "clean". [stated, Restraint]
- Avoid "unnecessary fakery, over-texturing, skeuomorphism". Make interactive elements
  clearly identifiable. Do not disguise calls to action. [stated, Restraint]

### 1.4 Simplicity is a feeling, not a style

- "less can very often lead to, well… less." Complexity can be "beautiful, elegant,
  necessary". The enemy is *perceived* complexity. [stated, Taming Complexity 2011]
- Techniques: release power in planned phases; divide content into buckets, then
  recombine; use smart defaults; use graphical timelines and colour keys as reference
  points; squint or blur at a layout to judge its overall weight. [stated]

### 1.5 Systems, not pages, then break the rules

- He repeats Andy Clarke's "We don't design web pages. We design systems." from 2010 to
  2015. Understand the system, then "break rules creatively". [stated, Notes From The
  Edge; Old Dog, New Tricks]
- Modular content goes into asset containers at fixed ratios (1:1, 2:1, 1:2, 4:3, 16:9),
  with pattern libraries and a shared palette held in Sass variables. [stated, Old Dog
  2014]
- "The frame": the outer limits of a design, where all its elements work together.
  [stated, Notes From The Edge]
- Typography is in the designer's domain. He quotes Mark Boulton: HTML is markup, i.e.
  typesetting. [stated, Being Essential 2015]

### 1.6 Truth to materials on screen

- His FOWD 2009 talk "Bauhaus Ideology and the Future of Web Design" argued for honesty of
  construction: designers should not pretend a coffee cup has been rested on the screen.
  [stated, as summarised in the 2010 redesign post; the deck itself was not found]
- Influences he names: Modernism, the Bauhaus, the Swiss style. [stated, 24 ways 2010]

## 2. Process and thinking

- Step away from the browser to sharpen your reasoning. [stated, DIBI 2010] Yet for New
  Adventures he found half the design in the browser (see `work.md`). Both hold: reason
  away from the screen, refine on it.
- "Find problems and design responses", not final answers. [stated, Designing the
  Designer; Being Essential]
- Do creative inquiry outside the field and bring the findings back. He studied fine art
  and ran two Nottingham art festivals. [stated, Designing the Designer; Heart Internet]
- Tools serve ideas. He quotes Reichenstein that the tool does not make the craftsman.
  [stated]
- Sit with finished work before launch, run audits, and remove what isn't needed.
  [stated, Restraint]
- He quotes Milton Glaser, "I move things around until they look right", as an honest
  description of arranging. [stated, Being Essential]
- "Necessary naivety": experimenting without knowing the rules pushes the medium.
  [stated, Elegance Without Compromise]
- Delight: he cites Frank Chimero on delight as a small lesson in seeing the world as good.
  [stated, A More Meaningful Web]

## 3. Taste

- Wary of trends ("more HTML5 and even more crazy CSS3"). He wants a precise vocabulary
  for patterns unique to the web. [stated, Heart Internet 2011]
- From 2017 on he turned to nature: biophilia, open-ended exploration over tasks, avoiding
  guilt and addiction, calm and invisible computing (Mark Weiser), and a tracked osprey as
  the story. [stated, IoNT decks 2018]
- He returns to people more than products: craft for its own sake, protecting time away
  from work, a John Muir quote. [stated, Being Essential]
