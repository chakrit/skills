## Moves

### 1. A neutral frame so the members' work is the only colour

Dribbble's interface was kept deliberately plain: "monochrome, Helvetica type, simple
glyphs", with the stated priority "to feature the work and the designer" [stated, TNW
2013]. "We intentionally made the interface generic", and holding back his own interface
instincts "was always a challenge" [stated, WTTJ]. The one spot colour is pink, pointing
back to the logo, on a grey-and-black palette [reported, TNW 2013].

- Replaces: chrome that competes with content (branded gradients, decorated cards).
- Fits: any page whose job is to show other people's images; the designer's personality
  goes into the logo and a single accent, not into the frame.

### 2. Small, fixed-size previews as a deliberate constraint

Dribbble shots have a pixel limit so people can show part of a piece without revealing
all of it, and so each one is "quickly and easily digestible" [stated, WDD 2011]. The
format came from Cameron Moll's "Screen Grab Confab" posts and Twitter's brevity
[stated, WDD 2011].

- Replaces: full-bleed case-study images of any size.
- Fits: grids of many items where rhythm matters more than any one item. (The exact
  400×300 size is from memory, not from these sources.)

### 3. Progressive enrichment: a sound base, then rewarding details

Talks at FOWD and AEA in 2009 under "Progressive Enrichment with CSS3": add RGBa colour,
subtle drop shadows, styled form controls, hover scaling and rotation, and let older
browsers skip them [reported, swissmiss 2009]. Earlier, the MIT seminar's stated goal
was to "let go of pixel precision" and plan for varying text size and content [reported,
MIT 2006]. In the transitions chapter he puts motion in the "experience layer", "not
integral to the brand, readability, or layout", with a short ~0.3 s example (a link
background going from light to darker green, the same on focus as on hover) [stated,
ALA 2010].

- Replaces: identical pixel-perfect rendering, or no detail at all.
- Restraint is part of the move: "One can easily go overboard adding transitions to
  everything on the page", an "annoying, pulsating monster"; motion must not slow "an
  otherwise snappy action" [stated, ALA 2010].
- Fits: the details layer of any page: soft shadows, translucency, small hover lifts,
  never the structure.

### 4. Texture and decorative edges around a plain white reading column

His 2004 SimpleBits layout as he describes it: light grey page, a tiled birch-wood
texture, a striped decorative edge on the left, a wide white content column, a 1 px rule,
a light-brown side column, and the striped edge mirrored on the right [stated, ALA Faux
Columns 2004]. Boxes with softened corners built from tiny two-colour images, with the
note that the same trick can make other silhouettes, such as a mountain ridge [stated,
ALA Mountaintop 2004].

- Replaces: flat, untextured page backgrounds and hard-edged boxes.
- Fits: personal or small-studio sites wanting warmth; keep the reading column clean and
  put the texture in the margins and edges.

### 5. Start from a real-world object, then simplify it

His favourite shot, "Electric Service Co.", began as an iPhone photo of a manhole cover
on his walk to work [stated, Timeout 2014]. The Dribbble script wordmark took the Kleenex
logo as "the biggest inspiration" and blended two script faces (Handsome Pro Bold, League
Night) [stated, Tales from the Script 2014]. What he says he loves: "real-life
inspiration, a great typeface, warm colors, and simple forms", plus texture from grunge
brushes, and "you can't go wrong with a lightning bolt" [stated, Timeout 2014].

- Replaces: abstract geometric marks and stock iconography.
- Fits: logos, badges, merch-style illustration, header art: flat shapes, warm limited
  palette, light texture, a found vernacular source (utility hardware, packaging).

### 6. Refine the mark instead of redesigning it

For the Dribbble wordmark he smoothed jagged joins between letters, evened line weight
and baseline, and opened the counters so it holds up small: "refining as opposed to
redesigning" lets "the old and new ... easily coexist" [stated, 2014]. The same instinct
for the product: "subtle, consistent tweaks", "iterating internally and deploying
slowly" [stated, 2019].

- Replaces: periodic rebrands and big-bang redesigns.
- Fits: any existing identity; fix the details that have bugged you, keep recognition.

### 7. Build pages from small named patterns ("slats")

In "Handcrafted Patterns" (AEA, early 2012) a "slat" is a repeated teaser block:
thumbnail, title, description, optional date or tags, linking through [reported,
circlecube]. Break the page into patterns and "solve small problems" with big impact;
keep your own pattern library (Pears) rather than adopting a framework, since
"Frameworks are great tools, but they are learning tools" [reported, circlecube]. For
Dribbble's responsive layout, one breakpoint at 500 px, chosen by content not devices
[reported, circlecube].

- Replaces: framework components used as-is; one breakpoint per popular device.
- Fits: list and index pages; design one strong slat and repeat it.

### 8. Personality and humour in the made thing

Merch should show personality; "It's not just like, let's stick our logo on a mug"
[stated, Podcast Awesome 2025]. His test for a project: "Is this making me laugh a little
bit or amusing me?" [stated, 2025]. Font names are misspellings so each is "its own
thing" [stated, 2025]. His self-published book aimed to look "almost like this midcentury
zine": cloth hardcover, two-colour offset printing, a bookmark, and his own doodles
despite being "not much of an illustrator" [stated, Overtime 2020].

- Replaces: neutral, safe corporate tone in small-brand work.
- Fits: personal brands, shops, side projects; limited-colour print aesthetics.

## Process and thinking

- **Strip it first.** View the unstyled page, "like taking an X-Ray of the document", then
  question each element and pare it down before styling [stated, WSG 2004]. Complex
  design and perfectly semantic markup pull against each other [stated, 2004].
- **Imitation, repetition, innovation.** Learn by studying the greats' source, repeat
  patterns until second nature, then reinterpret and make it your own; he framed this
  through learning clawhammer banjo [reported, circlecube 2012]. He learned the web the
  same way: "The blueprints ... are available for all to see and dissect" [stated, WTTJ].
- **Pave the cowpaths.** Dribbble features (rebounds, playoffs, teams) came from watching
  what members already did [stated, TNW 2013; WDD 2011; 2019].
- **Design is not decoration.** Design is "not an afterthought of 'now make it pretty'"
  [stated, TNW 2013]; "design is not just about the visuals", and a designer synthesises
  what the user asks for [stated, Overtime 2020]. Typography, layout and the marketing
  are all part of the toolbox [stated, 2020].
- **Constraint as working method.** Vector work in Photoshop's "smaller toolset":
  "Constraints and all that" [stated, Timeout 2014]. One typeface at a time with the next
  idea waiting [stated, 2025]. A single 15-inch laptop, no external screen [stated, 2014].
- **Commit, then let go.** A few letters into a typeface he commits to finishing; it is
  "never done", so at some point "this is enough" [stated, 2025]. "Embrace the
  imperfection of your code" [stated, WTTJ]; values "the imperfectness" in type [stated,
  2025].
- **Type process.** Sketches, gathered inspiration, iteration on letterforms, then Glyphs
  [reported, Dribbble workshop 2020]; Glyphs "changed my life in a way" [stated, 2025].
- **Make for yourself first.** "if someone else likes it, that's bonus" [stated, 2020];
  "it's OK to create things just for the sake of creating something" [stated, 2014].
- **Hand-set code as craft.** Reluctant to adopt Sass because generated output styles
  "none of them matched my own" [stated, net mag ~2013]; later called that resistance
  stubbornness [stated, 2019].

## Taste

- **Likes:** warm colours, simple forms, a great typeface, texture, custom lettering
  [stated, 2014]; Curtis Jinkins, "a master of texture and simple shapes and custom
  lettering" [stated, 2014]; Dana Tanamachi's chalk lettering [stated, 2011]; mid-century
  zines and two-colour print [stated, 2020]; lightning bolts [stated, 2014].
- **Formative sources:** packaging and visual identity, the Muppets, breakdancing and
  bubble letters, band covers, skateboard graphics [stated, TGD 2012; 2025].
- **Mentors and influences:** David Searson's rule to "stay as simple as possible";
  Jeffrey Zeldman; the Wired.com CSS redesign [stated, TGD 2012; WSG 2004]; Cameron Moll's
  screenshot posts [stated, 2011]; Coudal Partners' love of experiments [stated, 2025;
  name garbled in the auto-transcript].
- **Specific over general:** people "really passionate about these very specific things"
  [stated, 2025]; "AI can't have taste hopefully right now"; "taste is something that's
  unique to everyone" [stated, 2025].
- **Dislikes:** chasing trends ("use the style that fits the problem and stick with it")
  [stated, 2019]; overused motion [stated, 2010]; generic logo-on-a-mug merch [stated,
  2025]; bloated markup [stated, 2004]; the claim that Dribbble is a "glorified online art
  gallery" [stated, 2011].
- **Self-assessment:** "I am not a great logo designer" [stated, 2014]; "not much of an
  illustrator" [stated, 2020]. The modesty sits next to a strong hand for badges and
  lettering; the work streams should check it against the work.
