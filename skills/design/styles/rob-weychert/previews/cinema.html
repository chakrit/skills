Series

  • Philadelphia on film5 films, 6 screenings

    Five films shot in this city, shown to people who live in it. Audiences here laugh in different places, mostly at the geography. Bring someone from out of town to Rocky and watch them work out the running route.

  • Elaine May, director4 films, 7 screenings

    All four features she directed. Three come from archives on 35mm; The Heartbreak Kid is a digital restoration, because the last print we were offered had turned pink. Ishtar plays once, on a Friday, and we would like you to come.

  • Saturday matinees3 films, 4 screenings

    16mm prints from our own shelf, at two o’clock. Children under twelve get in for $4 and may talk during the short that runs before each feature.

  • One print, three nights1 film, 3 screenings

    Playtime on 70mm, a borrowed archive print that took us two years to book. It arrives in nine cans and leaves on the 22nd.

The box office opens 45 minutes before the first show and takes cards and cash. Seats are not numbered. Members may book the front row of the balcony, which is the best seat in the building and is twenty-two seats long.

Visit

1400 Spring Garden Street, at 14th, Philadelphia 19123. The Broad Street Line stops at Spring Garden station, on the corner. The 43 bus stops at the door.

Step-free entry is on 14th Street, with six wheelchair places in the back row of the stalls. Assisted-listening headsets are at the box office; ask for one when you buy your ticket.

The booth holds two 35mm projectors, one 70mm projector that arrived on a flatbed in 1968 and has not left, a 16mm machine older than anyone on the staff, and a digital server that needs restarting more often than all of them together.
