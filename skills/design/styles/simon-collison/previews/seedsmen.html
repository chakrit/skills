The firm has sold seed from the same shop on Sheep Market1 since 1887. We keep about two hundred old varieties of vegetable and save most of the seed ourselves on four acres behind the railway. It goes out by post in paper packets with the sowing notes printed on the back.

Some of these varieties were ordinary in our great-grandmother’s day and have since dropped out of every other list in England. We grow them because they still crop, and because somebody keeps writing to ask for them.

A Short History of the Firm

The shop has had five generations of Pasks and one sign.

Edwin Pask opened the shop in 1887 with a sack of broad beans and a loan from his brother-in-law.3 His daughter Hannah took it over in 1919 and added “& Daughter” to the sign, which is the only change the sign has had.

The present Pasks are her great-grandchildren, Ruth and Tom. Ruth keeps the seed and walks the four acres every morning in the same coat. Tom keeps the books, and the ledger of every packet sent since 1952, now in eleven volumes on the shelf above the till.
