Two films a night, on film

One ticket buys the double bill. Nearly every print is 35 mm, run from the booth upstairs by Ines and Walt.

The Orpheum opened on Calder Street in 1927 as a vaudeville house with a screen, and it has shown films in the same 412-seat room ever since. We program by the week. Each night pairs two pictures that have something to say to each other, and you can stay for both on one ticket or leave at the interval.

We still run 35 mm on two Century projectors with a changeover, and keep a 70 mm head for the few prints that ask for it. When a film exists only as a digital restoration we say so in the listing. Seats are unreserved; the doors open forty minutes before the first show.

This season: Rooms

Through 20 December every double bill is set mostly inside four walls: an office floor after hours, a courtyard seen from one window, a dinner party nobody can leave, a hotel shut for the winter. Lena Okafor, who books the Orpheum, chose the pairs after watching Rope and The Exterminating Angel back to back and finding that the second picks up almost where the first stops.

Three evenings in the season come with someone at the microphone before the first film:

  • 2 December, the set designer Hal Breuer on building the Playtime office maze at full scale.
  • 9 December, a reading of Cornell Woolrich's story It Had to Be Murder before Rear Window.
  • 16 December, Ines on why The Shining print weighs 41 kilos and how she threads it.

Members

Membership is $90 a year. It gets you $6 double bills instead of $12, a guest on Tuesdays, and the printed calendar by post on the first of each month. Members also vote, twice a year, on one double bill of their own choosing; the autumn vote put Repulsion and The Shining together.

  • Join at the box office or online; the card is posted within a week.
  • Students and anyone over 65 pay $8 a night without a membership.
