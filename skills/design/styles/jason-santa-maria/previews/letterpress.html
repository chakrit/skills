Every job here is set by hand from cases of lead and wooden letters, locked up in an iron chase and printed one sheet at a time on a 1961 Vandercook proof press. The type bites into soft cotton paper and leaves a shallow, even dent you can read with your thumb.

Ines Abernathy has run the shop since 1998, first above the hardware store and, since 2011, at the street level of number 14, where the press sits in the window. She prints wedding invitations, business cards, letterheads, menus and the occasional small book, and on three evenings a week she teaches anyone who wants to learn to do the same.

Most of our type was rescued from a newspaper in Leith when it closed in 1987. Some of it is older than the building.

The November broadside◆No. 11 of 2026 Damson ink on Somerset cream

“Somebody’s name goes into the forme backwards.”

Every month Ines prints one short piece of writing by someone who lives within a mile of the shop, set in a single face and printed in a single ink chosen for it. This month it is a poem by Ruth Okafor, who teaches at the primary school on Garnet Row and came to her first workshop in March.

The poem is about setting a friend’s wedding invitation, letter by letter, upside down and right to left, and only seeing the name the right way round when the first sheet comes off the press.
