Sunrise in Brunswick, 21 June

We find beauty not in the thing itself but in the pattern of shadows, the light and the darkness, that one thing against another creates. Jun’ichirō Tanizaki, In Praise of Shadows, 1933

A job starts on the shortest day of the year, even when that day is months away. We measure your lot, your fences and your neighbours’ walls, and draw the shadows they cast from sunrise to sunset on 21 June, on tracing paper at 1:100.

That drawing decides where the new room goes. In most Brunswick, Coburg and Northcote terraces the yard faces the lane, and the winter sun comes over the back fence low and from the north. Put a room in its way and the yard stays cold from May to August.

We charge a fixed $1,900 for the shadow study and a first sketch. You keep both whether or not we go on to build.

Ruth Ng and Hugh Weir run the practice from an upstairs room at 214 Sydney Road, above the shoe repairer. Ruth trained at RMIT and spent eight years on public housing with the state architect’s office. Hugh was a bricklayer before he was an architect, and still lays the sample panels himself.

We take on four or five houses a year, and we are on site every week while one is being built. Most of our clients live within three kilometres of the studio, and we would rather keep it that way.

If you send us your address, we will look at the lot from the lane before we reply.
