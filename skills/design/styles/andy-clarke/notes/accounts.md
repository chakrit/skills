# Andy Clarke: accounts

Stream `accounts`: what other people report about Andy Clarke, about working with him
and about his work. All pages were read on 2026-11-24. "Undated" means the page shows no
date.

Every client quote below was found on Stuff & Nonsense's own portfolio and about pages,
where Clarke chose and published it. No client account written and published by the
client was found (see "Looked for and not found"). The other accounts are reviews of
his books, a publisher's descriptions of them, and podcast show notes.

Marks: **[reported]** means someone other than the designer said it. Nothing in this file
is marked "stated by the designer" or "observed in the work": those belong to the `words`
and `work` streams.

## Findings

### Process: what he does first, what he shows, how a design changes

- **[reported]** He takes time over the brief and brings ideas the client had not
  thought of: "They take the time to fully understand the brief and regularly present
  creative ideas and perspectives that we hadn't considered." Paul Bradley, Portico
  Investment, who also says the studio "designed multiple websites for our business".
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/portfolio-portico-investment/ (undated)
- **[reported]** He guided branding as well as the website: "He didn't just build us a
  website; he expertly guided our branding and design". Adi Berkovits, University
  College London. https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/portfolio-shared-homeland/ (undated)
- **[reported]** Count Binface says Clarke showed ideas first and was then asked to
  build them: "after seeing his ideas, I commanded him to turn them into what humans
  call a 'website'" (written in the client's comic persona).
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/portfolio-count-binface/ (undated; 2024 campaign)
- **[reported]** Jas Dosanjh reacted to the design while it was in progress: "I'm so
  glad that you're having fun. It looks amazing ... I couldn't believe that my sad
  little story could look so pretty!" Clarke introduces the message by writing that she
  "was closely involved as the website developed".
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/portfolio-jas-dosanjh/ (undated)
- **[reported]** A publisher describes his process for *Hardboiled Web Design* as
  establishing "a design atmosphere", developing "a design style guide", creating "type
  proofs", and using "brand personality interviews".
  https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/11/no-hard-feelings-the-waiting-for-hardboiled-web-design-is-over/
  (2015-11)
- **[reported]** Reviewers of *Transcending CSS* (2006) report that it covers
  wireframing and "quick HTML prototyping" (Snook) and a "content-out" approach, in
  which offline objects are marked up as practice for ordered markup (Moll).
  https://snook.ca/archives/snooks_book_nook/review_transcending_css (2006-12-16);
  https://www.cameronmoll.com/archives/2006/12/transcending_css/ (2006-12-28)
- **[reported]** A Goodreads reader describes the book's method as "boiling the content
  down to their cores of lists, paragraphs, and headings; and then working out in style
  sheets how to make the content look like the designer wants".
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/258800 (review undated)

### What he refuses or dislikes (as others report it)

- **[reported]** Readers of *Hardboiled Web Design* report that he holds a site need not
  look the same in every browser: "a site doesn't need to look the same in every
  browser, and indeed we shouldn't even try ... design for the best browsers first ...
  then provide alternate experiences for less capable browsers."
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9537411 (review undated)
- **[reported]** An Italian Goodreads reader says the first 100 pages of that book
  repeat "ignore old browsers and concentrate on new ones" until it becomes heavy
  (paraphrased from Italian). https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9537411 (review
  undated)
- **[reported]** A Transcending CSS reader disagreed with "the argument that we really
  need to design for cutting edge browsers and not worry about how the design translates
  to older browsers". https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/258800 (review undated)

### Where his ideas come from (as others report it)

- **[reported]** The *Art Direction for the Web* launch text says he studies "the work
  of some of the most influential art directors, luminaries like Alexey Brodovitch, Bea
  Feitler, and Neville Brody", and does not ask readers "to merely mimic work from a
  previous era and medium, but to understand their thinking".
  https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2019/03/art-direction-release/ (2019-03)
- **[reported]** Snook reports the *Transcending CSS* chapter on inspiration sends
  readers "outside (literally) of the online world", "from architecture to a fabulous
  discussion on grid design to mood boards".
  https://snook.ca/archives/snooks_book_nook/review_transcending_css (2006-12-16)
- **[reported]** A Goodreads reader of *Art Direction for the Web* lists the book's
  principles as "balance and symmetry, flow, scale, ratio (including the golden ratio
  and Fibonacci)", then grids, type and pictures. Another says the book reports that
  "The best results have been produced when art direction and editorial have worked as
  one team rather than as two departments."
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44596660 (reviews undated)
- **[reported]** A Transcending CSS reader complained of "far too much name dropping"
  and examples drawn "from within a narrow spectrum of the web design community".
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/258800 (review undated)

### Phones

- **[reported]** Mike Worth: "super light and very mobile friendly."
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/portfolio-mike-worth/ (undated)
- **[reported]** A workshop attendee: "As a developer, I would also like to see how some
  of these designs translate to mobile." Clarke replies on the page that he "didn't
  cover small-screen designs" in that workshop.
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/blog/what-i-learned-from-hosting-last-weeks-practical-layout-workshop/
  (2025-09-28)
- **[reported]** A Goodreads reader of *Art Direction for the Web* says the book shows
  "how a design can be done a different screen size like mobile, tablet and desktop",
  with "some parts of design ... responsive while some parts ... adaptive".
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44596660 (review undated)
- **[reported]** A reader of *Art Direction for the Web* found "Many of the designs (in
  the art direction section) were questionable in terms of real-world usability and
  appearance across multiple platforms". https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44596660
  (review undated)
- **[reported]** Kosmaczewski, reviewing *Transcending CSS* in 2023, notes the book came
  out the year of the iPhone and before "Responsive Web Design" was coined in 2010; he
  does not report what the book says about phones. A Goodreads reader reports the book
  argues it is easier "to write a new style sheet for Internet-enabled phones and PDAs"
  than a separate page. https://deprogrammaticaipsum.com/andy-clarke/ (2023-01-02);
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/258800 (review undated)

### What he believes about design in general (as others report it)

- **[reported]** Dave Shea calls *Transcending CSS* "about design and code playing
  together nicely. It's the way code is meant to support design considerations."
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/transcending-css-revisited/foreword.html (Oct 2006)
- **[reported]** Rachel Andrew summarises the book's call as "to fully embrace the
  possibilities of the web, rather than being frustrated by its limitations".
  https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/transcending-css-revisited/foreword.html (Oct 2019)
- **[reported]** Trent Walton: the book "inspires each of us to be more purposeful about
  the choices we make" and "follows up all that inspiration with demos and the practical
  knowledge". https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2019/03/art-direction-release/ (2019-03)
- **[reported]** A reader summarises *Art Direction for the Web* as urging "that
  designing for the web need not be boring / static due to technology limitations".
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44596660 (review undated)
- **[reported]** Critical readings of *Art Direction for the Web*: examples are "Nearly
  all ... centered around blog post layouts", it "feels more like a print design manual
  awkwardly applied to the web", and it has "no dedicated section on color"; another
  says it "misses guidance on how to bring the ideas presented into larger projects
  where handcrafting every page isn't an option."
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44596660 (reviews undated)

### Where he changed his mind

- **[reported]** Rachel Andrew's 2019 foreword reports that in the revisited edition
  Clarke "explains in his sidenotes how the landscape has changed", with Grid Layout
  making the 2006 designs "more straightforward and robust", while "The underlying
  principles ... still hold true." This reports changed techniques, not a changed
  design view. https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/transcending-css-revisited/foreword.html
  (Oct 2019)
- No other account reports a change of mind.
