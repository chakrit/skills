## Findings

### Process

- His illustration process puts the work into the concept first. "All the effort is
  front-loaded" toward finding a good concept, which he compares to a joke: two unrelated
  things put together that spark a thought or delight. **[stated, quoted by PRINT]**
  https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- "Everything I do starts as something to amuse myself." **[stated, quoted by PRINT]**
  https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- He redesigns his own site often and in public. Ethan Marcotte: "you seem to redesign it
  every six months … It's really interesting to see how you reinvent yourself every
  year." **[reported]** https://responsivewebdesign.com/podcast/frank-chimero/
  (2017-03-13). Daniel Eden names him with Ethan Marcotte as designers who "quite
  publicly went through their own flavours of dismantling and patiently reassembling
  their websites". **[reported]** https://daneden.me/blog/2021/redesign (2021-04-29)
- The 2019–2020 redesign was written up as it happened, with "a few sections dedicated to
  type"; a forum poster valued seeing "the decisions being made, comparisons, and how
  someone articulates all that". **[reported]**
  https://typedrawers.com/discussion/3500/choosing-typefaces-for-redesign-behind-the-scenes
  (2020-02)
- Ethan Marcotte reports that web fonts and performance are "a very early aesthetic
  question" for him, from their private conversations. **[reported]**
  https://responsivewebdesign.com/podcast/frank-chimero/ (2017-03-13)

### Reasons given for particular moves

- Illustration palette of two or three pastel colours: PRINT says he "picked up" the
  preference "while screen-printing posters in college". **[reported]**
  https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- No other source in this stream gives Chimero's reason for a specific visual move.

### What the work shows, as others describe it

- Illustration (Un Sedicesimo, Edizioni Corraini): "flat backgrounds; two or three pastel
  colors …; geometric vector illustrations humanized by hand-tracing the outlines and
  rescanning". PRINT calls this "classic Chimero". **[observed by PRINT]**
  https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- Typefaces identified in his work by Fonts In Use contributors **[observed by Fonts In
  Use]**:
  - *The Shape of Design* book (2012): Quadraat. https://fontsinuse.com/uses/1327/the-shape-of-design-book
  - *The Shape of Design* website and online edition (2012): Quadraat, Quadraat Sans,
    Trade Gothic. https://fontsinuse.com/uses/1716/the-shape-of-design-website-and-online-editio
  - Portland State University "Show & Tell" poster (2010): History, Heroic Condensed, FF
    Tisa; tagged "chromatic". https://fontsinuse.com/uses/1617/portland-state-university-show-and-tell-serie
  - *What Screens Want* site (2013): Tiempos Text for the text and Lato for "large
    interstitial type" (from the site's colophon, quoted on the entry); a commenter adds
    Calibre "in one or two of the graphics". Tagged "animated type", "flat design",
    "skeuomorph", "navigation". https://fontsinuse.com/uses/5760/what-screens-want-webpage
  - *The Great Discontent* issue one (2014): Maison Neue, A2 Antwerp, Leitura News;
    tagged "pull quotes". https://fontsinuse.com/uses/7163/the-great-discontent-issue-one
- Readers' reactions to *What Screens Want*: "the amount of work that went into it"; "A
  beautiful site and a great read." **[reported]**
  https://fontsinuse.com/uses/5760/what-screens-want-webpage (2014-01-15)
- His responsive sites, per Ethan Marcotte: "they don't just fit on different sized
  screens, they really kind of feel at home"; his work "settle[s] in nicely with most
  devices I look at it on". **[reported]**
  https://responsivewebdesign.com/podcast/frank-chimero/ (2017-03-13)
- His 2020 redesigned site, as one marketing blog describes it: "three boxes on the
  homepage, and a simple column running down the middle of his post archive – no boxes
  or complicated layout structure". **[observed by Yaguara]**
  https://www.yaguara.co/blog/designs-of-the-times-04-layouts/ (2021)

### What he refuses or dislikes

- "I love ideas more than prettiness." As a teenager he designed MP3-software skins and,
  at 17, a Windows Media Player interface for Microsoft; "Luckily, I've lost all records
  of it." **[stated, quoted by PRINT]** https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- Yearly-renewal web font licences: he called such a deal "a no-go for ideological
  reasons" and said "I'm uninterested in paying a web tax". **[stated, quoted on
  TypeDrawers]**
  https://typedrawers.com/discussion/3500/choosing-typefaces-for-redesign-behind-the-scenes
  (2020-02)
- Sites that show off technique: Bramus quotes his bear-on-a-bicycle passage ("it's
  really not what a website is supposed to do") and his line that media queries might be
  "points of reassembly" rather than breakpoints. **[stated, quoted by Bramus]**
  https://www.bram.us/2015/03/04/the-webs-grain/ (2015-03-04)

### Where his ideas come from

- His teachers at Missouri State University "had worked under Communism, so they were
  masters of concept and symbolism … I learned how to empathize with the audience and
  design conceptually." **[stated, quoted by PRINT]** https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890
  (2010)
- New York magazine's Approval Matrix: he made a personal version with axes "serious and
  fun, nourishing and empty" and uses it to decide whether something is worth his time.
  **[reported, with his quote]** https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- Reviewers of *The Shape of Design* name the book's references: Yosa Buson's candle
  haiku and Marshall McLuhan (Popova, **[reported]**
  https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/04/23/the-shape-of-design-frank-chimero/
  2012-04-23); the Shaker, Eames plywood and Gehry Wiggle chairs (Brown, **[reported]**
  https://aworkinglibrary.com/reading/shape-of-design 2013-01-10).

### Clients, collaborators, and phones

- Client lists given by others: The New York Times and Wired editorial illustration
  (PRINT 2010); New York Times, Nike, Microsoft (Format 2015); Microsoft, Starbucks,
  Adobe, Facebook (Creative Boom 2019); a "top-secret project for NPR" (Millman 2015).
  **[reported]** URLs above. None of these sources describe what he made for these
  clients or how the work went.
- *The Great Discontent* print edition: he designed it with Tina and Ryan Essmaker
  **[reported]** (Millman,
  https://www.printmag.com/design-matters-with-debbie-millman/design-matters-in-print-frank-chimero/
  2015-09-24; Creative Boom, https://www.creativeboom.com/work/frank-chimero/
  2019-06-24). The studio statement quoted on Fonts In Use, credited to "Another" with
  creative direction by Ryan Essmaker, says the magazine's "bold photography and
  typography take a nod from the website" and that the site would later be "reimagined
  in light of the magazine—a true multi-format design system". **[reported]**
  https://fontsinuse.com/uses/7163/the-great-discontent-issue-one (2014)
- Abstract: Creative Boom reports he "helped co-found" it, "a software company focused on
  supporting a modern, collaborative, and open design process". **[reported]**
  https://www.creativeboom.com/work/frank-chimero/ (2019-06-24)
- Editing *The Shape of Design*: Mandy Brown, its editor, writes that "credit for its art
  goes entirely to Chimero". **[reported]**
  https://aworkinglibrary.com/reading/shape-of-design
- Phones: the only third-party account is Marcotte's remark above that his sites feel at
  home on different screens.

### What he believes about design in general, as others summarise it

- Mandy Brown: "His characterization of design as a response—rather than a problem to be
  solved—changed the way I think about my own work." **[reported]**
  https://aworkinglibrary.com/reading/shape-of-design
- PRINT: his philosophy is that "design should be easy to absorb and 'nourishing.'"
  **[reported]** https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890 (2010)
- Format: he "believes that design is what happens on the borderlands", quoting "art
  versus commerce, material requirements versus creative idealism, screens versus print,
  audience needs versus client desires". **[reported]**
  https://www.format.com/magazine/features/design/frank-chimero (2015-11-25)
- Ethan Marcotte reads *What Screens Want* and *The Web's Grain* as framing "what a web
  native aesthetic might be", and quotes his About page phrase "making modest things
  consistently well". **[reported]**
  https://responsivewebdesign.com/podcast/frank-chimero/ (2017-03-13)
- Responses to *Everything Easy is Hard Again* (2018) report his complaint that web
  skills keep going stale. Chris Coyier and Robin Rendle attribute the complexity to
  rising demands on websites. A commenter on Michael Tsai's roundup, "remmah", sees
  "two decades of progress" where Chimero sees cycles. **[reported]**
  https://css-tricks.com/?p=266794, https://mjtsai.com/blog/2018/02/16/everything-easy-is-hard-again
- Bill Mill on *The Web's Grain*: "I don't agree with all of it, and some hasn't aged
  perfectly". **[reported]** https://notes.billmill.org/link_blog/2024/05/The_Web_s_Grain.html
  (2024-05-20)

### Where he changed his mind

- PRINT reports a shift from surface to concept: "prettiness" was fine when he was a
  teenager; by 2010 he valued "the why". **[reported]** https://www.printmag.com/?p=5890
  (2010)
- Format, in his words: design "started out simply as decoration … developed into a
  manner of construction … Now, I think of design as a process of articulation."
  **[stated, quoted by Format]**
  https://www.format.com/magazine/features/design/frank-chimero (2015-11-25)
