## 1. Moves others noticed

### 1.1 Making a default face look deliberate (v4, 2010)

- Richard Rutter replied to the v4 launch by quoting Bringhurst: when only Times is
  available, make the typography "richly and superbly ordinary", so attention goes to the
  composition. Collison was "humbled" to be compared with Bringhurst. [reported; stated]
- Several commenters (Anthony Casey, Steve Rydz, Travis Neilson, David Trang) said they had
  avoided Times and were now reconsidering it. Yaili praised the Times-then-Cambria stack.
  [reported]

### 1.2 Grid discipline under the ornament

- Jonathan Snook said the header type "rests naturally on grid lines" and that "everything
  sits nicely on a grid". He thought the visible `#hashgrid` overlay a gimmick, and noted
  that the background grid image was a few pixels off. Collison agreed on both points.
  [reported; stated] Andi and others confirmed the offset. [reported]
- Aaron Gustafson chose the site for his .net magazine media-queries feature "due to its
  rigid grid structure". [stated by Collison, reporting Gustafson's reasoning] Zeldman
  showed it as an early responsive retrofit. [reported]

### 1.3 Restraint with personality

- Veerle Pieters: the "unique feeling" came from "minimal colors and typography". Erwin
  Heiser: "It takes guts to strip back and remove unnecessary ornamentation." Colin
  contrasted its restraint with the gratuitous CSS3 shadows of the time. Kelly Packer
  called it "enduring" and "timeless", and Collison said that was exactly the aim.
  [reported; stated]
- Erik Wallace preferred the final graph-paper header to the earlier worn treatment:
  "more scholarly". [reported]

### 1.4 Voice in the interface

- Ryan Taylor: because the animals do not match their sections, readers have to read the
  descriptions, and that is where the personality lives ("Ignoring old acquaintances:
  Bloody Facebook"). Stewart McCoy called the navigation labels and header/footer copy
  "delightfully literate". Emma Boulton called him "a true Edwardian Gent". [reported]
- Aimée, a non-designer, singled out the footnotes, which take you to the note and back.
  [reported]

### 1.5 Hand-drawn simplicity (Erskine Design, 2007)

- Unmatched Style called the studio site "remarkably simple". The details carry it:
  pencil drawings, a bird illustration and pencil-line borders in a three-column layout. A
  commenter noted a more monochrome palette than before. [reported] This matches what was
  seen in the 2007 capture (see `work.md`). [observed]

### 1.6 Curation as design (New Adventures)

- Trent Walton: the organisers "put love into every component", including choosing and
  ordering the speakers' topics. Ian Thomas: "cerebral" talks about ideas over tools, a
  single room, and a printed conference newspaper with Collison's editorial asking
  attendees to "work a bit harder". Laura Kalbag: a premium feel on a modest budget.
  Andy Clarke: he "poured his heart and soul" into it. [reported]
- Chris Allwood, quoted by Collison: you leave a day of talks with "clear, powerful and
  resounding themes". [reported]

### 1.7 Client and peer verdicts he quotes

- Sam Thorne (Frieze): an "attractively minimal design" that drew comparisons with the
  New Yorker, the Guardian and Wired. [reported]
- Naz Hamid: "Super thoughtful, broad surfaces in his work, and multi-disciplinary."
  [reported]
- Frank Chimero praised the Internet of Natural Things research. [reported]
- Jonathan Snook on *Beginning CSS*: humorous writing, and it walks through the thought
  process of designing with CSS. Snook made it his default recommendation for beginners.
  [reported]

## 2. Criticisms

- The v4 type was "eyeachingly small on a humble laptop" (Rachel). It scaled poorly on
  small displays (Odne, February 2010, before the responsive retrofit). It used px rather
  than flexible units (Nick Toye). The navigation broke when text was resized, which
  Collison fixed within two days. [reported; stated]
- Collison wrote that the redesign was "singled-out and dragged into a spiteful
  post/thread" elsewhere. That thread was not found. [stated]
- Snook's book review: slow first chapters; advanced topics skimmed. [reported]

## 3. Taste, as others see it

- People see his work as detail-obsessed, warm, British and personal rather than trendy.
  "Attention to detail" recurs in about a dozen v4 comments. [reported]
- His peer circle is the 2000s British web-standards scene (Andy Clarke, Mark Boulton,
  Richard Rutter, Jon Hicks, Jeremy Keith) and the Brooklyn Studiomates/Fictive Kin crowd.
  [reported; stated]
