Paddle out at dusk and come home by lantern light

Small-group sea kayaking from the slipway at Porth Skerrow

Every boat carries a lantern on its stern. On a still summer night the plankton lights up green-gold round your paddle, and our guides know the bay well enough to take you out in the dark and bring you back. Day trips run all year; night paddles run from May to September. How a night paddle goes

Three trips from the slipway

Six paddlers to one guide, never more. You don't need to have kayaked before for the first two; we teach you on the beach before we launch.

How a night paddle goes

Times are for a mid-July evening. We move the start with the sunset, and we cancel if the wind is over fifteen knots or the sea is lumpy at the harbour wall. You get your money back or another date, whichever you prefer.

  1. 20:30

    Meet at the boathouse

    Wetsuits, spray decks and buoyancy aids are hanging up in your size. Bring a warm layer and a hat.

  2. 20:50

    Twenty minutes on the beach

    How to hold the paddle, how to turn, and what to do if you go in. Then everyone lights their lantern.

  3. 21:10

    Out to the point

    A slow paddle along the harbour wall and out to Trwyn Du while the sky goes orange, then purple, then dark.

  4. 21:50

    Lanterns down

    We raft the boats together and turn the lanterns off. Trail a hand in the water and watch it light up.

  5. 22:40

    Back to the slipway

    Follow your guide's lantern home. Hot chocolate in the boathouse, and the showers are warm.

Guides who grew up on this bay

Nia Pritchard started Lanternfish in 2014 with four boats and a shed. She had worked as a lifeboat crew member for nine years and wanted to show people the bay the way she saw it on night shouts: dark, quiet, and full of light underneath. The boathouse is still the shed, a bit bigger.

All our guides hold British Canoeing Sea Kayak Leader awards and current first aid. Each night trip has a second guide in a safety boat once the group is over four, and the coastguard at Milford gets our float plan before we launch.

Questions people ask

Will the water definitely glow?

No one can promise that. The plankton is there most nights from late June to early September and is brightest when there's no moon. On the nights it's faint, the stars and the dark bay are still worth the trip, and if it doesn't show at all you can come back on another night for half price.

I've never been in a kayak. Is that all right?

Yes, for the glow paddle and the seal caves. Our boats are stable sit-on-tops for beginners, and the beach session covers what you need. The Skerry crossing is the one trip that needs experience.

How old do you have to be?

Eight and over, with an adult in the same double kayak until twelve. Over-sixteens can paddle on their own.

What if I fall in?

You'll float in your buoyancy aid, your guide will be with you within a minute, and we practise getting back into the boat on the beach first. It happens on about one trip in twenty, usually to someone laughing.

Where do we park?

The harbour car park at Porth Skerrow, SA62 3XX. It's free after 6pm, and the boathouse is the blue shed at the far end.
