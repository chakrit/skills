# Bans

Patterns that mark a design as AI-made. They hold in every style.

A ban is broken by the pattern, whatever the markup. Replacing a banned pattern with its
nearest sibling (listed under each ban) breaks the same ban.

- **Eyebrows everywhere.** No small tracked uppercase label above section headings as a
  section stamp. Siblings: a hairline or dash before the label, a numbered kicker
  (`01 —`), a pill badge above the headline. A heading carries its own section.
- **Emphasis on words inside a headline.** No word or phrase inside a headline set
  apart by italic, colour, weight, size, underline, highlight or a drawn mark. Siblings:
  a slanted, hand-drawn or brush underline, a highlighter swipe, a circled word, gradient
  text on one word. A headline is one voice; contrast comes from its size, placement and
  the lines around it. Stacked display lockups may give each whole line its own weight.
- **The italic punchline.** No copy built as a setup followed by a short emphasised
  payoff, such as "Ship it today, because *you can*." Siblings: the same payoff as a bold
  fragment, after an em dash, or as its own one-line paragraph. Say the thing plainly.
- **Cards.** No content set in boxes: a fill, border, radius or shadow around an icon or
  number, a title and a blurb, repeated in a grid. Siblings: the same boxes as a bento
  mosaic, as frosted glass panels, or as a list whose every row is a box. Let the
  content itself be the block: a ruled list, a spec sheet, a contact sheet.
- **Writing tells.** No copy in a design may use any tell in the de-slop skill's
  `references/copy-tells.md`; read it in full before writing copy. That includes the
  contrastive frame: "It's not X, it's Y", "Unlike traditional X…", "The difference?
  …", "It's not about A. It's about B." State the thing directly.
