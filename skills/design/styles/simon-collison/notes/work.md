**[observed]** seen in Chrome; **[reported]** someone else says it. Raw files:

## 1. Moves

### 1.1 A tiny identity kit that makes everything

- **What:** for New Adventures 2019 he wrote that the system "consisted of a grid, two
  typefaces (Aperçu Medium for display, Source Sans for body), two colours (blue, and
  pink), one grey, almost black, and white". From it came "multi-line patterns, solid
  blocks and playful tints for unifying images". [stated]
- **Seen:** the Albert Hall photo split into three duotone panels (cyan / pink / cyan).
  Speaker names are right-aligned, large and blue, each on a pink hairline. Beside them
  sits a run of pink vertical lines as pure pattern, and the last names are reversed out
  of a solid blue block. [observed, Wayback 2019]
- **Variation:** 2020 "branches off with five critical colours for the year", but "the
  system still carries most of the work". [stated] On the live hub, 2020 is a pattern of
  topic words in clashing colour blocks. [observed]
- **Same move for Citipo:** he extended the identity into "flexible reusable shapes and
  tiling patterns" so it could serve slides, flyers and posters. [stated]
- **Default it replaces:** a big brand guideline, or a gradient-and-stock-photo event
  site.

### 1.2 Duotone and tint to unify unruly photography

- New Adventures used single-colour treatments of venue photos: red in 2012, cyan/pink in
  2019, speaker videos in purple. They make disparate photos one family. [observed, live
  hub] He names "playful tints for unifying images". [stated]

### 1.3 Kicker / title / italic descriptor stacks

- **What:** a repeated three-part label. A small italic grey line sits above a caps title
  (colly v4: "Bottled for your pleasure / POTTED AUTOBIOGRAPHY"), or a big condensed caps
  name sits above an italic serif descriptor (NA 2012: "DAN MALL / *Art Director at Big
  Spaceship*"; NA 2013: "*Designer, type nerd, em dash abuser*"). Frieze uses tiny grey
  caps kickers over serif heads, with italic bylines. [observed]
- **Why it works:** the italic line carries the wit, and the caps carry the order.

### 1.4 Hand-made marks with typographic confidence

- **What:** the Erskine Design brand pairs a handwritten "erskine" in a thought bubble and
  a pencil bird with very large, confident type ("hello."). Torn-paper edges, pencil
  rules and a grey brush stroke add the hand. [observed] He stated that the logotype stays
  discreet except on the homepage, that the bird motif recurs, and that the opening
  "Hello" was chosen deliberately for a friendly tone. [stated, ALA 2009] Unmatched Style
  credited the "pencil drawings… pencil-line borders" with doing the work. [reported]
- **Echo:** both later personal sites also open with "Hello." [observed]

### 1.5 Vernacular and parody as reward

- **What:** Rushmore's reward "stickers" riff on classic album covers. He also notes that
  they "did, of course, do Unknown Pleasures". The stickers were printed for "analogue
  situations… as a mini-grid", and there were slipmats. He wanted to "explore the
  vernacular of fanship and old school record stores". [stated]
- **When it fits:** community products where the culture already has a visual language to
  borrow.

### 1.6 Content first, print and web in tandem

- **What:** at Frieze, "Content always came first". He "requested online highlights feature
  in the printed magazine's contents page and article footnotes". [stated] New Adventures
  had a printed newspaper (a 28-page Guardian print in 2012) and a magazine. Newsletters
  should "feel more like letters… on headed paper". Downloads "use formal print grids to
  support a dense delivery of information". [stated; observed 2012 page]

### 1.7 Reduction and breathing room in commercial UI

- **What:** at Samsung he "worked through a process of reduction and fought to have
  anything extraneous or confusing either removed or rethought… ensured everything could
  breathe a little more". He called the system "flexible but stern". [stated]
- At TeuxDeux, "much of the redesign rests on… hairline dividers start[ing] and stop[ping]
  in the right places, and adjusting font sizes". [stated]
- **Caveat he states:** reduction is not a style. In Brazil it was "irresponsible to throw
  a set of standard UX principles at it". [stated]

### 1.8 Ideation by provocation

- **What:** for TeuxDeux he asked, "What if it was an Apple app? What if we made it less
  Swiss? What if the text was huge?". He produced "hundreds of iterations in Sketch using
  my subconscious". He ended up "mainly realigning" the app, but deconstructed every
  detail on the way. [stated]

## 2. Process and thinking

- **Split of the work:** for New Adventures, "around 10% of the design with pencil and paper,
  another 40% with Sketch, and the remaining 50% I found in the browser". He says CSS is
  "one of the very best design exploration tools", and he likes "throwing the components
  around, squinting at the screen a lot". [stated]
- **Early R&D is unpressured:** "Sketch, think, go for walks. Sketch some more", pushing at
  an idea and "finding the edges". He then builds simple, mostly static navigable
  prototypes. Features survive "a succession of simplification exercises". "Spongy stuff
  calcified into explainable logic". [stated, Rushmore]
- **Audits:** regular design audits, "blunt but never unnecessarily negative". They always
  offer solutions, and patterns spotted in audits later become the system. [stated,
  Rushmore, TeuxDeux]
- **Studio process (Erskine 2009):** finalise the copy before visual design, then sketch,
  then wireframe with deliberately simple boxes, then prototype in the CMS. A physical
  wall of magazines and cut-outs serves as the scrapbook. [stated, ALA via talks stream]
- **Frieze:** collaborative sketch reviews, high-level wireframes, and championing one
  direction that "excited the entire client team". [stated]
- **Positioning:** a "background in cultural studies and critical thinking". Research is
  at the core, without subscribing "too rigidly to any methodology". [stated, /work]

## 3. Taste

- Art-world and music clients and subjects: Frieze, The Libertines / Dirty Pretty Things,
  record labels, Beatport, the Rushmore music startup. [stated]
- Witty, regional copy in event design: "The Rolling Stones rocked it in 1964, and now it's
  our turn"; "Mr. Binns' organ". [observed, NA 2011]
- Recurring motifs: birds (Erskine), journeys and dashed routes (NA 2011, 2013), and
  paper: kraft card, torn edges, memos, stickers. [observed]
- Inclusivity is part of the design work: NA name badges had sticker slots for pronouns
  and photo/conversation preferences. [stated]
