## 1. Moves

### A small set of spatial parameters instead of templates

His main ProPublica contribution was an article layout framework: text sits in the
centre eight columns of a 14-column grid (6 of 8, 4 of 6, all 4 on smaller grids), and
any figure is described by size (1 to 14 columns, or full bleed), position (left,
right, centre; pushed in or pulled out up to three columns), text wrap, and responsive
overrides such as full width or an alternate crop on small screens **[stated]**
(/propublica-article-layout-framework/). That makes 616 presentations from four
variables, all chosen in the CMS without code **[stated]**. He argues layout is "the
most invisible and least understood" part of editorial design, and that size and
position "can be just as communicative as their content": a small, off-centre image
in wide white space tells a different story than a large establishing shot **[stated]**.
He reused the framework on V7's project pages **[stated]**.

- Default replaced: full-width image, text, full-width image.
- When it fits: long reading pages where images need to change pace.

### Bend one convention to carry the story

In "An Unbelievable Story of Rape" the single text column shifts right for the Colorado
sections and left for Washington, then the threads converge at the end; the headline
splits left and right from a centre axis **[stated]** (talk text, `own`). In Lost
Mothers the gallery shows 800 slots with only 134 filled, so the gap in the data is the
point; the grid later collapses into a bar at the top that shows where you are in the
year **[stated]**; the black page of grey dots with a band of faces is as described
**[observed, seen]**. Both are plain HTML underneath: the grid is a bullet list
**[stated]**. His rule: don't let information look like what it is "supposed" to look
like **[stated]**, after Eric Meyer showing a table styled as a bar chart.

### Listings designed for scanning, with no single focal point

Redesigning concert email listings, he lists what a reader scans for (image, date, day,
genre, price, venue) and gives each its own fixed place and style: square images in a
left column, headliners large and bold, a calendar-like date block in its own right
column, genre as thin stripes that double as separators, price as a button, venue in
small caps **[stated]** (/designing-better-concert-listings/). He accepts that this has
"no obvious focal point", because readers isolate the pattern they care about
**[stated]**. He rejects centred text lists because each line starts in a new place
**[stated]**.

### Direct the hand-made from references, in words

For Robtober 2020 he gave letterer Jen Mussari seven reference logos with a note on
each (the size contrast and rising baseline of *The Hunger*, the "bludgeons more than it
stabs" bluntness of *Friday the 13th*, the dry-brush texture of *Black Fury*), then
judged rounds against a single target, "slasher" rather than "ghost story" **[stated]**
(/jen-mussari-lettering-robtober/). He accepted some genre cliché because the piece
"was meant to participate in a fairly specific tradition" **[stated]**. The final mark
moved from digital sketches to real ink washes for unpredictability **[stated]**.

### Each year, a genre costume, built from CSS

Robtober posts borrow a period of horror marketing and build it natively **[stated]**
(/projects/robtober/). 2021: a VCR blue screen with ROBTOBER 2021, SLP and 0:00:00 in
three corners as separate SVGs so it fits any aspect ratio, each pixel row an animated
path for scan lines, then VHS boxes in CSS 3D with spines in a colour eyedropped from
each poster and held to at least 75% luminance, which made them pastel and ’80s by
accident **[stated]**; in the browser it is a blue screen, then posters on a TV-static
background with blackletter Fruktur titles, small Poppins capitals and the box turned
in 3-D **[observed, seen]**. 2022: a ransom note whose eight letters come from eight
horror posters from eight decades, re-stacked vertically by an aspect-ratio media query
on tall screens **[stated]**. Motion and scroll snap sit behind
`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` **[stated]**.

### Title sequences from a rule set

All Day Hey! 2023 opens by showing its grid, then builds every screen on it, and every
transition is a tiling of eight ways a square can enter or leave a square; a fake
filmstrip dust effect keeps it from going stiff **[stated]**. Beyond Tellerrand 2024
draws speaker names as connect-the-dots letters on graph paper, filmed at an angle;
the letterforms are becoming a typeface **[stated, seen]**. Beyond Tellerrand 2022
started from drum music he wrote, which pointed to Reid Miles's Blue Note covers and
Saul Bass's *Vertigo*: vivid figures and white type on black; in the frame seen, pink
diagonal bands cross a blue halftone disc on black **[stated, seen]**. He animates with
Python, DrawBot and After Effects, and wrote a Sass function so CSS keyframes can be set
in frames rather than percentages **[stated]**.

### Enumerate every case, then show all of them

Incomplete Open Cubes Revisited numbers a cube's 12 edges, generates every combination,
discards 20,481 duplicates and keeps 4,094, then fixes drawing order with a fixed stack
and alternate parts **[stated]**. Plus Equals does this per issue: define a system, find
every permutation, explain the thinking, show all results **[stated]**. Issue 4 arranges
three blocks in a 3×3×3 isometric cube (54 arrangements), then turns the stacking-order
"problem" into a second layer of permutations, calling isometric projection "a beautiful
lie" **[stated]**. On screen each issue opens with a full-bleed white-line drawing of
its number made in that issue's system, then a serif essay with italic initial,
small-caps lead-in and numbered figure references, beside a gallery panel of coded
pieces (e.g. PE04-A01B04C01) **[observed, seen]**. The index is a black page of square
white-framed covers in date order **[observed, seen]**.

### Mission first on a homepage, then the structure

The Markup homepage opens with one sentence of purpose, with inline links to the
pillars, then a section per pillar (investigations, blueprints, impact, tools), each with
a short description and up to four stories, and related links that cross pillars
**[stated]**. In the 2023 capture each section has a left label column in red with a
"see more" link, stories to the right, dotted rules between sections **[observed, seen]**.
He got there by having stakeholders rank goals from a reader survey before mockups
**[stated]**.

## 2. Process and thinking

- **Purpose and inventory first.** The Markup began with survey data and ranked goals
  **[stated]**; Robtober and Tinnitus Tracker began with lists of what the data holds
  **[stated]**.
- **Sketch on paper, then a system, then code.** Cube variations were drawn by hand and
  in Illustrator until a formula showed, then moved to DrawBot **[stated]**. The grid
  partitions were planned on paper because they "can't be designed in the browser"
  **[stated]**.
- **Constraints chosen up front.** Zaly9 used only League of Moveable Type fonts
  **[stated]**; Once in a Lifetime targeted one 16:9 screen **[stated]**.
- **Music and film as starting points.** A title sequence began with a drum track
  **[stated]**; he grew up wanting to be an animator **[stated]** (about page).
- **Write the system so non-designers can use it.** The ProPublica framework had to be
  "comprehensible to users without a design degree or coding skills" **[stated]**.

## 3. Taste

- Midcentury graphic design (Reid Miles, Saul Bass), conceptual art (Sol LeWitt),
  isometric drawing (Chris Ware, Lego, Escher), post-punk austerity, horror posters,
  VHS, zines and gig handbills **[stated]**.
- Strong limited palettes: black and white for PhilaMOCA and Plus Equals; black with
  vivid figures for Beyond Tellerrand **[stated, seen]**.
- Clarity for clients, play for himself: client pages stay restrained; personal pages
  are themed and animated **[observed, seen]**. He says he likes "making big things for
  small audiences" **[stated]**.
