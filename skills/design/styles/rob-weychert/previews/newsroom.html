Of the 412 drinking fountains Calder put in its parks and streets, 97 can still be found

A parks ledger kept from 1958 to 2004 lists every fountain by number, year and street. We spent four months looking for each one. Most are gone, and for nearly all of them the work orders that recorded the removal have already been thrown out.

By Maren Oduya and Tom HalloranNovember 23, 2026Water

The ledger is a green clothbound book of 212 pages, kept in a cupboard on the second floor of the parks depot on Mill Lane. Each fountain has one line: its number, the year it went in, the street or park, and the initials of the foreman who signed it off. The last entry, No. 412, is dated 2004 and says only “Garrow St, library”. There is no fountain outside the library now.

In July the Ledger asked the parks department for a list of the fountains it maintains today. It sent a spreadsheet of 61. So we took the ledger’s 412 entries and went to every address on them.

What we found

Ninety-seven fountains are still standing, or the cast base of one is. Thirty-one gave water when we pressed the button. Of the other 315, we found a patched square of pavement at 140 addresses, a planter or a litter bin at 22, and nothing we could tie to a fountain at the rest.

Celia Brandt, deputy director of parks since 2019, said crews removed fountains when they broke and parts could not be had. “Nobody decided to take them out,” she said. “One stopped working, we couldn’t get a valve for it, and the crew capped it off.” Removals went on work orders, which the department keeps for seven years.

The newer a fountain is, the likelier it is to be standing. The losses cluster in the 1960s and 70s, when the city bought 273 cast-iron fountains from the Hesper foundry in Dunmore. Their valves were made to the foundry’s own pattern, and Hesper closed in 1983.

How we counted

Two reporters and four volunteers from the Calder Walking Club visited every address between July and October. A fountain counted as found if any part of it was still fixed to the ground, and as working if water ran for five seconds. Where a street had been renamed we used the 1970 city directory to match the address. The full list, with a photograph of each of the 97, is in our data repository, and the parks department has a copy.

If you know where one of the missing fountains went, write to fountains@calderledger.example. We will add what you tell us and say who told us.
