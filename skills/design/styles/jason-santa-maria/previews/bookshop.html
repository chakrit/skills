Dulas Brook Books, 11 Bear Street, Hay-on-Wye

Old books on
three floors, with England
across the brook.

Open today10 till half past 5. The stove in the back room is lit from November to March.

StockAbout 38,000 books, most of them priced in pencil on the front endpaper.

Margaret Pryce opened the shop in the spring of 1977, in a former saddler’s halfway down Bear Street, when there were already a dozen booksellers in Hay and the castle was full of them. She bought the stock of a retired vicar in Clyro to fill the first shelves, and a few of his books are still here, because nobody has wanted a commentary on Leviticus in forty-nine years.

Her daughter Elin runs it now. The ground floor holds Welsh history, the Marches, walking and natural history, and the poetry we can never resist buying. Upstairs is fiction, A to Z, with a room for Penguins and a shelf of green Penguin crime that people photograph more than they buy. The cellar is cookery, gardening and the overflow. The Dulas Brook, which divides Wales from England here, runs under the lane behind the shop.

We still print a catalogue three times a year and post it to anyone who asks. The latest is below. Ring or write to reserve a book; we hold it for a fortnight and send it anywhere.

Visiting the shop

We are halfway down Bear Street, on the left as you walk from the clock tower, with a green door and a trolley of 50p paperbacks outside when it isn’t raining. Pay for those through the letterbox if we are closed.

The ground floor has a step at the door and a ramp we can put down for you; ring the bell. The upper floor is reached by stairs only, but we will bring any book down.

Selling us books

We buy single books and whole libraries, and we come to the house for collections within about sixty miles of Hay. Bring a box in on a weekday morning, when Elin is in, and she will make an offer while you wait.

If you are looking for something we haven’t got, send us the author and title. We keep a wants list and write when a copy comes in, sometimes years later.
