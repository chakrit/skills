Being the tune book of the

Thirty-First

Hebden Bridge

Folk Festival

three days of songs, tunes and dancing at the bottom of a valley so steep that the houses are built on top of each other

Every June since 1997 the town fills up with fiddles. The festival began in the back room of the White Lion with eleven musicians and a borrowed piano,1 and it has grown, slowly and against the advice of the neighbours, into three days across six rooms, a square and a chapel up the hill in Heptonstall.

This year there are twenty-two acts on the bill and more sessions than anybody has counted. The tunes are listed below the way a tune book lists them: by number, with the key and the time they are played in. Anyone with an instrument may join a session. Anyone without one may sing.

The Tunes

a selection from this year’s bill, numbered from where last year’s left off

  1. № 412

    Ellen Sutcliffe

    Unaccompanied songs collected from farms around Colden and Blackshaw Head, some of them from her own grandmother.

    G major, 6/8Friday, Trades Club

  2. № 413

    The Mytholm Ranters

    A five-piece dance band from the bottom of Mytholm Steeps. Polkas, rants and a caller who will not let you sit down.

    D major, 2/4Saturday, Town Hall

  3. № 414

    Joe Ackroyd & Martha Lee

    Fiddle and Anglo concertina. Slip jigs mostly, and one waltz Joe wrote for the Rochdale Canal.

    A major, 9/8Saturday, Picture House

  4. № 415

    The Lumb Falls Sisters

    Three sisters singing in three parts. They have sung at every festival since 2004 and have never once agreed on a set list.

    E minor, 3/4Sunday, Heptonstall Chapel

  5. № 416

    Rowan Haigh

    Guitar and songs about the mills, written in Todmorden over the winter and played here for the first time.

    C major, 4/4Friday, Picture House

  6. № 417

    Cragg Vale Rapper

    Five dancers, five flexible swords and a fiddler. They dance on a square of plywood they carry with them.

    G major, 6/8Saturday, the Square, free

  7. № 418

    Niamh Ó Riada

    Uilleann pipes, from Ennis by way of Halifax. Slow airs first, then reels until the room is warm.

    D major, 12/8Sunday, Trades Club

  8. № 419

    The Old Town Mummers

    The same play every year, with a doctor, a dragon and a Turkish knight. Somebody dies and gets better.

    no known keySunday, the Square, free

The other fourteen acts are in the printed programme, sold for a pound at the Town Hall door. The money pays for the piano tuner.

Index of Places

every room with music in it, and how many it holds before the door is shut

  1. The Trades Clubholds 300Holme Street. Built by the town’s trade unions in 1924 and still owned by its members. The main concerts, every night.
  2. The Picture Households 490New Road, by the canal. A cinema since 1921, bought by the town in 1978 so it would stay one. Seated concerts, Friday and Saturday.
  3. The Town Hallholds 220St George’s Street. The ceilidh on Saturday night, and the workshops by day: clog, concertina, and songs for people who say they cannot sing.
  4. St George’s Squareholds the townDancing outside from eleven on Saturday and Sunday. If it rains, the dancing carries on and the audience stands under the shop awnings.
  5. Heptonstall Methodist Chapelholds 160Northgate, Heptonstall. Octagonal, built in 1764, and the best room for unaccompanied singing in the valley.2 Sunday afternoon only.
  6. The White Lion & the Shoulder of Muttonhold whoever fitsOpen sessions from noon each day. Bring an instrument, sit down, wait for a tune you know.

Thirty Summers

the festival’s life so far, written up from the minutes of the committee

  1. 1997

    Eleven musicians, one borrowed piano and the back room of the White Lion. Forty people come.

  2. 2003

    The Trades Club offers its hall for the Saturday night. The festival has a stage for the first time.

  3. 2008

    Cragg Vale Rapper dance in the Square, and have done every year since.

  4. 2012

    Hebden Water floods the town centre twice in one summer. The festival moves up the hill to Heptonstall for a year.

  5. 2015

    On Boxing Day the river comes into the Picture House and the shops on Market Street. The 2016 festival raises the money for a new piano.3

  6. 2026

    The thirtieth festival. The White Lion sessions run until twenty to four on Monday morning.
