# Bans

Patterns that mark a design as AI-made. They hold in every style.

A ban is broken by the pattern, whatever the markup. Replacing a banned pattern with its
nearest sibling (listed under each ban) breaks the same ban.

## Type and copy

- **Eyebrows everywhere.** No small tracked uppercase label above section headings as a
  section stamp. Siblings: a hairline or dash before the label, a numbered kicker
  (`01 —`), a pill badge above the headline. A heading carries its own section.
- **Emphasis on words inside a headline.** No word or phrase inside a headline set
  apart by italic, colour, weight, size, underline, highlight or a drawn mark. Siblings:
  a slanted, hand-drawn or brush underline, a highlighter swipe, a circled word, gradient
  text on one word. A headline is one voice; contrast comes from its size, placement and
  the lines around it. Stacked display lockups may give each whole line its own weight.
- **The italic punchline.** No copy built as a setup followed by a short emphasised
  payoff, such as "Ship it today, because *you can*." Siblings: the same payoff as a bold
  fragment, after an em dash, or as its own one-line paragraph. Say the thing plainly.
- **Template headline copy.** No interchangeable slogans ("Build faster. Ship smarter.",
  "Elevate your workflow") or invented metrics. Write a line only this business could
  say, from facts the user supplied.
- **Connector punctuation as decoration.** No middot or em-dash strings joining meta and
  labels (`Design · Build · Ship`). No arrow glyph on every link.
- **One default face for everything.** No Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Lato, Space
  Grotesk or the system stack as the whole type system. Siblings: the fashionable
  oversized italic serif hero, monospace labels as decoration.
- **Emoji as icons or bullets.**

## Layout

- **The card grid.** No row of equal rounded boxes, each holding icon, title and blurb.
  Siblings: the bento mosaic, the same cards with glass or neon borders.
- **The landing-page stamp.** No hero → three features → testimonials → logo strip → CTA
  → footer sequence chosen because pages usually look like that.
- **The centred hero formula.** No centred headline over a one-line subhead and a
  filled-plus-ghost button pair on a faint glow.
- **The hero metric and the stats strip.** No row of big-number tiles with icons as
  proof. Siblings: the same numbers as a scrolling ticker.
- **The logo belt.** No "Trusted by" row of muted logos unless the user supplied real
  ones; even then, not as a decorative strip.
- **Metronome rhythm.** No identical padding, width and alignment for every section.

## Colour and surface

- **The purple-blue gradient.** No indigo-to-violet hero or gradient headline. Siblings:
  the same gradient in teal or emerald.
- **The evenly split palette.** No four to six colours at equal strength. One dominant
  colour, used in clearly unequal amounts.
- **House palettes of AI tools.** No cream ground with a serif and terracotta accent; no
  near-black with a single acid-green or vermilion accent.
- **Reflex surfaces.** No soft shadow and mid radius on every surface, no glassmorphism,
  glow or blurred colour blobs as default decoration.

## Motion

- **Blanket reveal.** No fade-up or slide-in applied to every element on scroll.
- **Uniform hover lift.** No `translateY` lift as the hover for every surface.

## Imagery

- **Generic generated or stock imagery.** No glossy diffusion-look photos, clay or 3D
  pastel figures, isometric mini-scenes, or a stock team laughing at a laptop. Use the
  business's own material, or a drawn or typographic treatment.
