## 1. Moves

### Move 1. Scroll as a journey with a gauge

What: Atlantis is one 18,123-pixel page that you descend. A fixed red teardrop at the left
counts "0K … 20K LEAGUES" as you scroll (code: `scrollTop / 850`), with a ruler of ticks
down the left edge [observed]. A steel tube runs down the centre with porthole windows in
which a small figure rides the elevator; the sea changes from pale aqua through a dark
navy "Dark Zone" with glowing plankton and silhouettes of a plesiosaur and a squid, back
to light, then a countdown 10…1 in coloured circles, then a glass dome over a city skyline
and a "Hi." sign at the bottom [observed]. He described it: "The interactivity is
controlled by the user scrolling, and a story unfolds as they get closer to the bottom of
the sea" [stated (Wayback)].

Replaces: a static landing page or a carousel; scroll as mere reading.

Fits: one-off showcases, launches, stories where length can become a place. Give the
reader a running sense of progress (the gauge) and a payoff at the end.

### Move 2. Type as signage in an invented world

What: the Atlantis page shows off web fonts by making every piece of type a physical sign
from a 1962 fair: "ATLANTIS" in wide Hellenic Wide on a black banner with stars, "1962"
on a folded orange-yellow ribbon, WORLDS FAIR spelled one letter per coloured circle, a
vertical marquee "WELCOME", a subway-style "BIWAY MAP" with a pointing-hand "YOU ARE
HERE", a hexagon "AVAILABLE: AD SPACE", "AT/LAN/TIS" broken into three lines inside a
black diamond flanked by four colour tabs [observed]. Lettering.js splits words into
letters, words or lines so each can be coloured or placed separately [observed, HTML].
Copy is deadpan in-world jokes ("Gills or Lungs / Come as you are!", "The national bird
of Atlantis is the sting ray") [observed]. Palette: aqua sea, orange-red, yellow-orange,
lime green, teal, black, with grainy noise over flat vector shapes [observed].

Replaces: a type specimen grid or a hero headline.

Fits: whenever type is the product being shown. Invent a setting, then let every label
be an object in it; write the copy in character.

### Move 3. Let the content's character set the frame, not the convention

Each brief starts from what the content or event feels like, then bends structure to it:

- Do Lectures: the event is woodsy and surprising, so the site uses "rough edges,
  texture, and worn typography" to feel "lived-in", and talks are sorted by tone as well
  as topic, because a visitor is more curious about seeds or bees "if they know how it is
  going to make them feel" [stated (Wayback)].
- Thinking for a Living: the iPad's gestures are bi-directional, so "we flipped the site
  on its side to improve legibility"; the goal was "a calm environment suitable for
  reading and contemplation" [stated (Wayback)]. Seen: issues as horizontal panels paged
  by edge arrows, each opening on one huge magenta serif quotation on pale pink, then a
  three-column list of italic serif titles [observed (Wayback)].
- Web Press: newspapers share the web's "transience, cheapness, piecemeal" qualities, so
  web ephemera is printed as a newspaper [stated (Wayback)].

Replaces: the category's default (event site = schedule grid; magazine = vertical feed).

Fits: any brief. Name the feeling of the thing first and choose one structural departure
that carries it (navigation by mood, horizontal reading, a cheap medium).

### Move 4. One sentence is the whole page

What: studiofrank.co is a full-bleed sky photograph with a single yellow sentence in a
light serif, an email pill and a "Booking September" status dot; nothing else [observed].
design.frankchimero.com (2021) is a list with no images: each client name set huge in
Newsreader Light on black, year and one-line description small at the sides, hairlines
between rows [observed]. The book site shows one object, the cover [observed].

Replaces: hero + services cards + logo wall + case-study grid.

Fits: studios, personal practices, single products. Write the one sentence well; set it
large in a quiet serif; let a photograph or empty space do the rest.

### Move 5. Serif voice, sans utility, high contrast in size

Across live pieces the speaking voice is a light or text serif and the furniture is a
small neutral sans or mono: Studio Frank, Signifier Light sentence with Söhne buttons
[observed, CSS]; the design index, Newsreader Light names at `clamp(24px, 4vw, 92px)`
against 14px system sans captions at weight 600 [observed, CSS]; Shape of Design site, a
serif cover against typewriter-mono quotes [observed]; TFAL, giant serif quote against
small italic serif lists [observed (Wayback)]. Client faces he listed: Charter with Source
Sans (Safari Flow), Georgia with Gotham and Knockout (NPR Music), Tiempos Text with
Calibre (The Manual), Leitura with Maison Neue and Druk (TGD), Quadraat (his book)
[stated (Wayback)].

Replaces: one geometric sans for everything.

Fits: reading-led and brand pages. Make the big thing a light serif, the small things a
plain sans; skip the middle sizes.

### Move 6. A slow, ambient change instead of motion effects

What: the design-portfolio info page cycles its background through ten pastel hues over
130 seconds, barely noticeable [observed, CSS]. Atlantis changes the whole sea colour
with depth [observed]. Aaarrrppp turns an image into a slowly building piece of music and
a strip animation [stated].

Replaces: entrance animations and hover flourishes.

Fits: pages people sit with. Change atmosphere slowly; let time or progress drive it.

### Move 7. One design system that crosses media both ways

What: for The Great Discontent he translated a web interview site into print, then "the
design has flip-flopped": print choices went back to the web; "We developed a true
multi-format design system" [stated (Wayback)]. NPR Music: a responsive site plus concepts
for how its visual brand elements "could apply to the rest of NPR's brand" [stated
(Wayback)].

Fits: brands with both editorial and product surfaces. Design the system on the harder
medium, then let it inform the other.

## 2. Process and thinking

- Start stuck, return to "beginner's mind"; distrust preconceptions that "dress up as
  experience or expertise" [stated, work-looks-back].
- A wall of references and printed slides; rearranging them by hand finds the concept
  [stated, work-looks-back].
- Two modes of design: one of "specificity and clarity", one that "runs on suggestion,
  excitement, and provocation"; the second builds anticipation [stated].
- Design must be "attractive" in both senses, beautiful and magnetic; "design can inform,
  yet that very often needs to begin by seducing" [stated].
- Words can unlock the image: naming the talk "The Eye Knows" made the poster click
  [stated].
- "Once through, cleanly" for small self-initiated pieces: sketch, then make it in one
  sitting; if bad, start over [stated]. He says it is not how to work with others.
- Works in small teams of named peers (Paravel, JSM, Naz Hamid, Duane King, Draplin) and
  credits each [stated].
- From 2018 he described his client work as "mostly staring at colored rectangles"
  (branding and interface) and missed images that "look back" [stated].

## 3. Taste

- Warmth and content first: his own bio calls his work "warm, content-focused design"
  [stated, info page].
- Illustration and type play: invented worlds, in-world jokes, folk signage, noise
  texture over flat colour (Atlantis) [observed].
- Print and the cheap end of print: newspapers, posters, journals, books [stated].
- Art references: Baldessari's captioned images, Richter strips, Franz Marc, Winslow
  Homer [stated].
- Quiet, light serifs at large size; yellow and sky blue now [observed].
- Restraint in portfolios: lists and sentences rather than image grids [observed].
