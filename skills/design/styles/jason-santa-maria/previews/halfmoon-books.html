Mon 23 Nov

On Friday Margaret Ellery brought in the paperbacks her father kept in the spare room from about 1958 on. Forty-one of them, most with the orange band that meant a novel, several with his name on the flyleaf and a bus ticket still marking his place.

There’s Graham Greene, a run of early Muriel Spark, a Lucky Jim held together with a rubber band and three copies of the same Simenon, bought by a man who kept forgetting he owned it. Most are £3. The Spark first printings are £6, and the Lucky Jim is £2 because of the rubber band.

They’re on the low table by the front window until Sunday. On Monday they go to the shelves with everything else, and you’ll have to hunt for them.

Selling

We buy most weeks, from a single book to a house clearance. Bring them in between eleven and four, Tuesday to Saturday, and Ruth or Tom will go through them while you wait. For more than a few boxes, ring us and one of us will come to you.

We pay in cash, or in credit for the shop, which is worth a third more. We don’t take encyclopaedias, Reader’s Digest condensed books or anything that has spent a winter in a garage, and we’ll say so before you carry them up the step.
