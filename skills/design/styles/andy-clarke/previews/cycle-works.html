We mend the bike you already own

Brindle & Burr is a two-bench repair shop in Arch 14 on Cable Street. Nell Burr fixes the bikes. Brindle, a greyhound who stopped racing in 2019, sleeps under the repair stand.

Bring in whatever you ride, including the tourer that's been in the shed since 2009. Before any spanner comes out, Nell ties a paper ticket to the handlebar with the price written on it, and the parts she takes off go home with you in a bag.

What it costs

These are the five jobs we do most. Anything else, Nell looks at the bike and writes the price on your ticket. If it turns out to cost more once she's inside, she rings you first.

How a repair goes

  1. Wheel it in

    No appointment for punctures. For anything bigger, book a slot so the bike isn't waiting in the queue by the door.

  2. Get a ticket

    Nell writes what's wrong and the price on a brown card ticket and ties it to your handlebar. You keep the stub.

  3. We ring you

    If she finds something the ticket doesn't cover, a cracked rim or a seized bottom bracket, she phones before touching it.

  4. Ride it home

    Pay at the bench and take a lap of the yard before you go. If anything feels wrong, wheel it straight back in.

Asked at the counter

Do you sell bikes?

No. Now and then a customer leaves a bike they don't want and we fix it up and sell it on; those go on the board by the door.

Will you work on an e-bike?

Brakes, tyres, gears and wheels, yes. Motors and batteries go back to the maker's dealer; we can tell you which one.

Can I watch?

Yes, there's a stool by the bench. Nell will explain what she's doing if you ask, and keep quiet if you don't.

Is the dog friendly?

Brindle is eleven and mostly asleep. She likes people who ignore her, and she does not chase bikes any more.
