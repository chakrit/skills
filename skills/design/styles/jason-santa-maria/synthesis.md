# Jason Santa Maria: synthesis

The design model behind `style.md`, drawn from four research streams. Citations name the
note and its section: `own M5` is move M5 in `notes/own.md`; `work Move 4` and `work
table` refer to `notes/work.md`; `talks 1.8` to `notes/talks.md`; `accounts` sections by
heading. `book chN` is a chapter of *On Web Typography* (2014), which he offers as a free
download from his home page under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; its findings are summarised in `own
§2` ("Type method") and paraphrased here, never quoted at length.

Evidence marks: **(seen)** means the point was checked by eye in a browser on 2026-11-23
(live pages or Wayback captures); **(CSS)** means it rests on stylesheets, markup, image
files or write-ups and was not seen rendered; **(said)** means it is his stated view or
another person's report, with no visual claim.

## 1. The core idea: typography is the design

He is a graphic designer who works from type outward. Type is the smallest unit of
design, and he moves between the single letter and the whole page the way the Eames film
*Powers of Ten* moves between scales: the measure is set by the column, the column by the
measure (book intro, ch5–6; own §2). Peers describe his art-directed pages as carried
"almost exclusively" by typography (accounts, "Type carries the art direction"). Seen in
the work, that holds for the quote posts and many essays; photographs carry a handful of
posts as much as type does (accounts, same section; own M7, M8) (seen).

Two consequences shape everything else:

- **The setting matters more than the face.** The design comes through in how type is
  set, not in which typeface was picked; a face is a starting point, not the centrepiece
  (talks 1.8 warning; book ch4) (said). Ready-made novelty faces are refused because
  their design is baked in (own M5; book ch4) (said).
- **Reading comes first.** Readability means the reader wants to read, not only can
  (talks §2 "Empathy for the reader"; book ch1). Article pages should be big, full and
  generous all the way down, against "anemic" pages of small type and chrome (talks 1.6;
  accounts, "Full, generous article pages") (said; AIGA article page seen, work Move 9).

## 2. Where ideas come from

- **The content as a physical object.** Before layout, he asks what object the content
  would be in the world and borrows that object's structural devices: A List Apart as a
  book or McSweeney's-like journal read closely, with numbered issues, a laurel, a seal,
  ruled bylines (talks 1.1; accounts, "Model the site on a book…") (seen, Wayback 2005).
  His own v3 site was a pulp paperback with spine, price roundel and series copy (own
  M10) (CSS and image files). WordPress's logo came from publishing as a hand-made act,
  ending as a typewriter key (talks 1.1) (said).
- **The subject's own material, not its clichés.** Comhaltas kept a quiet knotwork band
  and no shamrocks (talks 1.1; accounts, "Research the subject") (seen). Ornament comes
  from inside the text: a nail divider because a nail matters in the story; each candy
  linked in its brand's colour (own M16) (CSS; the candy chart image seen).
- **Period and origin pick the face.** A face's time, place and purpose carry meaning:
  American Typewriter for a writer's quote, Cooper Black for 1970s candy, Franklin Caslon
  for Poe (own M5 table, all rows seen; book ch4 "appropriateness through origin").
  These are period faces, not novelty fonts (own M5, inferred line).
- **Morgue files, sketchbooks, piles of ugly ideas.** Clippings, matchbooks, old ads; a
  page a day of sketches for thinking, not art; a big mass of bad ideas to get past the
  obvious ones (talks §2 "Discovery first"; own §2 "Piles of ugly ideas"; work §2)
  (said).
- **Good over original.** He quotes Rand paraphrasing Mies: try to be good, not original;
  originality for its own sake falls into tropes (own §3; talks §3 "On trends") (said).

## 3. Process

1. **Define the client in words.** At Slate: a guide and companion, reliable, curious,
   witty; the visual direction then shows "story-making" (work §2; own §2) (said).
2. **Mood words before fonts.** List words the project must embody, group them, then look
   for faces that embody one or two (talks 1.8 step 1; book ch4; Mighty example in own
   §2 and book ch4) (said).
3. **Text face first.** Readers spend their time in paragraphs, so the reading face is
   chosen before the display voice (talks 1.8 step 2; work Move 4) (said).
4. **Type sketches.** Throwaway pages with real headings and paragraphs, the same text in
   candidate faces, smallest size first; reject a face that only looks good once refined;
   leave with two or three candidates (talks 1.8 step 3; book ch4) (said).
5. **Mould the chosen face.** Set it big, in caps, tracked, as paragraph, in colour, to
   find its range (talks 1.8 step 5) (said).
6. **Grey box.** A colourless comp to settle hierarchy and placement before colour,
   because a polished comp pulls attention to detail and loses the message (talks §2;
   accounts §2; own §2) (said). Zeldman hired him on the reasoning of that method
   (accounts §2).
7. **Several directions, then revise.** Three distinct comps, no "Frankensteining", the
   second round spent on typography (talks §2; accounts §2) (said). He finds revising
   easier than creating (work §2; talks §2) (said).
8. **Judge by eye.** Scales and grids are starting points; when maths and the eye
   disagree, trust the eye (own §2 "Optics over numbers"; book ch6, conclusion;
   accounts §2) (said).
9. **Start small, spread the system.** At Slate, one article template outside the CMS
   first, then the rest; at WordPress, a handful of key pages for others to extend (work
   Move 10) (said).

## 4. Type: choosing

- **Two jobs.** "Type for a moment" (headlines, nav, buttons, short lines) can carry
  strong personality; "type to live with" (long reading) must be even and unobtrusive
  (book ch4; accounts, "Hierarchy through contrast") (said).
- **Text-face tests.** Enough x-height; low to medium stroke contrast; distinct
  letterforms (Il1); squint at a block and reject recurring odd shapes; "a touch of
  personality", never cute over pages (book ch3–4; talks 1.9) (said).
- **Pairing by distinction and harmony.** One face can be enough, two is usually plenty;
  the safe fallback is one serif and one sans; never two serifs, two sans or two display
  faces (talks 1.7; book ch4) (said). Harmony from a shared skeleton, a superfamily, the
  same designer, or one big family in two or three styles (book ch4). In practice: a calm
  book serif for reading against a characterful, often light or condensed display voice
  (work Move 4, across ALA, TMN, Editorially, Slate, Loud Room) (CSS; Loud Room and Type
  Founders seen).
- **Few faces, rich palette.** Distinction from size, weight, colour, spacing, tracked
  caps, italics and widths rather than more faces; a big family matters most (book
  ch3–4; talks 1.7) (said). v5 used four faces, each with one job (own M13) (CSS).
- **Deconstruct to escape the workhorse.** List the defining traits of an over-used face
  and find a face that shares some of them (book ch3) (said).
- **Against the default.** Helvetica and Arial chosen out of fear "say nothing"; corporate
  blue is "blanding" (book ch3; talks 1.3) (said).
- **A small working set.** Know a few faces deeply; add one new one now and then (talks
  §2; accounts §2) (said). Favourites: Chaparral, Proxima Nova, FF Meta Serif, Quadraat,
  Calluna, Caslon, Trade Gothic, News Gothic; he leans to "simple and sturdy sans serifs
  and book serifs" (own §3; book ch3–4) (said).

## 5. Type: setting (his numbers)

From the book (ch3, ch5, ch6) and talks, all (said); the site values are (CSS):

| Setting              | His guidance                                                       |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Body size            | 16–18px base, relative units; when in doubt, bigger                |
| Measure              | 45–75 characters                                                   |
| Line-height          | start 1.2–1.8, unitless; longer lines need more                    |
| Small screens        | line-height down toward 1.2–1.3, size down as lines shorten        |
| Paragraphs           | space equal to or under the type size, or a 1em indent, not both   |
| Headlines            | 2–3× body, subheads between, then adjust by eye                    |
| Small text           | about 80% of base, a change of voice, a few lines at most          |
| Captions             | about 12px on screen                                               |
| Margin around text   | at least 1.5–2em                                                   |
| Text colour          | dark grey such as #333 often reads better than black               |
| Typographic colour   | aim for a medium grey; light faces tighter, heavy faces looser     |
| Baseline grid        | not on the web; rhythm from size and spacing                       |

His own sites: v4 Georgia 14/20 on a 520px measure (own M9) (CSS); v5 Chaparral 20px/1.5
in #444 with book indents (own M13) (CSS; seen); TMN Minion 17/23 (work table) (CSS);
Loud Room body up to 40px (work Move 5) (CSS). Display sizes are hand-picked: small steps
at text sizes, big jumps at display sizes (own M13) (CSS).

Large type is treated like a photograph: few words, breaks tuned per width, troublesome
pairs kerned; a decorative face may appear here once (book ch4–5; own M6) (said). Craft
details are part of the design: real quotes and primes, true italics and bolds, old-style
figures in text, tabular in tables, true small caps (book ch3, ch6; talks 1.12) (said).

## 6. Hierarchy and composition

- **Hierarchy is combined contrast.** The most important element is the most distinct,
  by two or more of size, weight, colour, placement; if everything is big, nothing is;
  style toward what the visitor should do (book ch5; talks 1.7) (said). He wants one
  focal point per page (talks 1.7) (said).
- **Extreme scale contrast** is a recurring signature: thin or condensed display at many
  times body size, so the jump is the composition (work Move 5: Loud Room HEX Franklin
  Thin up to 150px, TMN 62px weight 200, Type Founders condensed caps at 5–12rem)
  (Loud Room and Type Founders seen; TMN CSS).
- **An offset reading column.** Text is hung off-centre on a coarse grid and the empty
  side frames what spans the full width: v4's 520px column pushed right (own M9) (CSS);
  Loud Room paragraphs from column 3 of 6 under a full-width headline (work Move 8)
  (seen). v5 is the exception, a centred single measure for everything (own M13) (CSS,
  seen).
- **Grid as possibilities.** A divisible column grid (12 into 6, 4, 3) with one or two
  "supercolumns" breaking out; image sizes planned as column spans (talks 1.5; accounts,
  "Grids that are mathematically flexible") (said). A "layer cake" of horizontal bands
  down the page, with column grids inside some bands (book ch6) (said).
- **Interludes.** Long pages get rhythm from recurring devices (drop caps, a big photo, a
  colour splash) that repeat like a chorus (own M19) (said).
- **Inverted hierarchy as a move.** The story's first sentence as the display line and the
  title small (own M15, "Monkey Nuts") (seen, with a correction on placement).
- **Quiet pages are allowed.** ALA 4.0's home page had no shouting focal point because an
  issue has several peer items; it drew criticism, so a reading order must still be clear
  (work Move 3; accounts, "No shouting focal point") (said; ALA seen).

## 7. The fixed frame and the variable piece

This is the idea he is best known for (accounts, "A fixed frame, a free interior").

- **The frame** is a stable set of named hooks: masthead, logo, navigation, utility text,
  base grid, base type, footer (own M1) (CSS). Each piece restyles the same hooks.
- **The frame is re-tinted, never removed.** In v4 the logo and nav stayed in place on
  every post but came in the post's colours; even the hand-drawn post kept the frame's
  position (own M4) (seen). v5 did the same with its red (own M13) (seen).
- **One variable per instalment.** ALA changed only colour per issue through a tiny CSS
  file (work Move 1) (seen); A Book Apart one hue per book in a fixed cover layout (work
  Move 7) (seen); Loud Room a random colour pair per visit, bounded by a 4.5:1 contrast
  floor (work Move 1, Move 9) (seen). The family holds by layout; the variable names the
  piece.
- **A plain default is part of the system.** Most pieces stay on the default; art
  direction is "a practice of restraint" and goes only to pieces that earn it (own M2,
  M13) (said; observed in tag counts). Custom design for every post made him feel posts
  could not be short, "a design trap" (own M13) (said).
- **Name the variable as a spec.** v4 tagged each post's design: one typeface plus two to
  four colours and a treatment (own M3) (CSS).
- **Series identity lives in a small kit** (byline treatment, footer note) while each
  issue varies the rest (own M17) (seen).
- **A piece and its design are one work.** Old designs are preserved, not restyled (own
  M12; accounts) (said).

## 8. Colour

- **A calm field and one accent.** A quiet neutral base with one deliberate, slightly
  surprising accent reserved for focal points and actions; WordPress admin cleared of
  blue, one orange for actions only (talks 1.3) (said). v5: greys and one red (own M13)
  (seen).
- **Tonal floods for short pieces.** A quote or announcement floods the page in one hue,
  text in a dark shade of that hue rather than black, one contrast accent; four to six
  steps of one hue, not a rainbow (own M7) (seen).
- **Palette sampled from one artefact.** A watercolour wash (Happy Cog), VHS and cassette
  packaging (Megazoid), a photo, wine labels (work Move 6; talks 1.3) (Happy Cog seen;
  sampling is inference).
- **Cheat colour optically.** A small element in the brand hex reads lighter or darker
  than a large one; shift the small one to restore the perceived colour (talks 1.4)
  (said).
- **Taste.** Warm neutrals with a confident accent in his 2005–2013 client work (work §3)
  (CSS; Happy Cog and ALA seen). He enjoys a near-clashing or "garish" palette when the
  subject is play (work Move 1, Move 6) (said).

## 9. Imagery and surface

- **The photograph can be the page**, with text inside it ("Royal": the post typed on the
  sheet in the typewriter) (own M8) (seen, with correction). Photos bleed off edges or
  float on a ground matching the page (own M8) (seen).
- **Illustration per piece** in ALA, one illustrator's hand across issues (talks 1.2)
  (said).
- **Hairlines and small ornaments as structure** rather than boxes and shadows (work §3)
  (CSS; ALA dashed rules seen).
- **Material references only when the content calls for them.** He calls heavy
  skeuomorphism "Blech" yet dressed v3 as a paperback; the reference belongs to content,
  not interface (own §3, inferred) (said).
- **Motion** appears only where the subject plays: Megazoid letters extrude on hover
  through the font's own axes (work Move 9) (CSS).

## 10. Taste summary

Likes: books, magazines, letterpress, posters, found vernacular type; sturdy text faces
with a little personality; thin or condensed display voices; real white space; restrained
bases with a vivid accent; work you stop and absorb (own §3; work §3; talks §3).
Dislikes: Helvetica and Arial as defaults; generic corporate blue; category clichés;
trend-chasing; ready-made fonts; faux styles and dumb quotes; small body text; anemic
article pages; clutter; whitespace treated as space to fill; "generic design spat out by
a machine" (own §3; work §3; talks §3; book ch3–6).

## Cautions and gaps

- **Ban conflicts.** Two of his moves break `../../references/bans.md` as he made them;
  `style.md` leaves them out or rewords them:
  - Navigation as a sentence (work Move 2; talks 1.11) puts coloured capital links inside
    a large display sentence: word-level emphasis inside a headline. `style.md` keeps the
    idea only as a lead-size sentence with ordinary body links.
  - The "Sugar Smack" title with a photo hung between its two words (own M6) sets an
    object inside a headline; `style.md` leaves it out.
  His letterspaced capital labels (ALA, v4) are utility text in the frame, not a stamp
  above each section heading, so `style.md` keeps them on that condition. A per-letter
  striped title (own M16) treats the whole title evenly and stays.
- **Fixed widths.** v4 (910px) and v5 (916px) were fixed-width; ALA was too (own M9, M13;
  work Move 8). Phone design is the thinnest evidence: only the book's small-screen
  numbers, his current site scaling the root size, and clamp-based display sizes (book
  ch6; own M11; work Move 5). The phone guidance in `style.md` extends these.
- **His own site is not his client work.** On his site he put experiment before
  robustness and accepted costs (tiny typed text in "Royal", a sIFR overlap bug) (own §2,
  M8). Client work reversed that order.
- **Focal point tension.** He insists on one focal point (talks 1.7) yet defended ALA's
  home page without one (work Move 3). Read it as: one focal point unless the page truly
  has several peers, and then a clear reading order.
- **Not seen rendered.** Most v4 art-directed posts beyond about 20 (own §4); TMN article
  pages, Editorially's app, Typekit's fonts, the WordPress admin, AIGA's column count,
  Megazoid and Chonk/Citywide specimens (work §4; talks §4). The accounts stream is
  second-hand throughout.
- **Book only by paraphrase.** The free book was read in full but is CC BY-NC-ND; nothing
  here reproduces its text.
- **Contested claim.** "Top halves of letters carry more information" is a teaching
  device disputed by reading researchers (talks §4).
- **Attribution.** Type Founders' scroll-driven type may be the developer's (work §4);
  TMN's credit rests on Fonts In Use alone.
