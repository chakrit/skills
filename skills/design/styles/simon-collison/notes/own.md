## 1. Moves

### 1.1 The site as a costume: one period object wraps everything

- **What:** pick a period artefact and dress the whole site as it. In v4 it was an
  Edwardian gentleman's journal inside "a miscellany of nonsense". Masthead: italic
  "Established Nottingham 2003", letter-spaced caps "THE CELEBRATED NEW MISCELLANY OF",
  then condensed red caps "MR. SIMON COLLISON". Section names are period phrases: "Potted
  Autobiography", "The Splendid Journal", "Exhaustive Archives". Each has an italic
  kicker above it: "Bottled for your pleasure", "Catalogued nocturnal matter". The footer
  is a lockup around a huge ampersand: "WARNING & THIS CONTENT IS WRY & DERANGED BUT 100%
  TRUSTWORTHY AND BONAFIDE". [observed, colly.com/v4/ and Wayback 2010]
- **Source of the idea:** Schott's Miscellany (its tone and attention to detail), plus his
  old sketchbooks and missing "archiving as opposed to blogging". [stated, 2010 post]
- **Default it replaces:** a blog that looks like a blog (CollyLogic 2004–06 was exactly
  that). [observed]
- **Why:** his own site "should be whatever the hell I want it to be". It is the place to
  "abandon sensible signage and navigation, and write copy in a slightly weird way", the
  freedom client work denies. [stated]
- **When it fits:** personal and editorial sites with a strong voice, where the conceit can
  run through every label, and not just a header graphic.

### 1.2 The irrelevant illustration (the menagerie)

- **What:** black-and-white natural-history engravings (pig, butterfly, fish, crab, flea,
  snake, pufferfish, lobster, turtle) appear one per card. Each animal is **deliberately
  unrelated** to its section: Flickr gets a flea, Delicious a snake, Facebook a snake
  captioned "Bloody Facebook", New Adventures a pufferfish. [observed; stated: "the
  wildlife should have no correlation to the section… that's where I get my kicks"]
- **Origin:** an old book about botanists and Darwin. The images came from a stock
  library, and he refused to apologise for that. The design clicked when he first put the
  butterfly next to the type. [stated]
- **Same instinct elsewhere:** the product name Rushmore was liked "because it's so
  irrelevant and yet people just accept it" (see `work.md`). [stated]
- **Effect reported:** readers had to read the witty captions, because the picture gave
  no clue. [reported, Ryan Taylor comment]
- **Default it replaces:** an icon that literally depicts the link (a camera for photos, a
  bird for Twitter).

### 1.3 A tired default typeface, used with full craft

- **What:** v4 set almost everything in Times New Roman: Regular and Italic, with Bold
  only for drop caps. Colleague Greg Wood suggested it. Size and colour vary through many
  spans. Uppercase navigation is letter-spaced; he quotes Goudy to justify spacing only
  caps. [stated] Measured: body Times 13px/22px, article body 15/22 #333, article title
  35px. [observed]
- **Why:** when Typekit and Fontdeck were arriving, "it actually feels quite good to be
  taking a tired old typeface and trying to squeeze every possible ounce from it". It also
  kept an image-heavy page light. [stated]
- **Reported:** Richard Rutter answered with Bringhurst's advice to make Times "richly and
  superbly ordinary". Several commenters said they had disliked Times until then.
  [reported]
- **Display pairing:** a condensed Franklin Gothic, chosen because a wide face "sucks" with
  the double L in "Collison", and because a narrow sans "doesn't feel too modern". It is
  paired with Clarendon for the Edwardian logo, with a touch of Mrs Eaves italic "to
  antiquate", a Baskerville ampersand and the odd monospace. [stated] The face was fitted
  to the letterforms of his own name.
- **When it fits:** to show craft through composition rather than through the choice of
  typeface, or for a period voice.

### 1.4 Editorial furniture: drop caps, numbered entries, footnotes, asides

- Big red Times drop caps open every card and article. The journal card uses a § sign as
  its drop cap. [observed]
- Posts are numbered like catalogue entries ("№ 809" in a black roundel between engraved
  PREV/NEXT arrows; archive lists "866—…" in bold red numerals). [observed]
- A left "SUPERFLUOUS ASIDE" column carries right-aligned italic metadata between dotted
  rules. A double rule sits under the title, and the date is in small caps ("16TH JULY
  2010"). [observed]
- Footnotes with return links were built as an Easter egg. He calls footnotes "so
  difficult to do properly on the web". [stated] The current site still footnotes the
  home page. [observed]
- Microcopy stays in voice: comments are closed because of "spamming guttersnipes", and
  the colophon reads "kept alive with tea and roll-ups". [observed]

### 1.5 Texture tempered by honesty: strip the fake wear

- **What:** the journal concept pulled him towards a "wicked-worn" look: textures, PNG
  layers, opacity. One honest critic said the backgrounds were "cheapening" the concept, so
  he stripped them down to "a more sober, less obtrusive canvas". What remained was a
  faint graph-paper grid and off-white cards. He also cut CSS3 rotations, fades and a
  spinning home button as gimmicks. [stated; observed]
- **Why:** his FOWD 2009 talk "Bauhaus Ideology and the Future of Web Design" argued for
  "truth to materials". He objects to fake coffee-cup rings on screens. [stated]
- **Result:** an antique *voice* on an honest screen surface. [observed] Erwin Heiser and
  Colin praised the restraint. [reported]

### 1.6 The life told as a timeline poem (current site, 2019)

- **What:** the home page is a horizontal timeline from 1973 to 2022, laid out in rows. On
  each row hang short poetic fragments in narrow columns ("There is football; there is
  pop and soft rock;"). Between them sit small framed images on stems: a Notts County
  sticker, a Peak District postcard, the New Order *Substance* sleeve, Sillitoe's
  paperback, a Polaroid. Years sit as pill labels on the horizontal rule. Rotated
  vertical labels mark turning points ("Graduate.", "Home."). Italic song lyrics carry
  footnote numbers that resolve to the song, album or painter. [observed]
- **Why:** a "museum of me". The things we love "speak volumes about who we are, and why
  the things we put into this world look or feel a certain way". Songs "carry the emotion
  of my story better than I can". Items are chosen because they "imply a lot": New Order
  implies Joy Division. [stated, 2019 post]
- **Inspiration:** Brian Cox's lecture on world lines, compressed "into a much less
  interstellar one-dimensional linearity". [stated]
- **Layout philosophy:** a flexbox layout he lets "twang around, shift and realign" as the
  window changes. He accepts that it is "a bit weird" on narrow screens and loves that
  weirdness. [stated]
- **About page follows the same line:** it opens with loves (music, art, birds, running,
  coffee) before clients. [observed]
- **When it fits:** a personal or about page that has to show a person's taste rather than
  their CV.

### 1.7 Calm dark ground for the later site

- **What:** a dark teal ground rgb(26,55,67) with pale blue-grey text rgb(186,207,215). FF
  Meta Serif for reading, PT Sans for navigation and labels, and a small italic serif
  tagline ("Well-meaning waffle since 2003."). Pull quotes are italic serif under a large
  blue quote mark. The type is quiet and the colour does the mood. [observed]
- He kept the old site at `/v4/` and links it from every footer ("Miss the old site?").
  The link carries a Zeldman quote about not letting the masterpiece go. [observed]

## 2. Process and thinking

- He started about a dozen concepts for v4 over a year and threw most away "with no
  regrets". The winning idea came fast, in October 2009, and was "blitzed" night after
  night. [stated]
- Type comes first. The design is "type-led", and he looks for faces "that would inform
  the whole feel" before layout. [stated]
- Juxtaposition test: the design clicked when the butterfly first sat beside the type
  ideas. [stated]
- Iteration happens in Photoshop and the browser for about two months, and he published
  the iterations as a Flickr set. He posted Dribbble teasers and showed three or four
  designers, but acted on the one honest critic. [stated]
- He divides opinion on purpose: "the work I'm most proud of has been the stuff that
  divides opinion… it tells me that I'm not being boring." [stated]
- He distrusts perfection: "pursuing perfection is—I think—a misguided quest". He
  attributes the two undesigned years to it. [stated]
- The timeline took five months of editing, likened to "chipping away at a block of
  marble… or constructing a poem". He spent most of that time on content rather than
  code. [stated]
- "I aim to please only myself with my personal website." [stated]

## 3. Taste

- **Music first:** New Order, The Stone Roses, R.E.M., Björk, Sigur Rós, DJ Shadow, Bowie,
  The National, Boards of Canada, Autechre, Kraftwerk. He makes music as Site Nonsite.
  [stated, home/archive]
- **Art:** Peter Lanyon and the St Ives painters, abstract expressionists, land art (his
  "new image of nature" idea dates from studying it), Olafur Eliasson's *Weather Project*.
  On Hockney he wrote that he learned "about colour, scale, landscape". [stated]
- **Place:** Nottingham, the Peak District, the Pennine moors, Iceland (artist residency
  1998), Newfoundland, Tokyo, Copenhagen. He is a lifelong birder and a Notts County
  supporter. [stated]
- **Print and period ephemera:** Schott's Miscellany, Victorian/Edwardian engravings,
  record sleeves, paperback covers, stickers. [stated; observed]
- **Culture over craft:** in 2026 he quoted Naz Hamid approvingly: "Culture is the moat.
  It's not craft and it's not aesthetic." [stated, quoting]
- **Dislikes:** fakery (coffee-cup rings, over-texturing), gratuitous CSS3 effects, being
  boring, chasing perfection. [stated]
- **Voice:** wry, British, self-deprecating, slightly bawdy (the "Carry On Responsively"
  post is a run of innuendo). [observed]
