# Dan Cederholm: synthesis

The design model behind `style.md`, drawn from four research streams in `notes/`.
Citations name the note and its section: `own 1.3` is move 1.3 in `notes/own.md`; `work
1.1` and `work table` refer to `notes/work.md`; `talks 5` is move 5 in `notes/talks.md`
and `talks process` its "Process and thinking" list; `accounts` sections go by heading.

Evidence marks follow the notes: **(seen)** means a note saw it in a browser on
2026-11-23 (the live site and shop, Dribbble shots, and Wayback captures of
simplebits.com from 2005 to 2016 viewed before the Wayback Machine was ruled out);
**(said)** means it is his stated view, or someone else's report, with no visual check.
The work stream is thin (most client work survives only in the Wayback Machine), so the
model leans on his own site, his goods and his words.

## 1. The core idea: a quiet structure, warm bits

His pages are plain in structure and warm in detail. The layouts are a centred column
with a narrow sidebar (2005 to 2011), then a single column of about 500px (2016), then a
plain stack of sections today (own 1.3) (seen). Peers described the 2010 site as "a
simple three column grid" with "a lot of whitespace", a "spartan beige color scheme" and
patterns only at the edges (accounts, "Quiet grid…") (said). The personality sits in
the small things: a cream ground, braces round the current nav item, a coloured italic
ampersand, small icons beside dates and permalinks, tab-shaped post markers hanging off
the sheet (own 1.3) (seen). The book reviewers call the result "handcrafted": "a high
level of craftsmanship and finish on everything" (accounts, "Craft as finish") (said).

Two consequences follow:

- **Decoration lives at the edges, never in the reading field.** The 2004 layout put a
  birch texture and a striped edge in the margins around a clean white column (talks 4)
  (said); the 2010 one put geometric patterns along the top, left and bottom edges
  (accounts, "Quiet grid…") (said).
- **The voice is strong enough to recognise.** By 2004 peers called his style "almost too
  strong" for a template: readers would see "a Cederholm template with my writing"
  (accounts, "A signature…") (said).

## 2. Two modes: his voice, or a frame for others

- **His own brand or a small shop:** the signature at full strength, in type, colour,
  ornament and jokes (own 1.3, 1.8; work 1.2) (seen).
- **Other people's work:** for Dribbble he recedes. The UI was kept "agnostic
  (monochrome, Helvetica type, simple glyphs)" so the shots supply all the colour; one
  pink accent comes from the logo; the play moves into the names (shots, rebounds,
  buckets) (talks 1; accounts, "Recede when…"; work 1.3) (said, not seen). Holding back
  his own instincts there "was always a challenge" (talks 1) (said).

The rule underneath: the voice goes where it does not compete with the content. In a
gallery it goes into the mark, the names and the copy; in a shop it goes everywhere
except the reading column.

## 3. Where ideas come from

- **A found object from ordinary commercial life.** A manhole cover photographed on his
  walk became "Electric Service Co." (talks 5) (said); the Kleenex logo led the Dribbble
  script (talks 5, 6) (said); every SimpleBits font is redrawn from vintage lettering: a
  Cairo hotel luggage label for Kyroh, a Cuban cigar box for Hubano (own 1.6) (said). He
  keeps the source's quirks: "the wacky crossbars of the A and H set the tone" (own 1.6)
  (said).
- **Americana and trade ephemera.** Ghost signs, Topps cards with flat offset printing,
  packaging, skateboard graphics, racing badges, hardware, baseball (own 3; work 3;
  talks taste) (said and seen).
- **What people already do.** Dribbble features came from watching members: rebounds
  started as people linking shots in comments; "pave the cowpaths" (talks process;
  accounts §2) (said).
- **Make it for yourself first.** "If someone else likes it, that's bonus" (talks
  process) (said). His test for a project: "Is this making me laugh a little bit or
  amusing me?" (talks 8) (said).

## 4. Process

1. **X-ray the content.** View the unstyled document "like taking an X-Ray", question
   each element and pare it down before styling (talks process) (said).
2. **Find the object** the brand's type and ornament will come from (section 3).
3. **Lay a quiet structure**: one column, or one column and a narrow side column, centred
   and flexible, with nothing hidden (own 1.3, process) (seen and said).
4. **Break the page into small named patterns** and design one strong version of each.
   His example is the "slat": thumbnail, title, description, optional date or tags,
   linking through, repeated down an index (talks 7) (said). He keeps his own pattern
   library rather than adopting a framework (talks 7) (said).
5. **Add the warm bits**: type, one signature colour, punctuation, small icons, edge
   ornament (sections 5 to 7).
6. **Enrich last.** Soft shadows, translucency, rounded corners, a short transition, all
   in an "experience layer" that older or smaller screens may skip (talks 3; accounts,
   "Craft as finish") (said).
7. **Ship small steps, then refine.** "Baby steps"; small improvements "keep us moving";
   progress over perfection (own §2) (said). He tries a mark on Dribbble, takes feedback,
   then ships (own 1.2) (said).
8. **Evolve, never restart.** Keep "hints to the past": the orange returns, the cube
   becomes a hexagon, the Dribbble script is smoothed, not redrawn (own 1.1, 1.2; talks
   6; accounts, "Refine the mark…") (seen and said).

## 5. Type

- **A plain text face and a characterful caps face, in every era.** Verdana with Georgia
  (2007); FF Milo, chosen as "versatile in various sizes" and good "in all caps" (2011);
  Whitney with Tungsten condensed caps (2012); Freight Sans 20/30 with Brandon Grotesque
  bold caps (2016); Söhne with his own display faces (2026) (own 1.5) (seen).
- **Display type with a source in the world.** Chunky all-caps faces redrawn from
  commercial lettering, shipped in "Rough" (worn, inky edges) and "Soft" (rounded
  terminals) versions (own 1.6; work table) (seen). "Imperfect, inky,
  rounded-but-rough shapes" (own 1.6) (said).
- **Tiny letterspaced caps as labels and navigation.** Georgia caps at about 10px with 2px
  tracking over a hairline (2007), orange Milo caps (2011), Freight Sans caps with
  inactive items at 30% black (2016), "LATEST FONTS →" with an orange arrow (2026). Nav
  items often carry a lowercase grey second line: "WORK / things I made" (own 1.4) (seen).
- **The ampersand as ornament**: italic and in its own colour in the 2007 and 2009
  taglines, a red "&" print and mug in 2026; reviewers note pages spent on ampersands
  (own 1.3, 3; accounts, "Craft as finish") (seen and said).
- **Comfortable text.** 2016 set text at 20/30 in a column of about 500px (own 1.5,
  1.3) (seen).

## 6. Colour and surface

- **One warm signature colour that survives every redesign**: orange, later red-orange
  (#f45923 in 2016), on headline bars, badges, drop caps, end marks, link underlines and
  arrows. He brought back "the original orange" on purpose (own 1.1) (seen and said).
- **A warm off-white ground**: cream (rgb 243/241/233 in 2007), "spartan beige" in 2010,
  off-white today (own 1.3, 1.7; accounts, "Quiet grid…") (seen and said).
- **Flat, warm, limited palettes in the goods**: three to five flat colours, no
  gradients, drawn from red-orange, mustard, cream, navy, teal and forest green (work 1.1,
  3) (seen). He loves "warm colors, and simple forms" and two-colour offset print (talks
  5, 8) (said).
- **Texture is welcome, at the edges and in the type**: grunge brushes, rough font
  variants, a birch texture in the margin (talks 4, 5; own 1.6) (said and seen).
- **Soft over hard.** Softened corners and gentle shadows as finish (talks 3, 4;
  accounts, "Craft as finish") (said).

## 7. Imagery and ornament

- **Real objects, photographed.** The 2026 hero is a still life of the studio's own goods
  on off-white: cartridges labelled in his fonts, wood type, an ink brayer, a mug, dice,
  stickers, a patch (own 1.7) (seen). It shows the craft rather than claiming it.
- **Badges and pastiche marks.** The SimpleBits badge redrawn as the Santa Cruz dot, a
  racing badge, a sheriff's star for a font called Serif (work 1.2) (seen).
- **The specimen poster.** A frame packed edge to edge with words from the subject's own
  world (trade terms, prices, dates, names), outer words cropped, lines in different
  sizes and weights, three to five flat colours, small icons from the font (work 1.1)
  (seen).
- **Small icons and simple glyphs**: beside dates and permalinks (2007), on each
  book cover (own 1.3, 3) (seen). "You can't go wrong with a lightning bolt" (talks 5)
  (said).
- **Physical craft in a digital story**: letterpress prints, cloth-bound books with a
  ribbon, shirts and handwritten notes mailed to Dribbble's first members (work 1.4;
  accounts, "Hand-made touches…") (seen and said).

## 8. Voice and naming

Dry and self-deprecating: "Never perfect, always a okay", "Danmade™ typefaces for people
like you", a "Secret Club™" in blackletter, strikethrough jokes in a list (own 1.8)
(seen). Font names are misspellings so each is "its own thing" (talks 8) (said). Merch
must show personality: "not just like, let's stick our logo on a mug" (talks 8) (said).
Dribbble's vocabulary came from basketball (accounts, "Recede when…") (said). The type
stays plain where the jokes are, so the words carry them (own 1.8) (seen).

## 9. Motion and enrichment

Motion belongs to the experience layer, "not integral to the brand, readability, or
layout": his example is a link background moving from light to darker green over about
0.3s, the same on focus as on hover (talks 3) (said). He warns against transitions on
everything, an "annoying, pulsating monster", and against slowing "an otherwise snappy
action" (talks 3) (said). His CSS3 talks added RGBa colour, subtle shadows, styled form
controls and small hover scaling or rotation, and let older browsers skip them (talks 3)
(said). Fisher recalls his advice to animate "in subtle and tasteful ways" (accounts,
"Craft as finish") (said).

## 10. Phones and flexibility

- **Flexible over pixel-perfect.** The books rebuild rigid pages to survive text
  resizing and missing images; "let go of pixel precision" (talks 3; accounts, "Craft as
  finish") (said).
- **Adapt the page you have.** Dribbble's small-screen version was an adaptive stylesheet
  on the existing site: horizontal items stacked, short vertical lists set in columns,
  content not hidden. Flexible layout "feels like web design", not "designing sheets of
  paper" (own §2; work table) (said).
- **Breakpoints from the content.** Dribbble's responsive layout used one breakpoint at
  500px, chosen by content rather than devices (talks 7) (said).

## 11. Taste summary

Likes: vintage commercial lettering, letterpress and wood type, inky imperfection, warm
flat colour, simple forms, badges, lightning bolts, ampersands, cream grounds, H&FJ and
FontFont faces, mid-century zines, Americana trade ephemera, small physical goods, people
"really passionate about these very specific things" (own 3; work 3; talks taste).
Dislikes: chasing trends ("use the style that fits the problem and stick with it"),
overused motion, bloated markup, logo-on-a-mug merch, clean-slate rebrands, perfectionism
that blocks shipping (talks taste; own 3, inferred).

## Cautions and gaps

- **Ban conflicts.** Three of his moves meet `../../references/bans.md`:
  - The coloured italic ampersand inside a tagline (own 1.3) is word-level emphasis when
    the tagline is a headline. `style.md` keeps the ampersand as a stand-alone ornament
    or in small utility text, never inside a headline.
  - His tiny letterspaced caps labels (own 1.4) become eyebrows when stamped above a
    section heading. `style.md` keeps them as navigation, metadata, and as a section's
    only heading (his "LATEST FONTS →" is the heading itself), never as a label stacked
    above another heading.
  - The slat (talks 7) and the soft-cornered colour boxes (talks 4) can turn into cards.
    `style.md` sets slats as ruled rows with no fill, border, radius or shadow round each
    item, and keeps soft corners for controls and single objects.
  - The specimen poster mixes sizes and weights (work 1.1). `style.md` lets each whole
    line take its own size, weight and colour, and never changes treatment inside a line.
- **Self-assessment.** He calls himself "not a great logo designer" and "not much of an
  illustrator" (talks taste), yet his badges and lettering are the strongest part of the
  seen work. The style follows the work.
- **Not seen.** Cork'd, the Blogger templates, MTV.com, TravelPost, IconShoppe, Foamee,
  the 2009 to 2010 Dribbble interface, the 2008 and 2016 to 2025 sites beyond one home
  page, and the essays on craft, logos and letterpress (own §4; work §4). The Dribbble
  "frame mode" rests on his words and one reporter's description.
- **Quotes.** Many quotes passed through a summarising fetch (own §4; talks open
  questions); `style.md` uses them as paraphrase.
- **Phones** rest on one 2011 post and one talk note; the guidance in `style.md` extends
  them.
