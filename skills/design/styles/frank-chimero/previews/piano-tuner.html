Every tuning starts from the same fork. Alan Pryce, who taught me, gave it to me in 1994, the year I started. I strike it on my knee, stand it on the piano’s bridge, and bring the A above middle C into agreement with it. Every other string on the piano is tuned from that one note, by ear.

I tune by ear because an ear hears what the room and the old strings are doing to the sound.

How a note comes into tune

Most notes have three strings. I mute two, tune one, then bring each of the others to it. While two strings disagree, you hear a slow throb in the sound: the beat. As the pin turns, the beat slows, and when it stops the two strings are one note.

  1. 7 beats a second
  2. 4 a second
  3. 2 a second
  4. 1 a second
  5. None: one note

A full tuning is about 230 strings and takes me two hours. Bring the piano to me only for restoration; tuning happens where it stands, because the room is part of the sound.
