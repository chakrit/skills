## Findings

### Process: what he does first and what he settles before layout

- Starts every project with a conversation about the organisation, audience and aim;
  "my job is to listen, ask questions" (stated) [HELP]. In 2018 he said brief-shaping
  should be done face to face: "the best way ... is to sit and talk with them
  face-to-face", and he asks about business goals, not user goals, at that stage
  (stated) [BR].
- "I always start with type, because it sets the tone for everything else" (stated)
  [SHI, 2026]. His type process: shortlist a handful, show how each "feels" and what its
  "tone of voice" says (neutral or opinionated), try them on the organisation's name, set
  them in "type sheets" with real or self-written copy, "rather than use dummy lorem
  ipsum", inspect individual letterforms, numerals and special characters (stated)
  [SHI]. He does not hand the choice to the client: "I don't ask clients to make the
  decision, as that's my job" (stated) [SHI].
- The Zombie Hunt series, which he calls a walk through "my process and the decisions I
  make", runs colour first, then dark and light themes, then grid, then small screens,
  then code (stated) [Z1–Z5, 2022]. The order of the posts is his; he does not say the
  order is fixed.
- For his own 2018 site: wrote and rewrote the copy about himself "over the course of
  several weeks", then sketched layouts on paper and in Sketch, chose "a fairly unusual
  4/6 compound grid", designed four desktop layouts, then "designed entirely in a browser
  using black and grey and placeholder illustrations" (stated) [ERR]. In 2021: "I often
  work in black and white and use cursive typefaces while experimenting with ideas for
  layout compositions" (stated) [G45].
- Sketches on paper first and treats wireframes "as storyboards rather than diagrams of
  boxes" that decide "what someone should discover, in what order" (stated) [PMW]. For
  the Brodovitch-inspired design he formed "a storyboard from a short series of sketches"
  across screen sizes (stated) [AB].
- Looks outside the client's sector for ideas: "When I begin a project like this, I don't
  spend much time looking at other composers' websites" (stated) [PBP]; "I rarely begin by
  researching websites in the same sector" (stated) [PCM].
- Looks for a single client-specific idea and builds the identity on it: Begoña Pereda's
  love of cassette decks and tape machines [PBP]; Michael Baugh's interest in 1950s–60s
  science fiction paperbacks [PMB]; Tristan Jakob-Hoff's interest in Saul Bass [PTJ]; a
  story character (Orango Jones) for Mike Worth, so that "the story shaped the
  experience from the beginning" rather than decoration added after layouts (all stated)
  [PMW].
- Reframes the brief before designing: Codeboxx "I expected the challenge to be
  organisational. Instead, I discovered the real problem was creating trust" [PCB];
  Academy of Scoring Arts "I thought I was designing a website for an educational
  platform. I quickly realised I was designing for a community" [PSA]; Portico "this
  website didn't need pictures of property, it needed a strong visual language" [PPI]
  (all stated).
- Colour palette method: pick a base colour, derive relationships from the colour wheel
  (split complementary, double split, triadic), then adjust by eye: "colour theory is a
  starting point, not a set of hard and fast rules"; he desaturated the Zombie Hunt
  colours and made a yellow by blending two palette colours with a screen blend mode;
  he recoloured stock illustrations to match (stated) [Z1]. Checks contrast during design,
  not at the end: accessibility "shouldn't be a consideration we test at the end"
  (stated) [Z1]. Tints and shades are made with multiply and screen blend layers since
  2018, replacing his 2004 method of opacity over black and white (stated) [CPAL].
- Samples accent colours from photographs and illustrations, one accent (or its tints)
  per article, on headline, standfirst, drop cap, pull quote (stated) [ER].
- Builds "foundation styles" first: "my process always starts by applying foundation
  styles, which include colors and typography" (stated) [BT]; markup first, from "the
  most appropriate elements which describe my content", previewing small screens before
  larger ones (stated) [D18].
- Code early: "After just one or two rounds of creative concepts made using Sketch, I
  switch to code as quickly as possible" (stated) [Z5, 2022]. In 2026 he builds a
  working Eleventy prototype instead of static mock-ups or Figma files, even when the
  production site is rebuilt in WordPress (stated) [PPI]; "As always, I developed an
  interactive Eleventy prototype" (stated) [PVK].

### What he shows a client and how a design changes along the way

- 2014: high-fidelity static visuals for sign-off are "bringing a knife to a gun fight";
  showing the whole design at once makes conversations unfocused, so he splits a design
  into components and discusses layout separately from "atmosphere", which he defines as
  "the feelings that are evoked by color, texture and typography" and which does not
  depend on layout (stated) [MDC]. He is "wary of defining it as a 'process'" (stated)
  [MDC]. The same layout/atmosphere split appears in his CSS thinking in 2012 [DL].
- 2014: "We don't keep our work secret while they wait for a 'big reveal.' Instead we
  talk to them all day, every day" (stated) [CB].
- Shows type shortlists and asks which "felt right" [SHI]. Shows concepts that may be
  dropped: the Shared Homeland bird concept was rejected by the client in favour of
  topographical and pre-1948 maps; "that's never a failure. Design decisions are often
  subjective, so every attempt becomes a conversation" (stated) [SHI], [PSH]. Mountain
  Duck shows "rejected illustrations" and a "rejected home page concept" (stated, image
  captions) [PMD].
- Academy of Scoring Arts: first ideas from notation and instruments were dropped as not
  distinctive; he moved to abstract graphics after modernist posters and album covers
  (Erik Nitsche named) "after dozens of sketches and banner concepts" (stated) [PSA].
- Explains decisions: "I'll sometimes ask awkward questions and challenge your
  assumptions, but I'll always explain the thinking behind every design decision"
  (stated) [HELP].
- Selling ideas is a skill to learn: "it's not always the best idea or the best execution
  that wins a pitch ... but the one that's been sold the best" (stated) [ALA, 2014];
  repeated almost word for word in 2020 (stated) [EM].
- Some ideas only for the dark or light theme: the outline logotype face was lost on the
  light background, so he used the "worn" version there (stated) [Z2].

### Reasons he gives for particular moves

- **Compound grids (3+4, 4+5) instead of 12 columns.** Twelve even columns are "Think of
  each column as a beat ... the rhythm is monotonous"; a 4+5 compound gives
  `6|1|4|3|3|4|1|6` and a 3+4 gives `3|1|2|2|1|3` (stated) [Z3], [CG], [G45]. A 12-column
  grid can produce the same layouts, but "would starting with twelve columns have forced
  the break from conventional thinking? ... No" (stated) [CG]. The interplay of two grids
  is "more interesting than one grid used in isolation" (stated) [G45].
- **Narrow ("skinny") columns.** Used to offset a headline, to widen an image by one
  skinny width, to set indents of images and pull quotes, and as "precise amounts of
  whitespace" separating main from supplementary content (stated) [CG]. In print the
  narrow column is the "bastard column"; an image squeezed into one standard column
  "reduces its visual weight", so he splits a column and adds half its width to the image
  (stated) [AV]. An empty skinny column between standard columns makes "a structured
  layout feel more dynamic" (stated) [AV].
- **Three- and four-column feel.** A three-column grid "has an editorial feel but retains
  a certain informality"; four columns pack text "into tighter blocks" and give "a more
  serious tone"; the formality of four columns "can be softened by an oversized headline"
  (stated) [CG]. Single columns "have a classical feel", twin columns are "most flexible",
  modular grids are for "drama and energy" (stated) [Z3]; twin columns "feel orderly",
  three or more with white space "take on an editorial feel" (stated) [ER].
- **Ratios between column widths.** Framework grids "offer little or no help in deciding
  the width of or proportional relationship between elements"; he uses Wolfgang von
  Wersin's orthogons. Lower ratios (biauron 1:1.236) for "academic or editorial content";
  higher (sixton 1:1.732, doppelquadrat 1:2) "add drama ... for designs with a strong
  purpose"; even 1:1 "feels harmonious and unchallenging" (stated) [RA].
- **Modular grids and pace.** More, smaller modules quicken reading; "Varying the amount
  of modules across several pages allows me to change the pace of someone's experience";
  empty modules "add breathing space" and "An empty space can be just as important as one
  filled with content" (stated) [MG]. A six-step scale from one full-screen module
  ("Slowest ... time to pause") to many tiny ones ("Fastest ... ideal for mobile
  navigation") (stated) [ER]. A modular page different from its neighbours is "an
  excellent choice for interrupting the rhythm of reading" (stated) [ER].
- **No conventional full-width header on his own home page.** The 2021 composition
  "avoids the conventional full-width header seen on countless framework-based websites"
  (stated) [G45]. Full-width banners "aren't inherently bad, but using them as filler or
  because everyone else has them is" (stated) [EL2].
- **Message near the top.** Labour's five missions and the Conservatives' key message
  moved from low on the page into the banner area; fundraising moved out of tabs (stated)
  [EL1], [EL2].
- **Steep type hierarchy for confidence.** "Bold type projects confidence, and a steep
  typographic hierarchy, weighted towards large type, is unapologetic. Small type and a
  shallow hierarchy are less challenging" (stated) [EL1]. Symmetry "might suggest
  stability, while asymmetric layouts are more dynamic" (stated) [EL1].
- **Expressive headlines from one family.** Black and light weights, negative tracking,
  tight leading make "a block of type which demands attention"; "Expressive typography
  needn't need fancy fonts" (stated) [HL].
- **Bottom-aligned captions, drop and initial caps, first-line emphasis, clip-path and
  shape-outside.** Presented as print techniques now easy in CSS; caps "mark where
  someone should start reading"; organic shapes "add movement, which draws people to
  them" (stated) [MEMO]. CSS Shapes "have become a major part of my editorial style"
  (stated) [D18].
- **Paragraphs without spacing.** He removes space between paragraphs to make "solid
  blocks of text which better define the structure"; "I mostly use first-line
  indentation", and shows alternating weights (after Richard Hollis) or alternating
  colours as alternatives (stated) [QT].
- **Ledes.** A lede "needn't look different from other paragraphs", but may be marked
  with a heavier weight or a drop/initial cap "to add personality" (stated) [LD].
- **Accent colours sampled from images.** Connects typography to imagery and gives each
  article "its own distinctive style" while grid and foundation styles keep consistency
  (stated) [ER].
- **Monochrome palettes plus an accent.** "I still find working with color the most
  challenging aspect of design. Perhaps that's why I so often gravitate towards
  monochromatic color schemes"; the accent "acts as a counterpoint" (stated) [GP]. Darker
  shades for borders, lighter tints for highlights; for dark mode he raises lightness and
  saturation (stated) [GP], [Z2].
- **Dark themes.** Off-black and off-white, not pure: Zombie Hunt dark background
  HSL 0,0,15; pure black and white "make text trickier to read" (stated) [Z2], [Z1]. In
  dark themes he uses a lighter weight, raises small text (14px to 14.5–16px), adds
  tracking and a little word spacing (stated) [Z2]. One colour swaps role between themes
  (yellow accent in dark, yellow background in light) to "visually connect both themes"
  (stated) [Z2].
- **Original illustration instead of stock.** Property and software sectors are
  criticised for "faded stock photos of generic city skylines or ethnically diverse
  people shaking hands" (stated) [AA2]; EPD and Portico got abstract skylines and
  geometric illustrations (stated) [AA2], [PPI]. Codeboxx product photos are "dull", so he
  made animated graphics per product category (stated) [PCB]. "Every illustration on a
  website has a job to do" (stated) [PCB].
- **Interface elements in the story's material.** Mike Worth's navigation became a stone
  tablet, buttons carved stone, news on scrolls: "A conventional menu or generic rounded
  button would have felt imported from another website", while buttons "still needed to
  look clickable" (stated) [PMW].
- **Ambient animation.** Principles: "Keep animations slow and smooth. Loop seamlessly
  ... Use layering ... Avoid distractions. Consider accessibility and performance";
  "If someone's eyes are drawn to it, it's probably too much, so I dial back the
  animation" (stated) [AA2]. Animation "works best when it adds character and atmosphere
  rather than movement for its own sake" (stated) [PMW].
- **Easter eggs.** "I love to hide Easter eggs in my client work" (stated) [RR]; Mike
  Worth's magnifying-glass light shafts [AA2], Mountain Duck's hidden joke [PMD], his
  2011 site [EW].
- **Graphical text as SVG.** A display face used once per page is converted to outlines
  (8kb SVG versus 17kb WOFF2) with accessible hidden text (stated) [Z5].
- **Navigation visible on small screens when short.** Zombie Hunt kept links visible
  rather than behind a menu icon because the space saving was not significant (stated)
  [Z4].

### What he refuses or dislikes, and why

- Framework-led sameness: "That websites with 12 or 16-column grids look so similar has
  become a cliche" (stated) [Z3]. In 2018 he "banned frameworks from my projects" because
  frameworks, like FrontPage, "sacrifice semantics for ease and speed" (stated) [D18].
  See the dated changes below for the other statements on frameworks.
- "Sites we like" sections in briefs ("the creative equivalent of the slick haircut
  photographs ... on barber shop walls"); "Never acknowledge even the existence of that
  prior work" (stated) [BR]. Prescriptive briefs containing solutions (stated) [BR].
- Lorem ipsum in type tests (stated) [SHI].
- Pastiche: Mike Worth's brief was "not to copy DuckTales or create a nostalgic
  pastiche"; "inspiration and imitation are very different things" (stated) [PMW], [RR].
- Templates: "Templates begin with generalisations, but I prefer to begin with unique
  individuals" (stated) [PBP].
- Conservative typography as a credibility shortcut: "Financial businesses often hide
  behind conservative typography ... I don't think that's true" (stated) [PPI].
- Approval on static visuals: "asking for approval on visuals should be considered
  harmful" because it fixes a web page as a frozen image (stated) [MDC].
- Pure complementary pairs, "unless I aim for a very high impact"; near-primary red with
  its complement "created uncomfortable vibrations" (stated) [Z1].
- Mobile-first as a method (2022): "Designing mobile 'first' has never particularly
  suited my approach to design" (stated) [Z4]. See dated changes.
- Squeezing elements onto small screens without regard to hierarchy (stated) [HI].
- Pre- and post-processors: abandoned for CSS custom properties (stated) [D18]; build
  tools considered and declined for his 2018 site (stated) [ERR].
- Managing people: "I don't want to manage a team ... I want to get paid for making
  things look great" (stated) [BF].
- Military suppliers as clients: "We also say no to organisations whose main business is
  to supply the military of any country" (stated) [CB].
- Not refused outright: conventions. "Conventions exist because they work ... The
  problem begins when convention turns into imitation"; he kept Mountain Duck's feature,
  edition and download journey (stated) [PMD].

  paperback covers [PMB]. '90s cartoons and '90s websites (Cheestrings, Goosebumps,
  Cartoon Network 1998) [RR], [PMW].
- "Editorial design / Graphic design / Magazines / Posters / Literally everywhere other
  than the web" (stated) [MEMO]; "Take a trip to your nearest art gallery, bookstore,

- Web designers named as heroes or influences: Trent Walton ("my design hero"), Jason

### How he works with clients

- Works directly with the client, no layers (stated) [HELP]. Prefers phone or meeting to
  email when a brief dictates design: "pick up the phone" (stated) [BR].
- Briefs should state business goals and "describe challenges, explain opportunities and
  even talk about dreams", be written in one "singular voice", and be developed jointly
  after hiring (stated) [BR].
- Advises clients to choose a designer from work they admire, pay a good share up front,
  not haggle, and commit for three years (stated) [BR].
- Fees: charging a comfortable rate means "I don't have to watch the clock" and "you have
  my 100% attention" (stated) [BF].
- Project teams in one room with the client's developers and content people; ISO named
  (stated) [CB], [MDC].
- Hands over working code: responsive HTML and CSS templates to the Home Office [HSB];
  Eleventy builds via GitHub [PMD]; prototypes for WordPress developers [PPI] (stated).
- Works inside constraints when given: Squarespace for Count Binface via code blocks and
  injected CSS (stated) [PCF]; his imagined Labour redesign kept their 12-column grid,
  Poppins and red (stated) [EL2].
- Sue Clarke, his wife and business partner: "Remember Andy hates to be told 'what to
  do,' much better to remind him 'what needs to be done'" (reported) [HSO].
- Client testimonials: "If you want something bold and different I highly would
  recommend Stuff & Nonsense" (Peter O'Flynn, Codeboxx); "his design perfectly tells my
  story" (Ryan Knaggs) (reported) [HELP].
- Researches the subject deeply when it is serious: read everything on Hillsborough and
  extended the UX lead's personas with the websites each person visits (stated) [HSB].

### How he treats phones

- 2011: desktop-first then pruning is "the opposite of how we should be designing"; his
  boilerplate starts with "a tiny screen stylesheet that contains only reset, colour and
  typography styles" (stated) [320].
- 2012: a wide banner squeezed into a narrow column "loses its position in the visual
  hierarchy"; crop it instead (stated) [HI]. Design testing on a phone and tablets held
  in the hand is separate from device testing (stated) [DT].
- 2019: on small screens he puts tall images into horizontally scrolling panels and uses
  a narrow column to indent running text: "There's no need to fall back on predictable,
  single-column designs for small screens" (stated) [ER]; scattered images become a
  horizontal scroller on phones, vertical on medium, horizontal on large (stated) [AB].
- 2022: small screens are "an extra creative challenge"; keep the compound grid but use
  fewer columns; crop large illustrations with `picture` or clip-path rather than shrink
  them; consider horizontal scrolling panels; "I set my type sizes so they're readable on
  every screen and rarely increase them as screens get bigger"; viewport units for
  whitespace; "many of my Zombie Hunt page designs use a single column on small screens"
  (stated) [Z4].

### What he believes about design in general

- "Bootstrap doesn't create unimaginative designs. Lazy designers do" (stated) [ER].
- A grid "brings cohesion", "suggests hierarchies", and tells people "what to read first,
  then next, and how much attention to give it" (stated) [G45]. A design system "should
  support a design, not define, nor dictate the end result" (stated, about Brody) [NB].
- Making people look twice can make designs more memorable, against "don't make them
  think" (stated) [NB].
- "Personality isn't decoration. It's communication" (stated) [PMD]; "Personality isn't
  expressed through one bold idea. It's revealed through hundreds of thoughtful
  decisions" (stated) [PCF].
- Ideas over iteration: "You can't turn a poor idea into a brilliant one by iterating, so
  don't make fewer ideas. Make more" and let ideas run "unhindered by practicalities"
  before asking how they will be built (stated) [ALA]. "no amount of research is a
  substitute for a good idea" (stated) [MDC].
- "Making a website pretty" is "a skill that we should be very proud of" (stated) [MDC].
- Web design is responsive by definition: "There's just web design" (stated) [WD].
- Accessibility and performance belong in design, not a test phase (stated) [Z5], [Z1].
- Today's sites are "safer–but often, they're also duller, less expressive, and
  ultimately less memorable" (stated) [RR].

### Where he changed his mind, with dates

- **Mobile first.** 2011: launches "320 and Up" as a small-screen-first kit and calls
  desktop-first pruning "the opposite of how we should be designing" (stated) [320]; 2014 lists "What if we designed
  mobile first?" among good questions (stated) [MDC]. 2022: "Designing mobile 'first'
  has never particularly suited my approach to design ... Instead, I now try to make as
  many aspects of my designs flexible" (stated) [Z4].
- **Atomic design and design systems.** 2014: on atomic design's periodic-table model, "I
  can't imagine anything less creatively stimulating"; creativity should not be moulded
  into a process (stated) [MDC]. 2018: his business had shifted "towards creative
  direction, design systems and style guides" (stated) [ERR]. 2021: design systems "are
  just tools" and did not create the visual-skills gap; the cause is education (stated)
  [DS].
- **Frameworks.** 2018: "banned frameworks from my projects" (stated) [D18]. 2019:
  "Bootstrap doesn't create unimaginative designs. Lazy designers do" (stated) [ER].
  2026: "Frameworks aren't entirely to blame ... The danger comes when the framework
  starts making creative decisions on your behalf" (stated) [PMD]. These are positions at
  three dates; he does not say he changed his mind.
- **Static comps.** 2014: against sign-off on static visuals (stated) [MDC]. 2018 and 2022:
  he still makes first concepts in Sketch, then moves to the browser (stated) [ERR], [Z5].
  2026: Eleventy prototypes "instead of a PDF" or Figma (stated) [PPI].
- **Line length.** 2012: no max-width on his site; "I don't want to put constraints on
  line-length. That's not a designers' job. It's a user's job" (stated) [LL]. No later
  statement on this was found in this half's sources.
- **Tint method.** 2004 opacity over black and white; 2018 multiply and screen, because
  the old method made colours "appear a little flat" (stated) [CPAL].
- **Paragraph signalling.** 2021: had mostly used first-line indents; will try
  alternating weights (stated) [QT].
- **Starting point.** 2022 series begins with colour [Z1]; 2026 says "I always start with
  type" [SHI] (both stated; he does not comment on the difference).
- **Burnout and return.** By 2017–2018 "I had just about had enough", and the Australia
  job "brought me back" (stated) [BF]; in 2019 he wrote his interest in web design had
  waned five or six years earlier and magazines restored it (stated) [AV].
