## 1. Moves

### Make the interface itself the expressive piece, not a frame around content

- What: the navigation, the clock, the home screen or the index is the work. Content
  arrives through a behaving system rather than in a list or grid of cards.
- Where: MoMA Design and the Elastic Mind site, where image bubbles "connected by
  fleeting arcs of colour" push onto a black field and each image spawns linked terms
  that lead to more bubbles; Womack contrasts this with what a "lesser designer" would
  do, falling back on labels such as 'artist' or 'timeline' (Eye, 2008) [reported].
  Search snippets of the MoMA post say tha named as one aim that the site itself be a
  form of creative expression on the exhibition's theme [reported, from snippet only].
  Ars Electronica's jury praised yugop.com for its "aesthetic of interaction", not only
  its surface (2005) [reported].
- Replaces: a standard index of categories, timelines or tiles.
- Fits: when the subject is about relationships, emergence or play, and the visitor has
  time to explore.

### A known object as anchor, re-wired by an algorithm from elsewhere

- What: take something everyone reads instantly (a clock, a logo, the Mona Lisa, a
  message board) and drive it with an unexpected rule, so the familiar form carries the
  new behaviour.
- Where: the Mynavi critic names his ongoing method "collage at the level of algorithm"
  (アルゴリズムの次元におけるコラージュ), citing dropclock and clashclock, where a clock is
  the reference point combined with something from another context to make a new
  balance (2010) [reported]. Monalisa Nervous Matrix (1999): keypad 1 to 9 drives a
  3 x 3 matrix and the face swells at that cell, amplifying a small input into
  enjoyable motion [reported]. UNIQLOCK pairs a clock with dance clips and a time-signal
  jingle (D&AD, Hypebeast) [reported]. UNIQLO GRID makes the logo the toy that users
  split, merge and rotate (GIGAZINE, Ryan Inspiration) [reported]. ecotonoha recasts a
  thread-style message board as a tree whose leaves are messages (Mynavi) [reported].
- Replaces: novelty for its own sake, or decoration beside a fixed object.
- Fits: brand pieces where the brand already owns a simple shape or ritual.

### Text as both form and content

- What: letters and messages are the building material of the image, not captions on
  it.
- Where: Mynavi calls his letters a knot (結節点) of form and content, with ecotonoha's
  message-leaves as the example (2010) [reported]. NEC's ecotonoha: visitors write a
  message, it becomes a leaf on a growing tree; the SHIFT interviewer gives the concept
  as "A shape is formed by a group of people unexpectedly, and with fun" (2004)
  [reported].
- Fits: participatory pages where many small contributions should add up to one
  picture.

### Design with time: rhythm, schedule, habit

- What: the piece changes by the clock or by the season so people come back, rather than
  being a static page.
- Where: UNIQLOCK runs 24/7 with dance segments between clock readings and a special
  routine on the hour (Hypebeast) and outfits that change with the season (secondary
  case study) [reported]. Phase 2: a pendulum swinging in sync with the music and a
  background photo slideshow that changes every second, pausing when the cursor nears
  the pendulum; a circular clock-like menu to pick a style (Muroichi) [reported]. In
  the SHIFT interview he speaks of building regular daily rhythms that form habits,
  something TV struggles to do (2004) [stated].
- Fits: brand sites that should feel alive and worth revisiting; a page can have a
  tempo.

### Many visitors, one shared surface, an emergent picture

- What: everyone acts on the same canvas; the result is a collective shape nobody
  planned, and the scoreboard of that collective is part of the display.
- Where: UNIQLO GRID, one big shared "graph paper" where coloured logos collide,
  players repaint each other or co-operate with unseen strangers; GIGAZINE's captions
  narrate an arc from warring states to near-unification to pieces (2007) [reported].
  A scrolling bar logs every move and a scorecard tracks participation (Ryan
  Inspiration) [reported]. UNIQLOCK's world map of users (D&AD) [reported]. HUMANITY
  began as "How many digital people can we put on a screen at once?" (Wikipedia)
  [reported], and from bird flocking (Boids) and Comiket queues (PlayStation Blog)
  [stated].
- Fits: launches and campaigns where participation is the message.

### Few, direct gestures; economy of means

- What: a small set of mouse gestures with physical meaning (drag across to cut, circle
  to merge, double-click to turn) instead of menus and buttons.
- Where: UNIQLO GRID's split, merge and rotate gestures (Ryan Inspiration, GIGAZINE)
  [reported]. Ars Electronica jury: "highly economical expressive means" and "startling
  originality, charm and whimsy" (2005) [reported].
- Fits: any interactive piece; keep the verb count low and make each verb feel like
  handling material.

### Restraint against literal skeuomorphism; a neutral stage, smooth motion

- What: avoid copying the physical product's look into the screen; keep the virtual
  layer neutral, ordered and fast.
- Where: iida UI for INFOBAR A01. He rejected a colourful tile UI mirroring the
  hardware because reproducing real texture in virtual space made the gap between real
  and virtual look "fake" (嘘っぽく); he regrouped Android into three module sizes
  (icon S, widget M, app L) on a grid "like stacked audio components", stacked
  vertically because precisely arranged modules looked cleanest that way; drew a Latin
  typeface to pair with the Japanese system font; used a Flash prototype as the
  smoothness target (ケータイ Watch, 2011) [stated]. Reviewers: never saw Android changed
  so completely, it recalls Windows Phone 7 (gori.me) [reported]; vertical flicks scroll
  smoothly and continuously, a search box added later "slightly dilutes" the design's
  distinctiveness (ITmedia, 2012) [reported].
- Fits: product UI next to a strong physical or brand object.

### Abstract figures, sterile stage, motion as the expressive element

- What: people reduced to small uniform figures on bare architecture, so that the
  movement of the mass carries the meaning.
- Where: HUMANITY combines cel-shaded humans with brutalist architecture (Wikipedia)
  [reported]; a person in the crowd reads "like a speck of dust" and the VR view is like
  "observing a swarm of ants"; early visuals were harsher, and Mizuguchi moved them "in
  a more gentle direction" (PlayStation Blog) [stated, reported]. Search snippets of
  the VGC and GameSpot reviews call the look deliberately minimal and sometimes
  monotonous [reported, not read in full].

## 2. Process and thinking

- Starts from material, not from a plan: he calls himself weak at top-down planning and
  compares his method to finding an interesting brick and asking what house suits it,
  rather than starting from a family's room list; HUMANITY's system came from small
  discoveries of emergent behaviour, and the story was written afterwards (デザインの手前,
  2025) [stated].
- Engineer-designer-programmer in one: SHIFT (2004) describes him as director, designer
  and engineer [reported]; he calls programming and animation his strengths and hobbies
  overlapping with design, and MONO*crafts "programming craftsmanship" rather than design
  (デザインの手前) [stated]. Wikipedia says he uses the mathematics behind natural
  complexity for interactions modelled on nature [reported, uncited].
- One interface from concept down to the detail of use, simple but flexible (SHIFT)
  [stated].
- Prototype to set the feel: a Flash prototype was the performance target for iida UI
  (ケータイ Watch) [stated].
- Imitate, then push further; show the work to peers and enjoy the reaction (デザインの手前)
  [stated].
- Peers: hosts of デザインの手前 call him a pioneer whose widening of the field reassures
  younger designers [reported]; Mizuguchi calls him "a superstar creator" (PlayStation
  Blog) [reported]; Eye calls him a "Web legend" [reported]; Ars Electronica cites his
  "global influence on a generation of artists and designers" [reported].

## 3. Taste

- Wants design that is "fun, beautiful, interesting, and exciting", treats design as a
  playground (Japan Design Committee profile) [stated].
- As a juror he rewards a "service mindset" that makes things fun, fluid technology
  joined with "highly empathetic" storytelling, and small works made from a simple urge
  to make something rather than to critique the medium (JMAF critique) [stated].
- Charm and whimsy over solemnity; wit over technical display (Ars Electronica jury;
  student blog on MONO*crafts' "fluid, naturalistic" images and humour) [reported].
- Collectives and emergence: communities shaped by participation and difference (SHIFT)
  [stated]; flocks, queues, crowds (HUMANITY) [stated].
- Dislikes the fake: literal reproduction of physical texture in UI (ケータイ Watch)
  [stated].
- Background of note: UNIQLO's US history page reportedly lists him with Kashiwa Sato,
  Masamichi Katayama and Markus Kiersztan as the team for the US push [reported, from a
  search snippet; the page timed out].
