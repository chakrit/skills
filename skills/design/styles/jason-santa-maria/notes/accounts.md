# Jason Santa Maria: accounts

What other people report about Jason Santa Maria (JSM): clients and collaborators on
working with him, and reviews of the work. Collected 2026-11-24 for the `accounts`
stream of step 4 in `docs/spec/design.md`.

Every claim is marked:

- **[reported]**: another person says it, in their own words.
- **[stated, via]**: JSM's own words, quoted or paraphrased inside another person's
  page (an interview or a liveblog). Kept here because the page is an account; the
  `words` stream owns JSM's own writing.
- **[observed in account]**: something a reviewer saw in a page and described.

## Findings

### Process

- **Comps before build, then details settled in the browser.** For ALA 4.0 he "did
  flesh out PSDs for the majority of pages", keeping things "even and on their grid",
  and made full comps because the design "was such a departure I had to really sell it
  to the gang with flat comps." Hover styles were worked out by "playing with live XHTML
  and CSS"; the issue stamp "came about during CSS programming". [stated, via] S2
  (2005-08-24)
- **The ALA 4.0 layout began fluid and became fixed** "after the first or second round
  of designs revisions." [stated, via] S2 (2005-08-24)
- **Build handed to a specialist.** Eric Meyer took "the visual goodness of Jason Santa
  Maria" and turned it into XHTML and CSS, describing his own role as "building XHTML
  and CSS around existing designs". [reported] S1 (2005-08-23)
- **Discovery and research first.** Clark's liveblog records JSM putting discovery
  ("getting informed about your client and trying to figure out the message they want
  to convey") and research ("the most important part of the design phase") ahead of
  design; for the Comhaltas site the Happy Cog team travelled to Ireland and sat in on
  pub sessions. [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Sketchbook and morgue files.** He keeps a sketchbook and physical and digital morgue
  files of "old advertisements, weird old photos", matchbooks, textures. [stated, via]
  S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Grey-box comp between sketch and Photoshop**: "with no colour and focussing on
  hierarchy and layout", because going straight from sketch to Photoshop leaves him "too
  caught up in the details". [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Three comps, then narrowing.** For AIGA, three broad designs were shown for the
  client to choose from; the team told clients not to "Frankenstein these comps"
  together, though "that doesn't really happen"; the second round focused on typography.
  "It's much easier to revise than create." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Three mockups for WordPress 2.5.** "Jason Santa Maria created three designs to
  present to Matt, who would choose one to move forward." Mullenweg chose the visually
  lighter one. [reported] S13 (undated)
- **Many logo variations.** For the WordPress logo he showed a slide of about 30
  variations exploring word balloons, metal type, typewriter keys; the final mark is
  "implicative of a typewriter key" and "didn't actually get that far from where we were
  before." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Outside firm for the identity, then in-house build-out (Slate, 2018).** "Our design
  director, Jason Santa Maria, and his team ... brought in the design firm Gretel to help
  develop our new logo and define aesthetic principles, and then spent months building
  on those ideas." [reported, Slate release quoted] S14 (2018-01-24)

### Reasons given for particular moves

- **No focal point on the ALA 4.0 home page.** "I want people so [sic] peruse the place, find
  somewhere to settle down ... So, there is no overt focus of the homepage ... Nothing
  screams because it doesn't need to." [stated, via] S2 (2005-08-24)
- **No visited-link states on ALA 4.0**: "I wanted to give the impression that this was
  more than a website." [stated, via] S2 (2005-08-24)
- **Bookish identity for ALA.** He looked at "things that had a lot of content"
  (McSweeney's, H.G. Wells) and "things that are read religiously, not skimmed";
  banners, stamps, numbered issues and a laurel wreath came from books, "because we
  produce these as issues, not just updating our blog." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Per-issue colour on ALA.** "Each article has its own unique illustration by Kevin,
  and each issue has its own colour scheme", to make it "not quite so templated."
  [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04). Zeldman confirms ALA 4.0 "employed a system of dual
  link colors that changed with each issue." [reported] S11 (2013-01-25)
- **Sentence navigation on the Happy Cog site**: the navigation and tagline "both
  basically saying the same thing. Why not just make it our navigation." [stated, via]
  S5 (2007-10-04)
- **AIGA article page**: "Nice big reading column, nice big text, a drop cap to pull
  people in", so "the design can feel full all the way down to the bottom" instead of
  "adrift in a sea of nothingness". [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **AIGA grid**: a 120-column grid "subdividable into four, three, six", with a style
  guide for image sizes. [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Grids** make "the locations of things predictable", contrasted with London's street
  plan, which you must learn before you can use it. [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)

### Refusals and dislikes

- **Leprechaun clichés**: a Comhaltas branch site with "spinning logs and marquee" and
  dancing leprechauns was shown as what not to do. [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Piggybacking on a trend** in branding: "This makes us look like anybody else."
  [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **Dumb quotes and `<br><br>`**: shown as the typographic equivalent of bad markup.
  [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **The fold**: "I actually don't really concern myself too much with the fold."
  [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)

### Sources of ideas

- Old printed material, "especially hand-set"; books (McSweeney's, H.G. Wells);
  Bringhurst and Lupton on type and grids; William Penn's plan of Philadelphia as a grid
  example. [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- Print background: "With a background in print design, Jason had a vision to create a
  blog more in the style of a magazine." [reported] S9 (2009-11-19)

### Working with clients

- Expectations set "very early on, in the first meeting"; client feedback reiterated at
  every stage; "It's their site; it's your job to steer them in a good direction
  visually." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- **WordPress 2.5**: Zeldman led, JSM designed, Liz Danzico did the information
  architecture; "Happy Cog produced designs that WordPress developers then coded."
  [reported] S13. Danzico: "We worked with the valliant [sic] Matt Mullenweg, who in turn
  gathered feedback from the team at Auttomatic [sic]." [reported] S7 (2008-03-31)
- JSM on open-source clients: "there's this whole gamut of other people that you will
  only ever get to talk to a small portion of. I think that that's really difficult."
  [stated, via] S13
- Zeldman, in hindsight: "We had to please one user: Matt ... We completely bypassed the
  community ... the design then became unpopular because nobody got their say in it."
  [reported] S13
- **Slate 2018**: the release credits "Jason Santa Maria, and his team" with a "visual
  language that conveys our approach". [reported] S14 (2018-01-24)

### Phones and screen sizes

- ALA 4.0 was fixed width. JSM: "We don't support 800 x 600 anymore, nor do we 640 x
  480 ... you are going to see more sites stretching out their legs." [stated, via] S2
  (2005-08-24)
- Readers objected: horizontal scrollbars at 800×600, "non flexibility of the layout",
  "lack of fluidity", and at 1024×768 with a sidebar open. [reported] S1 (2005-08-23),
  S2 comments #15, #21, #24, #25
- For a site that must be fully fluid, he suggested ems for images, CSS cropping,
  thumbnails that click to larger images, and noted "As a line gets longer, you need
  more space between the lines." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- A Book Apart's site went responsive in work by JSM and Jonnie Hallman: "I helped out,
  but @jasonsantamaria and @destroytoday were the real magicians." [reported, Weychert]
  S15 (entry dated 2013-01-10 on the archive page)

### Beliefs about design in general

- Liquid layouts are "a fantastic option", not "the last step in evolution for web
  layouts." [stated, via] S2 (2005-08-24)
- Art direction is "all of the pieces that add up to make a design. And not only that,
  it's the concept." "No design is too small, whether it's a favicon or a line of text
  or a border." [stated, via] S5 (2007-10-04)
- On his 2008 site: "a system for the way content gets handled, but every layout and
  story can take on a look of their own within that system." [stated, via, quoted by
  Zeldman] S8 (2008-06-19). Also: "what you're looking at isn't the design so much as
  the plan for design." [stated, via] S10 (2009-11-10)

### Reviews of the work

- **ALA 4.0 (2005).** Meyer: "Four columns on the main page? Yes indeed!" [reported] S1.
  Readers: "a big departure from ALA's previous 'narrowness'", "I like the browns,
  creams, and oranges – echoes of Happy Cog." [reported] S1 comments. Small type was
  criticised: body 0.8125em and main 0.88em computing to about 11px. [reported] S1
  comments. The leftmost and rightmost columns were "mainly whitespace" to one reader.
  [reported] S1 comments
- **Focal point debate.** Matt Wilcox found the home page lacked a focal point, eyes
  "bouncing all over the page". Dan Mall defended it: "the layout is intended to lack a
  focal point", noting a "large image with lots of whitespace" in the logo column
  balanced against "a more content-heavy column". Laura Zucchetti and Dave Bedingfield
  saw the issue number as the focal point, reading top left to lower right. [reported]
  S2 comments #6, #8, #10, #16
- **Accessibility complaints.** With CSS on and images off, the issue number became
  white on white and the logo alt text overlapped the menu. [reported] S2 comment #5.
  The "No. 201" circle did not scale with text; top links dropped off the right; text
  set in images. [reported] S1 comments
- **"Bookish."** Zeldman: the 2005 design "cultivated a bookish look and feel
  indicative of A List Apart's stature as a time-tested resource." [reported] S11
  (2013-01-25). A reader said it "suggests 'Magazine' to me now and not 'Blog'."
  [reported] S2 comment #14
- **Typography as the design.** Garrett Dimon: "Jason's redesign of A List Apart, where
  almost the entire design revolved around typography, opened my mind." [reported] S4
  (2007-02-15)
- **2008 art-directed blog.** Zeldman: per-post control of "colors, typefaces, and the
  position and shape of copy blocks." [reported] S8 (2008-06-19). Smashing: differences
  between posts "are sometimes just subtle changes in background or typography" and
  sometimes "radical, but every one still has an element of 'Jason-ness.' The header and
  footer are usually consistent." [reported] S9 (2009-11-19)
- **WordPress 2.5 admin.** The Happy Cog design lightened Shuttle's "heavy blue palette";
  "An orange accent color complemented the lighter blues." [reported] S13. Wired, quoted
  by Zeldman, called it "the sleek new look". [reported] S6 (2008-03-19). Reception was
  mixed, "particularly with the menu hierarchy"; a Ball State usability study led to the
  2.7 "Crazyhorse" redesign, and user Ozh called 2.7 "light years beyond what the design
  studio produced for 2.5". [reported] S12 (2016-12-12)
- **Loud Room (2026).** Weychert: his new practice's site is "an appropriately simple
  introduction" with "a fresh, fun, procedurally-generated color scheme every time it
  loads." [reported] S15 (2026-01-28)
- **Megazoid.** Weychert: "super-fun microsite Jason Santa Maria designed for DJR's
  super-fun font, Megazoid!" [reported] S15 (2025-10-05)

### Changes of mind, with dates

- ALA 4.0's layout went from fluid to fixed during design revisions. [stated, via] S2
  (2005-08-24)
- ALA moved on from his design: ALA 5.0 (January 2013, Mike Pick and Tim Murtaugh)
  switched to responsive layout and "old-school" blue links. JSM was on the strategy
  team for 5.0 as "former creative director", but the accounts do not say what he
  argued for. [reported] S11 (2013-01-25)
